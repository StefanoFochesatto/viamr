\section{Introduction and goals} \label{sec:intro}

The VIAMR library (\href{https://github.com/StefanoFochesatto/viamr}{github.com/StefanoFochesatto/viamr}) implements a variety of adaptive mesh refinement (AMR) and mesh adaptation algorithms for variational inequalities (VIs).  Several methods incorporate heuristic strategies because our goal is to extend AMR to VIs and obstacle problems with highly-nonlinear and/or degenerate operator behavior, and for rough-data applications.

However, for classical obstacle problems, namely unilateral problems where the operator is the (weak) Laplacian and the corresponding PDE is the Poisson equation, the library also implements element marking using the fully-rigorous \emph{a posteriori} estimators from Nochetto, Siebert, \& Veeser \cite{NSV03,NSV05}.  Reliability (error bounded by estimator) and efficiency (estimator bounded by error) are proven in the $L^\infty$ pointwise norm for these estimators, plus localization for the latter \cite{NSV05}.  Such precision is generally not available for nonlinear and/or degenerate operators, and it is not proven for the heuristic methods in the library.

This paper explains how we have extended \cite{NSV03}'s proof of reliability and efficiency to the bilateral case, that is, with continuous lower \emph{and upper} bounds, and it takes up the refinements of \cite{NSV05} in the same setting.  The sequence of topics through \S \ref{sec:efficiency}, and much of the notation, generally follows \cite{NSV03}.  Note that the operator must still be the ordinary linear Laplacian; this is not changed.

We are not aware of any pointwise \emph{a posteriori} theory for the bilateral problem, which is why we spell this out.  Somewhat nearby, Ot\'arola, Rankin, \& Salgado \cite{Otarolaetal2019} prove reliable and efficient maximum-norm \emph{a posteriori} bounds for a problem with genuine box constraints, using the machinery of \cite{NSV03}, but their constrained variable is a control, and it is determined by a pointwise projection of the adjoint state, so there is no free boundary of the present kind, and no pointwise barrier construction of the sort in \S \ref{sec:barriers}.  (Our constrained variable is the solution of the VI itself.)

The paper is organized into sections, which group into three informal parts.  As the first part, Sections \ref{sec:setup} and \ref{sec:discretization} state the continuum bilateral problem, quantify the separation of its two contact sets, and build the discretization; nothing in this part is specific to the constructed estimators.  In the second part, Sections \ref{sec:estimator} through \ref{sec:efficiency} are the bilateral form of the estimator of \cite{NSV03}, which is assembled from continuous barrier functions and element-wise contact sets, and they prove reliability and efficiency.  Sections \ref{sec:localized} and \ref{sec:barriersets} form the third part, and concern \cite{NSV05}, namely its fully-localized, star-based estimator and its barrier sets, that is, computable regions containing a free boundary; FIXME these two are not yet written, and each states what its bilateral form requires.


\section{Continuum problem} \label{sec:setup}

\subsection{The variational inequality, and its residual}  Let $\Om$ be a bounded, polyhedral, not necessarily convex domain in $\mathbb{R}^d$ with $d \in \{2,3\}$.  Suppose $\psilo, \psiup \in H^1(\Om) \cap C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Om)$, $0 < \alpha \le 1$, are continuous lower and upper obstacles, respectively, which satisfy a fundamental non-degeneracy assumption:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:nondeg}
  \psilo < \psiup \qquad \text{on } \bar\Om.
\end{equation}
Let $f \in L^\infty(\Om)$ be a source function and $g \in H^1(\Om) \cap C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Om)$ be Dirichlet boundary data satisfying the compatibility condition
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:compat}
  \psilo \le g \le \psiup \qquad \text{on } \dOm.
\end{equation}

Strict separation \eqref{eq:nondeg} rules out ``pinched'' points where the two obstacles touch, and thus both constraints are simultaneously active; see for example \cite{BuelerFarrell2024}.  The analysis below is free of this extra case, but our VIAMR library implementation copes with near-pinching, since active sets are decided against a numerical tolerance.

Let $\mathcal{K}$ be the non-empty, closed, convex, \emph{box-constrained} subset of $H^1(\Om)$ defined by the bilateral obstacles:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:K}
  \mathcal{K} := \{v \in H^1(\Om) \mid \psilo \le v \le \psiup \text{ a.e.\ in } \Om,
    \ v = g \text{ on } \dOm\}.
\end{equation}
The fixed boundary condition $v=g$ is in the trace sense.  The continuous VI problem reads:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:VI}
  u \in \mathcal{K}: \qquad
  \left< \nabla u, \nabla(v - u)\right> \ge \left< f, v - u\right>
  \qquad \text{for all } v \in \mathcal{K}.
\end{equation}
This is the immediate generalization of problem (1.1) of \cite{NSV03}, from a one-sided constraint to the bilateral case.  The sign convention in \eqref{eq:VI} matches common conventions elsewhere in the literature \cite{BuelerFarrell2024,Falk1974,GraeserKornhuber2009,KinderlehrerStampacchia1980}, and in the library itself.  It is equivalent to the reversed form ``$\left< \nabla u, \nabla(u - v)\right> \le \left< f, u - v\right>$'' used in \cite[formula (1.1)]{NSV03}.  It is well-known that \eqref{eq:VI} admits a unique solution $u \in \mathcal{K}$ \cite[Theorem 6.2]{KinderlehrerStampacchia1980}.

Let $\sigma \in H^{-1}(\Om) = H_0^1(\Om)^*$ be the \emph{residual functional}
defined by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigma}
  \left< \sigma, \varphi\right> = \left< \nabla u, \nabla \varphi\right> - \left< f, \varphi\right>
  \qquad \text{for all } \varphi \in H_0^1(\Om).
\end{equation}
Note that definition \eqref{eq:sigma} switches the sign relative to \cite[formula (1.2)]{NSV03}, but our sign choice, consistent across the VIAMR library, matches the traditional choice for \emph{unilateral} complementarity.  That is, with this sign choice we have $u-\psi_\bot\ge 0, \sigma\ge 0, (u-\psi_\bot) \sigma = 0$ as the nonlinear complementarity form of the unilateral problem.

Consider what happens when the solution contacts the obstacle, creating sets where the inequality is active.  Unlike the unilateral case, $\sigma$ no longer has a fixed sign globally.  Write $\Cbot := \{x\,:\,u(x) = \psilo(x)\}$ and $\Ctop := \{x\,:\,u(x) = \psiup(x)\}$ for the closed
\emph{lower-} and \emph{upper-contact} (active) \emph{sets}, subsets of $\bar\Om$.  Because a general element of $H^{-1}(\Om)$ cannot be restricted to a closed set of measure zero, we first state the sign of $\sigma$ on open sets:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigmasignopen}
  \sigma \ge 0 \text{ on } \Om \setminus \Ctop \qquad \text{and} \qquad \sigma \le 0 \text{ on } \Om \setminus \Cbot,
\end{equation}
in the distributional sense, that is, $\left<\sigma,\varphi\right> \ge 0$ for every $\varphi \ge 0$ compactly supported in $\Om \setminus \Ctop$, and etc.  The proof of \eqref{eq:sigmasignopen} is by taking a smooth and locally-supported test function $\varphi\in H_0^1(\Om)$, with $\varphi\ge 0$ compactly supported in $\Om \setminus \Ctop$, or $\varphi\le 0$ compactly supported in $\Om \setminus \Cbot$, respectively, and substituting $v=u+\eps \varphi$ into \eqref{eq:VI}, for sufficiently small $\eps>0$, such that $v\in\mathcal{K}$.

Now, the non-degeneracy assumption \eqref{eq:nondeg} says $\Cbot$ and $\Ctop$ are disjoint, so the two open sets in \eqref{eq:sigmasignopen} cover $\Om$.  As a distribution with a sign on an open set, $\sigma$ is a signed Radon measure \cite{EvansGariepy2015}.  Furthermore, on the open \emph{non-contact set} (inactive set) $\{x\,:\,\psilo(x) < u(x) < \psiup(x)\}$, which lies in both of the sets in \eqref{eq:sigmasignopen}, the two bounds give $\sigma = 0$.  Thus $\sigma$ admits the Jordan decomposition $\sigma = \mu_{\bot} - \mu_{\top}$ into nonnegative Radon measures supported on $\Cbot$ and $\Ctop$ respectively.  In particular, $\sigma$ can now be restricted to the contact sets themselves, and \eqref{eq:sigmasignopen} implies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigmasign}
  \sigma \ge 0 \text{ on } \Cbot \qquad \text{and} \qquad \sigma \le 0 \text{ on } \Ctop.
\end{equation}


\subsection{Separation of the bilateral active (contact) sets}  While by \eqref{eq:nondeg} the two contact sets $\Cbot, \Ctop$ are disjoint, that condition does not by itself say how far apart they are.  We quantify the gap here because it is the natural scale at which one expects the discrete classification of \S \ref{sec:discretization} to begin tracking the true contact sets.  The separation bound $r_0$ below is \emph{not} needed for any later result, though the uniform gap $\delta_{\min}$ defined next certainly is.

For the continuum problem, compactness of $\bar\Om$ allows us to quantify the separation of the contact sets, as follows.  First, let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:deltamin}
  \delta_{\min} := \min_{\bar\Om} (\psiup - \psilo) > 0,
\end{equation}
equivalently the largest constant with $\psilo + \delta_{\min} \le \psiup$ on $\bar\Om$.  The minimum is attained, and positive, by nondegeneracy \eqref{eq:nondeg}, continuity of $\psiup - \psilo$, and compactness of $\bar\Om$.

Let $L^\top \ge 0$ be the H\"older seminorm of $\psiup$ on $\bar\Om$, i.e.\ the least constant with $|\psiup(x) - \psiup(y)| \le L^\top |x-y|^\alpha$ for all $x,y \in \bar\Om$.  This value is finite since $\psiup \in C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Om)$, but note it can be zero.

At each point of $\Omega$, one of the two bounds \eqref{eq:K} must be slack, so a continuous $u$ is, near any contact point, the solution of a one-sided problem, where $f \in L^\infty(\Om)$ and a $C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Om)$ obstacle give $u \in C^{0,\beta}(\bar\Om)$ also \cite[Theorem 2.1]{FinziVita1982}, with $\beta < \alpha$ under inhomogeneous boundary conditions \cite{Biroli1979}.  Let $L_u$ be the H\"older seminorm for $u$.

Regarding possible division by zero in \eqref{eq:r0} below, we make the convention that a term is omitted from the minimum if its denominator vanishes, i.e.\ if $\psiup$ or $u$ is constant.  That is, we regard the quotients as positive infinity in such cases.

\begin{proposition}[Separation of the contact sets]
\label{prop:separation}
If $\Cbot, \Ctop$ are each non-empty
then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:r0}
  \operatorname{dist}(\Cbot, \Ctop) \ \ge\ r_0
  := \min\left\{ \left(\frac{\delta_{\min}}{2 L^\top}\right)^{1/\alpha},
                 \left(\frac{\delta_{\min}}{2 L_u}\right)^{1/\beta} \right\}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $x \in \Cbot$ and $y \in \Ctop$ be arbitrary, and set $r := |x-y|$.  Since $u(x) = \psilo(x)$,
\begin{equation}
  \delta_{\min} \le \psiup(x) - \psilo(x) = \psiup(x) - u(x).
\end{equation}
Insert $\psiup(y) = u(y)$ and use the two H\"older bounds:
\begin{equation}
  \delta_{\min} \le \bigl(\psiup(x) - \psiup(y)\bigr) + \bigl(u(y) - u(x)\bigr)
  \le L^\top r^\alpha + L_u r^\beta.
\end{equation}
If $r < r_0$, then
$L^\top r^\alpha < \delta_{\min}/2$ and $L_u r^\beta < \delta_{\min}/2$, by definition of $r_0$,  so $L^\top r^\alpha + L_u r^\beta < \delta_{\min}$, a contradiction. Thus
$r \ge r_0$, and $\operatorname{dist}(\Cbot,\Ctop) \ge r_0$ follows.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:separation} gives an explicit, data-determined lower bound $r_0$ on the spatial separation of the bilateral contact sets $\Cbot, \Ctop$.  Figure~\ref{fig:separation} illustrates the geometry.  The gap $\delta_{\min}$ is vertical, and determined directly by the obstacles alone, whereas $r_0$ is a horizontal distance in $\Om$ depending partly on the H\"older regularity of the solution $u$.  (Thus $r_0$ is controlled by the data functions $f,g,\psilo,\psiup$ through the regularity theory \cite{KinderlehrerStampacchia1980}.)  As it turns out, discretization \S \ref{sec:discretization}, next, does \emph{not} need to impose $h < c\,r_0$ as a hypothesis for the estimator to be well-defined and correct.  Instead, $r_0$'s role is to bound the width of the transition band so that the discrete classification tracks the true contact sets closely.

Separation hypotheses of this kind are expected in the bilateral setting.  For example, in the displacement obstacle problem of clamped Kirchhoff plates, a fourth-order bilateral problem, Brenner et al.~\cite{Brenneretal2012} assume that the two obstacles are separated from each other and from the boundary values, and under that hypothesis obtain $L^\infty$ convergence rates and approximation of the coincidence set, though \emph{a priori} in both cases.


\section{Discretization} \label{sec:discretization}

Let $\{\Th\}$ be a sequence of conforming partitions of $\bar\Om$ into \emph{closed} simplices, namely triangles or tetrahedra in dimensions $d=2,3$, respectively.  Let $\Nh$ be the node set of $\Th$ and $\Nhr \subset \Nh$ the interior nodes.  Finally, let $\Sh$ be the interior sides, i.e.~the interior edges or faces.

Given a (closed) simplex $T$, $h_T$ is its diameter and $\rho_T$ is the maximal radius of a ball contained in $T$.  We assume shape-regularity, namely a bound $h_T/\rho_T \le \gamma^*$ for all $T \in \Th$, with $\gamma^*$ independent of $h$.  We also write $h$ for the piecewise-constant \emph{mesh-size function} with $h|_T = h_T$, and $h_S$ for the diameter of a side $S$.

For $\omega \subset \bar\Om$ any subset, define its \emph{star} as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:star}
\Uh(\omega) := \bigcup\{T \in \Th : T \cap \omega \ne \emptyset\}.
\end{equation}
This set is a finite union of closed simplices, thus closed.  Note that ``$T \cap \omega \ne \emptyset$'' only requires $T$ to meet $\omega$ in a single point, so for example $\Uh(T)$ includes every simplex with a shared vertex, edge, or face.  For a node $z \in \Nh$, its star \cite{Langeetal2016} is $\Uh(z) := \Uh(\{z\})$.


\subsection{Piecewise-linear elements and nodal interpolation}

Let $\Vh$ be the space of continuous piecewise-linear finite elements over $\Th$, with
$\Vhr := \Vh \cap H_0^1(\Om)$ the subspace with zero boundary values.  We denote the nodal basis of $\Vh$ by $(\phi_h^z)_{z \in \Nh}$, and note $\Uh(z)=\operatorname{supp} \phi_h^z$.

Let $\Ih$ be the Lagrange interpolation operator onto $\Vh$, specifically $\Ih:C(\bar\Om) \to \Vh$.  As in \cite{NSV03} we write $\left< \cdot,\cdot\right>_h$ for the lumped $L_2$ scalar product,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lumped}
\left< v_h, w_h\right>_h := \int_\Om \Ih(v_h w_h), \qquad v_h, w_h \in \Vh.
\end{equation}
Also as in \cite{NSV03}, in what follows $C$ denotes different constants depending only on $\gamma^*$, $\Om$, and $d$, and $\preceq$ indicates $\le$ up to such a constant.

The following two properties are special to piecewise-\emph{linear} interpolation.  They need no acuteness or mesh-fineness assumption on $\Th$, and they do not hold generally for higher-order elements.

\begin{lemma}[Vanishing affine functions on simplices]
\label{lem:affine}
If $w$ is affine on a simplex $T$, and if $w \ge 0$ at every vertex of $T$, then $w\ge 0$ everywhere on $T$.  Furthermore, $w = 0$ somewhere in $T$ if and only if $w = 0$ at some vertex of $T$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Most parts are obvious affine function properties.  For ($\Rightarrow$) direction of the equivalence claim, write a point $x \in T$ in barycentric coordinates, $x = \sum_i \lambda_i x_i$, $\lambda_i \ge 0$, $\sum_i \lambda_i = 1$, so $w(x) = \sum_i \lambda_i w(x_i)$.  If $w(x_i) > 0$ for every vertex $x_i$, then $w(x) > 0$ for every $x \in T$ (some $\lambda_i > 0$ always, and all terms are non-negative with at least one strictly positive).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Order-preservation of $I_h$]
\label{lem:monotone}
If $w, \zeta \in C(\bar\Om)$ satisfy $w \le \zeta$ on $\bar\Om$, then
$\Ih w \le \Ih \zeta$ on $\bar\Om$, for any mesh $\Th$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By linearity of $\Ih$, $\Ih \zeta - \Ih w = \Ih(\zeta - w)$, and $\zeta - w \ge 0$ at every node. On each $T \in \Th$, $\Ih(\zeta-w)$ is affine, hence $\Ih(\zeta-w) \ge 0$ on $T$, for every $T$, by the Lemma \ref{lem:affine}.
\end{proof}

Set $\psiloh := \Ih \psilo$ and $\psiuph := \Ih \psiup$.  Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:monotone} to $\psilo \le \psiup$ gives only $\psiloh \le \psiuph$.  Applying it instead to the uniform-gap form of \eqref{eq:deltamin}, and using that $\Ih$ reproduces constants, gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:disc-nondeg}
  \psiuph - \psiloh \ \ge\ \delta_{\min} \ >\ 0 \qquad \text{on } \bar\Om.
\end{equation}
Note this holds for \emph{every} mesh, not just fine ones. The compatibility condition \eqref{eq:compat} gives a second consequence, $\psiloh \le \Ih g \le \psiuph$ on $\dOm$.  (Lemma~\ref{lem:monotone} cannot be applied directly.  However, noting that $\psilo \le g \le \psiup$ is assumed only on $\dOm$, not on all of $\bar\Om$, we apply the idea face by face over $\dOm$.)


\subsection{The finite-dimensional problem}

Define the discrete constraint set as in \eqref{eq:K}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Kh}
  \Kh := \{v_h \in \Vh \mid \psiloh \le v_h \le \psiuph \text{ in } \Om,
    \ v_h = \Ih g \text{ on } \dOm\},
\end{equation}
This set is closed and convex, and it is non-empty.  (To show it is non-empty, choose $v_h \in \Vh$ nodally, equal to $\Ih g$ at boundary nodes and anywhere in the non-empty interval $[\psiloh(z),\psiuph(z)]$ at interior nodes, and Lemma~\ref{lem:affine} promotes these nodal inequalities to all of $\bar\Om$.)  As noted by \cite{NSV03} for the unilateral case, $\Kh$ is in general not a subset of $\mathcal{K}$ defined by \eqref{eq:K}, so in this sense our approximation is non-conforming.

Because the constraints in \eqref{eq:Kh} compare piecewise-linear functions, admissibility is decided at the nodes alone.  Indeed, for $v_h \in \Vh$ the differences $v_h - \psiloh$ and $\psiuph - v_h$ are affine on each $T \in \Th$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:affine} promotes nodal inequalities to pointwise ones.  For functions satisfying $v_h = \Ih g$, we observe
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:nodalKh}
  v_h \in \Kh \quad\iff\quad \psiloh(z) \le v_h(z) \le \psiuph(z) \ \text{ for all } z \in \Nh.
\end{equation}

The discrete problem corresponding to \eqref{eq:VI} reads
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:discVI}
  u_h \in \Kh: \qquad
  \left< \nabla u_h, \nabla(v_h - u_h)\right> \ge \left< f, v_h - u_h\right>
  \qquad \text{for all } v_h \in \Kh,
\end{equation}
which again admits a unique solution.


\subsection{The discrete residual and its sign}

For interior nodes $z \in \Nhr$, define $\sigma_h \in \Vh$ nodally by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigmah}
  \left< \sigma_h, \phi_h^z \right>_h
  = \left< \nabla u_h, \nabla \phi_h^z \right> - \left< f, \phi_h^z \right>,
\end{equation}
whence $\sigma_h(z) = \bigl(\int_\Om \phi_h^z\bigr)^{-1}
\bigl(\left< \nabla u_h, \nabla \phi_h^z\right> - \left< f, \phi_h^z\right>\bigr)$.  This is \cite[formula (2.1)]{NSV03}, but here with the same sign convention as for $\sigma$ itself (see \eqref{eq:sigma}).  Note that while $\sigma \in H^{-1}(\Om)$ is a quite general object, generally a measure, $\sigma_h \in \Vh$ is a piecewise-linear function which merely assigns a real value for each interior node in the mesh.  (On the other hand, the reader may regard $\sigma_h$ as a collection of delta measures at points.)  Definition \eqref{eq:sigmah} is extended to boundary nodes below.
% also, \eqref{eq:sigmah} is the Galerkin (weak-form) instance of the strong-form nodal residual underlying \cite{BuelerFarrell2024}'s mixed complementarity classification in their \S 7, and it matches the convention already used by viamr's \texttt{nsv03mark()}

Observation \eqref{eq:nodalKh} above has a strong consequence for the sign of $\sigma_h$ at interior nodes.

\begin{proposition}[Nodal complementarity]
\label{prop:nodalsign}  Let $\Nhbot := \{z \in \Nh : u_h(z) = \psiloh(z)\}$ and $\Nhtop := \{z \in \Nh : u_h(z) = \psiuph(z)\}$ be the lower and upper \emph{nodal contact (active) sets}; they are disjoint by \eqref{eq:disc-nondeg}.  Then
\begin{align}
\label{eq:nodalcomp}
  z \in \Nhr \cap \Nhbot \ &\implies\ \sigma_h(z) \ge 0, \\
  z \in \Nhr \cap \Nhtop \ &\implies\ \sigma_h(z) \le 0, \notag \\
  z \in \Nhr \setminus (\Nhbot \cup \Nhtop) \ &\implies\ \sigma_h(z) = 0. \notag
\end{align}
Combining these, if $z \in \Nhr$ is an interior node then $\sigma_h(z) > 0$ implies $z \in \Nhbot$ and $\sigma_h(z) < 0$ implies $z \in \Nhtop$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $z\in \Nhr$ and $t \in \mathbb{R}$ set $v_h := u_h + t \phi_h^z \in \Vh$, so $v_h(z)=u_h(z)+t$.  Since $z$ is an interior node, $\phi_h^z$ vanishes on $\dOm$, so $v_h = u_h = \Ih g$ there.  At any node $z' \ne z$ we have $\phi_h^z(z') = 0$, hence $v_h(z') = u_h(z')$, which satisfies the bounds in \eqref{eq:nodalKh}, because $u_h \in \Kh$, so
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tfeasible}
  v_h \in \Kh \quad\iff\quad \psiloh(z) - u_h(z) \ \le\ t \ \le\ \psiuph(z) - u_h(z).
\end{equation}
This is a condition at the single node $z$; no hypothesis about the rest of $z$'s star is involved.  Testing \eqref{eq:discVI} with any such $v_h$, and using \eqref{eq:sigmah}, gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tsigma}
  t\,\sigma_h(z) \ \ge\ 0 \qquad \text{for every feasible } t.
\end{equation}

Suppose $z \in \Nhbot$, so $u_h(z) = \psiloh(z)$.  Then $\psiuph(z) - u_h(z) \ge \delta_{\min} > 0$ by \eqref{eq:disc-nondeg}, so every $t \in (0,\delta_{\min}]$ is feasible in \eqref{eq:tfeasible}; dividing \eqref{eq:tsigma} by $t > 0$ preserves the inequality and gives $\sigma_h(z) \ge 0$.  Symmetrically, if $z \in \Nhtop$ then every $t \in [-\delta_{\min},0)$ is feasible, and dividing by $t < 0$ \emph{reverses} the inequality, giving $\sigma_h(z) \le 0$.  Finally, if $z$ lies in neither set then $\psiloh(z) < u_h(z) < \psiuph(z)$, so all $t$ of sufficiently small modulus are feasible, of either sign; both preceding conclusions then apply and $\sigma_h(z) = 0$.  The three cases are exhaustive, and the first two are mutually exclusive, because $\Nhbot \cap \Nhtop = \emptyset$ by \eqref{eq:disc-nondeg}.
\end{proof}

In the unilateral setting of \cite{NSV03}, $\sigma_h \ge 0$ holds \emph{throughout} $\bar\Om$ (under our sign convention).  In that case, raising $u_h$ by a non-negative multiple of any nodal basis function preserves admissibility, so \eqref{eq:tsigma} is available with $t > 0$ at every interior node.  With two obstacles that fails, and $\sigma_h$ has no global sign.  The nodal statement in Proposition~\ref{prop:nodalsign} takes its place; it is the discrete form of complementarity structure \eqref{eq:sigmasign}.

Note that $\sigma_h$ vanishes at every strictly inactive interior node, so the nodal values of $\sigma_h$ can be non-zero only on the nodal active sets $\Nhbot \cup \Nhtop$.  However, if we take the piecewise-linear form of $\sigma_h$ then Proposition \ref{prop:nodalsign}'s nodal complementarity does not extend into the elements; if $\sigma_h$ is regarded as piecewise-linear then it can be non-zero in any element having an active vertex in $\Nhbot \cup \Nhtop$.


\subsection{Thinned/eroded element-wise active sets}

Extending \cite{NSV03}, for elements we partition $\Th$ into three parts according to whether the star \eqref{eq:star} of an element is fully-active:
\begin{align}
\label{eq:Thsplit}
  \Thbot &:= \{T \in \Th : \Uh(T) \subset \{u_h = \psiloh\}\}, \\
  \Thtop &:= \{T \in \Th : \Uh(T) \subset \{u_h = \psiuph\}\}, \notag \\
  \Thplus &:= \Th \setminus (\Thbot \cup \Thtop). \notag
\end{align}
The contact (active) sets $\Lamhbot$ and $\Lamhtop$ are defined as the corresponding unions of elements,
\begin{equation}
\Lamhbot = \bigcup_{T \in \Thbot} T, \qquad \Lamhtop = \bigcup_{T \in \Thtop} T.
\end{equation}
These subsets of $\bar\Omega$ are defined \emph{element-wise}, in contrast to the nodal sets of Proposition \ref{prop:nodalsign}.  Figure~\ref{fig:nodalelem} shows both kinds of set on a common mesh.

\begin{remark}[The VIAMR library computes $\Thbot$, $\Thtop$]
\label{rem:thinactive}
The active element sets in \eqref{eq:Thsplit} are exactly what the library's \texttt{thinelemactive()} method marks.  An element is marked only if it \emph{and every element sharing a node with it} is active.  This erodes, by one element ring, the larger element-wise active set computed by \texttt{elemactive()}, which marks an element active if all its nodes are active, i.e.~they are in $\Nhbot,\Nhtop$ respectively.  The thinning/erosion is precisely the content of ``$\Uh(T) \subset \{u_h = \psiloh\}$'' because $\Uh(T)$ includes the ring of neighbors of $T$, every $T' \in \Th$ meeting $T$ in a node or edge.  The library calls are
\begin{equation*}
\Thbot|\Thtop = \texttt{thinelemactive(uh, psiloh, boxside="lower"|"upper")}
\end{equation*}
Regarding the implementation, both \texttt{elemactive()} and \texttt{thinelemactive()} decide activity by evaluating the gap at a single DG0 degree of freedom, the cell centroid, and comparing to a tolerance, rather than by testing every vertex.  For piecewise-linear $u_h$ and $\psiloh$ the centroid value of $u_h - \psiloh$ is the average of the vertex values, and this gap is non-negative because $u_h \in \Kh$, so at zero tolerance the two tests agree.  At a tolerance \texttt{tol}$>0$ the centroid test is slightly weaker, but the tests coincide in the \texttt{tol}$\to 0$ limit.
\end{remark}

The element-wise sets \eqref{eq:Thsplit} promote the nodal signs of Proposition \ref{prop:nodalsign} to pointwise ones within the elements.  Suppose $z$ is an interior node and a vertex of $T \in \Thbot$, thus $\sigma_h(z) \ge 0$ by Proposition \ref{prop:nodalsign}.  If instead $z$ is a boundary node then, because every element containing $z$ meets $T$, we have $\Uh(z) \subset \Uh(T) \subset \{u_h = \psiloh\}$, and the boundary-node definition \eqref{eq:sigmah-bnd} below gives $\sigma_h(z) = \hat\sigma_h(z)^+ \ge 0$.  On $\Thplus$ elements no pointwise sign is claimed, but the discrete residual $\sigma_h$ is signed on the thinned contact elements.

\begin{corollary} \label{cor:sigmahsign}
$T \in \Thbot \implies \sigma_h \ge 0$ on $T$, and symmetrically $T \in \Thtop \implies \sigma_h \le 0$ on $T$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Conceptual importance of separation distance]
\label{rem:sepconcept}
Proposition~\ref{prop:separation}'s constant $r_0$ plays no role in the correctness of what follows, but it matters for \emph{sharpness}.  One expects that once $h \lesssim r_0$, $\Thplus$ separates the discrete free boundaries, rather than swallowing-up contact elements that should have been classified into $\Thbot$ or $\Thtop$.  Under refinement this element-wise separation of the bilateral contact sets is seen in the examples, but we do not prove it.
\end{remark}


\subsection{The residual at boundary nodes}

For a boundary node $z \in \Nh \setminus \Nhr$, the hat function $\phi_h^z$ does not vanish along $\dOm$, and an admissible $v_h\in\Kh$ satisfies $v_h = \Ih g$ there, so the stationarity argument of Proposition~\ref{prop:nodalsign} cannot be applied.  Following \cite[\S 2.1]{NSV03} we therefore define $\sigma_h(z)$ at boundary nodes by a diagnostic construction.

If $z\in\dOm$ then the bracket in \eqref{eq:sigmah} is no longer the whole residual: integrating $\left<\nabla u_h,\nabla\phi_h^z\right>$ by parts elementwise picks up an extra boundary flux term $\int_{\dOm} (\partial_n u_h)\,\phi_h^z$, where $\partial_n$ is the outward-normal derivative, taken elementwise. Write
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigmahat}
  \hat\sigma_h(z) := \Bigl(\int_\Om \phi_h^z\Bigr)^{-1}
  \Bigl[\left<\nabla u_h,\nabla\phi_h^z\right> - \left< f,\phi_h^z\right> - \int_{\dOm} (\partial_n u_h)\,\phi_h^z\Bigr],
  \qquad z \in \Nh\setminus\Nhr,
\end{equation}
for this boundary-corrected nodal residual, normalized exactly as in \eqref{eq:sigmah}.

Since \eqref{eq:sigmahat} is not derived from a stationarity argument using VI \eqref{eq:discVI}, $\hat\sigma_h(z)$ carries no guaranteed sign, but \cite{NSV03} handles this by keeping only the part which has the sign which convention \eqref{eq:sigmasign} demands, and discarding the rest.  We extend the same device to both obstacles, defining, for boundary nodes $z \in \Nh\setminus\Nhr$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigmah-bnd}
  \sigma_h(z) := \begin{cases}
    \hat\sigma_h(z)^+, & \text{if } \Uh(z) \subset \{u_h = \psiloh\}, \\
    -\hat\sigma_h(z)^-, & \text{if } \Uh(z) \subset \{u_h = \psiuph\}, \\
    0, & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $v^+ := \max\{v,0\}$ and $v^- := -\min\{v,0\}$ are the positive and negative parts of $v$.  Since $\hat\sigma_h(z)^+ \ge 0$ and $-\hat\sigma_h(z)^- \le 0$ by construction, \eqref{eq:sigmah-bnd} satisfies the discrete version of \eqref{eq:sigmasign} for boundary nodes by fiat.

Thus, at a boundary node whose star lies wholly in one active set, we keep the correctly-signed part of $\hat\sigma_h(z)$; at any other boundary node, including one whose star meets both contact types or neither, $\sigma_h(z)$ is simply set to zero.  Up to the sign convention already noted, the first case of \eqref{eq:sigmah-bnd} is exactly \cite[formula (2.2)]{NSV03}.


\subsection{Positivity-preserving interpolation}

We record here the ball-averaging interpolation operator of Chen \& Nochetto \cite{ChenNochetto2000}, since the constructions in \S 4 and \S 5 both use it.  The construction does not depend on the obstacles, so it is unchanged for the box-constrained case; we simply state it following \cite[\S 2.2]{NSV03}, and cite \cite{ChenNochetto2000} for proofs.

To define the interpolation operator $\Pih : L_1(\Om) \to \Vhr$, fix the mesh $\Th$ and then, for each $z \in \Nhr$, fix a ball $B(z) \subset \Uh(z)$, centered at $z$ and contained in its star, with radius comparable to the local mesh size near $z$.  On a non-uniform mesh the radius will vary from node to node; $B(z)$ is generally \emph{not} one ball translated throughout $\Om$.  The precise choice of radius, needed for \eqref{eq:Pihprops} to hold uniformly in $h$, does not concern us, but see \cite[\S 6]{NSV03}.

For $\varphi \in L_1(\Om)$, set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Pih}
  \Pih\varphi(z) := \frac{1}{|B(z)|}\int_{B(z)}\varphi, \qquad z \in \Nhr.
\end{equation}
Linearity is clear, because the balls $B(z)$ only depend on the mesh and not the input $\varphi$, and positivity is immediate: the average of a non-negative function over a ball is non-negative.  (We call a linear operator $A$ \emph{positive} if $\varphi \ge 0$ implies $A\varphi \ge 0$.)  Note $\Pih$ is not a projection: $\Pih^2 \ne \Pih$ in general.

Write $\|\cdot\|_{0,p;\omega}$ for the $L^p(\omega)$ norm over measurable $\omega \subset \bar\Om$.  Chen \& Nochetto \cite{ChenNochetto2000} show that, for every $1\le p\le\infty$, $T\in\Th$, and interior side $S\in\Sh$, and for $\varphi \in W_0^{1,p}(\Om)$,
\begin{align}
\label{eq:Pihprops}
  \|\nabla\Pih\varphi\|_{0,p;T} &\preceq \|\nabla\varphi\|_{0,p;\Uh(T)}, \\
  \|\varphi - \Pih\varphi\|_{0,p;T} &\preceq h_T\|\nabla\varphi\|_{0,p;\Uh(T)}, \notag \\
  \|\varphi - \Pih\varphi\|_{0,p;S} &\preceq h_S^{1-1/p}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{0,p;\Uh(S)}. \notag
\end{align}
If in addition $\varphi \in W_0^{1,1}(\Om)$ and $\nabla\varphi \in BV(\Om)^d$, then also
\begin{align}
\label{eq:PihBV}
  \|\varphi-\Pih\varphi\|_{0,1;T} &\preceq h_T^2\, |D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Uh(T)), \\
  \|\varphi-\Pih\varphi\|_{0,1;S} &\preceq h_S\, |D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Uh(S)), \notag
\end{align}
where $|D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}} := \sum_{i=1}^d |D\partial_i\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}$ sums the variation measures of the components of $\nabla\varphi$, and $|D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\omega) = \|D^2\varphi\|_{0,1;\omega}$ whenever $\varphi \in W^{2,1}(\Om)$ and $\omega \subset \Om$ is measurable.  We will need the properties \eqref{eq:Pihprops} and \eqref{eq:PihBV} in following sections; observe that they the interpolation error bound using stars.


\section{A posteriori estimator} \label{sec:estimator}

This section begins the construction of a bilateral form of the \cite{NSV03} estimator, which runs through \S \ref{sec:efficiency}.  Our estimator is assembled from the element-wise contact sets $\Thbot,\Thtop$ of \eqref{eq:Thsplit}, but it later localizes (\S \ref{sec:localized}) to the star-dilated contact sets which we define in \S \ref{sec:barriers}.

Write $\ThC := \Thbot \cup \Thtop$ for the set of elements whose stars are in contact with the obstacles; note $\ThC$ is the analog of $\mathcal{T}_h^0$ in \cite{NSV03}.  Recalling \eqref{eq:Thsplit}, we have partitioned the elements as follows (Figure \ref{fig:nodalelem}, right):
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:elemdisjoint}
\Th = \ThC \sqcup \Thplus = \Thbot \sqcup \Thtop \sqcup \Thplus.
\end{equation}

\subsection{The Galerkin functional}

Define $\tilde\sigma_h \in H^{-1}(\Om)$ by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigmatilde}
  \left<\tilde\sigma_h,\varphi\right> := \sum_{T\in\ThC}\int_T \sigma_h\varphi + \sum_{T\in\Thplus}\int_T \Ih(\sigma_h\Pih\varphi), \qquad \varphi \in H_0^1(\Om).
\end{equation}
This is exactly \cite[formula (3.1)]{NSV03}: on wholly-contact elements $\tilde\sigma_h$ agrees with $\sigma_h$ itself; on the other elements $\Thplus$, which are inactive or transitional, the test function is first replaced by its positivity-preserving interpolant $\Pih\varphi$ before pairing with $\sigma_h$.  Nothing in \eqref{eq:sigmatilde} distinguishes lower from upper contact, so we have kept \cite{NSV03}'s definition.

The \emph{Galerkin functional} $\Gh \in H^{-1}(\Om)$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Gh}
  \left<\Gh,\varphi\right> := -\left<\nabla u_h,\nabla\varphi\right> + \left< f + \tilde\sigma_h,\varphi\right>, \qquad \varphi \in H_0^1(\Om).
\end{equation}
This is \cite[formula (3.3)]{NSV03}, but with $\tilde\sigma_h$ entering with the opposite sign, consistent with our sign for $\sigma_h$.  Definition \eqref{eq:sigma} of the continuum residual $\sigma$ eliminates $f$ to give an equivalent expression of the Galerkin functional:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Ghagain}
\left<\Gh,\varphi\right> = \left<\nabla(u-u_h),\nabla\varphi\right> - \left<\sigma-\tilde\sigma_h,\varphi\right>.
\end{equation}
Thus $\Gh$ measures two mismatches at once, between $u$ and $u_h$, and between the exact residual $\sigma$ and its discrete surrogate $\tilde\sigma_h$.

Consider the application of $\Gh$ to a piecewise-linear function $\varphi_h$.  The following Lemma can be read as saying that the result would be zero if $\sigma_h$ and $\tilde \sigma_h$ were computing the same thing, and if the inner products $\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>$ and $\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>_h$ were the same.  Because these statements are not true, we ``almost'' have orthogonality.  Every step in the proof uses only the disjoint split $\Th = \ThC \sqcup \Thplus$, and unconditional definition \eqref{eq:sigmah} of $\sigma_h$ at interior nodes, so the result is independent of which obstacle contacts the (thinned) active elements $\ThC$.

\begin{lemma}[Almost Galerkin orthogonality]
\label{lem:almostGO}
For all $\varphi_h \in \Vhr$,
\begin{align}
\label{eq:almostGO}
  \left< \Gh, \varphi_h\right> &= \left<\tilde\sigma_h,\varphi_h\right> - \left< \sigma_h,\varphi_h\right>_h \\
  &= \sum_{T\in\ThC}\int_T \bigl(\sigma_h\varphi_h - \Ih(\sigma_h\varphi_h)\bigr)
  \ +\ \sum_{T\in\Thplus}\int_T \Ih\bigl(\sigma_h(\Pih\varphi_h - \varphi_h)\bigr). \notag
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $\varphi_h\in\Vhr$, \eqref{eq:sigmah} gives $\left<\nabla u_h,\nabla\varphi_h\right> = \left< f,\varphi_h\right> + \left<\sigma_h,\varphi_h\right>_h$: this is $\sigma_h$'s defining relation at interior nodes, and it holds at \emph{every} $z\in\Nhr$ unconditionally, so by linearity it holds for every $\varphi_h \in \Vhr$.  Substituting into \eqref{eq:Gh},
\[
  \left<\Gh,\varphi_h\right> = -\bigl(\left< f,\varphi_h\right> + \left<\sigma_h,\varphi_h\right>_h\bigr) + \left< f,\varphi_h\right> + \left<\tilde\sigma_h,\varphi_h\right> = \left<\tilde\sigma_h,\varphi_h\right> - \left<\sigma_h,\varphi_h\right>_h,
\]
the first equality in \eqref{eq:almostGO}.  For the second, $\left<\sigma_h,\varphi_h\right>_h = \sum_{T\in\Th}\int_T \Ih(\sigma_h\varphi_h)$ by definition \eqref{eq:lumped}.  Subtracting this from \eqref{eq:sigmatilde} and collecting, the $\ThC$ part gives $\int_T \bigl(\sigma_h\varphi_h - \Ih(\sigma_h\varphi_h)\bigr)$, and the $\Thplus$ part gives $\int_T \bigl(\Ih(\sigma_h\Pih\varphi_h) - \Ih(\sigma_h\varphi_h)\bigr) = \int_T \Ih\bigl(\sigma_h(\Pih\varphi_h-\varphi_h)\bigr)$ by linearity of $\Ih$.
\end{proof}

In the following Lemma, $\Rh$ is a residual estimator, a conceptual descendent of the one in Babuška \& Rheinboldt \cite{BabuskaRheinboldt1978}, while $\Qh$ is a quadrature error.  We represent $\Gh$ as \eqref{eq:Gh-repn}, leading to the essential \emph{a posteriori} bound \eqref{eq:Ghbound} at the end of this section.

\begin{lemma}[Representation formula for $\Gh$]
\label{lem:Gh-repn}
For each interior side $S \in \Sh$, let $T^-,T^+ \in \Th$ be the elements meeting along $S$, with unit normal $\nu$ pointing from $T^-$ to $T^+$, and write
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:jump}
  \jump{\partial_\nu u_h} := \partial_\nu u_h|_{T^+} - \partial_\nu u_h|_{T^-}
\end{equation}
for the jump across $S$.  Define $\Rh \in H^{-1}(\Om)$, and $\Qh \in \Vhr^*$, a linear functional on the discrete space, by
\begin{align}
\label{eq:RhQh}
  \left<\Rh,\varphi\right> &:= \sum_{S\in\Sh}\int_S \jump{\partial_\nu u_h}\,\varphi + \sum_{T\in\ThC}\int_T (f+\sigma_h)\varphi + \sum_{T\in\Thplus}\int_T f\varphi, \\
  \left<\Qh,\varphi_h\right> &:= \sum_{T\in\ThC}\int_T \bigl(\sigma_h\varphi_h - \Ih(\sigma_h\varphi_h)\bigr). \notag
\end{align}
Then, for $\varphi \in H_0^1(\Om)$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Gh-repn}
  \left<\Gh,\varphi\right> = \left<\Rh,\varphi-\Pih\varphi\right> + \left<\Qh,\Pih\varphi\right>.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Split $\left<\Gh,\varphi\right> = \left<\Gh,\varphi-\Pih\varphi\right> + \left<\Gh,\Pih\varphi\right>$, and evaluate each term.

For $\left<\Gh,\varphi-\Pih\varphi\right>$, use definition \eqref{eq:Gh} with $\varphi$ replaced by $\varphi - \Pih\varphi \in H_0^1(\Om)$.  Integrate $\left<\nabla u_h,\nabla(\varphi-\Pih\varphi)\right>$ by parts elementwise: $u_h$ is affine on each $T$, so no interior element term survives, leaving only the interior jump terms.  (There is no boundary term since $\varphi-\Pih\varphi$ vanishes on $\dOm$.)  Concretely, for any $v \in H_0^1(\Om)$ the two elements along $S$ contribute $\partial_\nu u_h|_{T^-}$ and $-\partial_\nu u_h|_{T^+}$, their outward normals at $S$ being $\nu$ and $-\nu$, so with convention \eqref{eq:jump}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:byparts}
  \left<\nabla u_h,\nabla v\right> = -\sum_{S\in\Sh}\int_S \jump{\partial_\nu u_h}\,v,
\end{equation}
and the leading minus sign in \eqref{eq:Gh} turns this into the first sum in \eqref{eq:RhQh}, defining $\Rh$.  For $\left<\tilde\sigma_h,\varphi-\Pih\varphi\right>$, use definition \eqref{eq:sigmatilde}.  On $\ThC$, $\tilde\sigma_h=\sigma_h$ directly, while on $\Thplus$, $\tilde\sigma_h$ pairs against $\Pih$ applied to the test function, and by linearity of $\Pih$, $\Pih(\varphi-\Pih\varphi) = \Pih\varphi - \Pih^2\varphi$.  Altogether,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Gh-first}
  \left<\Gh,\varphi-\Pih\varphi\right> = \left<\Rh,\varphi-\Pih\varphi\right> \ +\ \sum_{T\in\Thplus}\int_T \Ih\bigl(\sigma_h(\Pih\varphi-\Pih^2\varphi)\bigr).
\end{equation}

For $\left<\Gh,\Pih\varphi\right>$, apply Lemma~\ref{lem:almostGO} with $\varphi_h=\Pih\varphi\in\Vhr$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Gh-second}
  \left<\Gh,\Pih\varphi\right> = \left<\Qh,\Pih\varphi\right> \ -\ \sum_{T\in\Thplus}\int_T \Ih\bigl(\sigma_h(\Pih\varphi-\Pih^2\varphi)\bigr).
\end{equation}

Adding \eqref{eq:Gh-second} and \eqref{eq:Gh-first} and cancelling leaves \eqref{eq:Gh-repn}.
\end{proof}

As with Lemma~\ref{lem:almostGO}, this derivation only used the $\Th = \ThC \sqcup \Thplus$ split, $\sigma_h$'s definition, and linearity of $\Pih$ and $\Ih$; which obstacle is active on $\ThC$ is not relevant.  The cancellation at the end of the proof is exactly why $\tilde\sigma_h$, rather than $\sigma_h$ itself, appears in \eqref{eq:Gh}.  (An un-cancelled $\Thplus$ term in \eqref{eq:Gh-repn} would not shrink at the rate needed for a second-order estimator; see the discussion after \cite[formula (3.6)]{NSV03}.)

Note that the sign convention for jump \eqref{eq:jump} does not affect the localized estimator \eqref{eq:Rp} itself, which needs only $|\jump{\partial_\nu u_h}|$.

\subsection{Localized residual and quadrature estimators}

Following \cite[\S\S 4--5]{NSV03}, our next task is to bound $\Gh$ in a dual space larger than $H_0^1(\Om)$ itself.  As in \cite[\S 3.2]{NSV03}, let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:W}
  W := \{\varphi \in H_0^1(\Om) : \nabla\varphi \in BV(\Om)^d\},
\end{equation}
equipped with the seminorm $\varphi \mapsto |D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Om)$ already used in \eqref{eq:PihBV}.  This space is slightly larger than $W^{2,1}(\Om) \cap H_0^1(\Om)$, because $\nabla\varphi \in W^{1,1}(\Om)^d$ implies $\nabla\varphi \in BV(\Om)^d$ but not conversely, and it still contains the discrete space $\Vhr$; both facts matter below.

For $1 \le p \le \infty$, with dual exponent $p' = p/(p-1)$, define the \emph{localized residual} $R_p$, element-wise over $T\in\Th$, by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Rp}
  R_p|_T := h_T^{-1/p'}\,|T|^{-1/p}\,\bigl\|\jump{\partial_\nu u_h}\bigr\|_{0,p;\partial T \setminus \dOm}
  \ +\ \begin{cases} |f + \sigma_h|, & T \in \ThC, \\ |f|, & T \in \Thplus. \end{cases}
\end{equation}
This is \cite[formula (3.7)]{NSV03}, again with the sign of $\sigma_h$ flipped: the first term is the usual interior-flux-jump contribution over $\partial T$, and the second is the residual magnitude $|f+\sigma_h|$ on wholly-active elements, matching the volume density of $\Rh$ in \eqref{eq:RhQh}, or the bare source term $|f|$ elsewhere.  Nothing here refers to the nature of the active obstacle, so \eqref{eq:Rp} is unchanged from the unilateral case.

By the BV-approximation properties \eqref{eq:PihBV} of $\Pih$, and because shape-regularity gives the patches $\Uh(T)$, $\Uh(S)$ finite overlap, so that $\sum_{T\in\Th} |D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Uh(T)) \preceq |D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Om)$ and likewise over sides, definition \eqref{eq:RhQh} of $\Rh$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Rhbound}
  \bigl|\left<\Rh,\varphi-\Pih\varphi\right>\bigr| \ \preceq\ \|h^2 R_\infty\|_{0,\infty;\Om}\, |D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Om)
\end{equation}
for $\varphi \in W$.  This is \cite[formula (3.8)]{NSV03}.

The second term $\left<\Qh,\Pih\varphi\right>$ in the representation formula \eqref{eq:Gh-repn} accounts for the effect of quadrature.  It too is independent of obstacle type, and it already arises for an unconstrained linear problem.  For $v_h,w_h$ affine on $T$ the mass-lumping error satisfies $\bigl|\int_T (v_h w_h - \Ih(v_h w_h))\bigr| \preceq h_T^2 \int_T |\nabla v_h| |\nabla w_h|$; applying this with $v_h = \sigma_h$ and $w_h = \Pih\varphi$, summing over $T \in \ThC$, and using the H\"older inequality with the conjugate pair $(d,d')$ gives the first bound in \eqref{eq:Qhbound} below.  Applying the first bound in \eqref{eq:Pihprops} together with the Sobolev-type inequality $\|\nabla\varphi\|_{0,d';\Om} \preceq |D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Om)$, valid for $\varphi \in W$, gives the second.  Altogether we have \cite[formula (3.9)]{NSV03}, namely
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Qhbound}
  \bigl|\left<\Qh,\Pih\varphi\right>\bigr| \ \preceq\ \|h^2\nabla\sigma_h\|_{0,d;\Lamh}\, \|\nabla\Pih\varphi\|_{0,d';\Om}
  \ \preceq\ \|h^2\nabla\sigma_h\|_{0,d;\Lamh}\, |D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Om),
\end{equation}
where $\Lamh := \Lamhbot \cup \Lamhtop = \bigcup_{T\in\ThC} T$ is the region combining all wholly-contact elements.  We have generalized from the unilateral case, namely \cite[formula (2.4)]{NSV03}, to bilateral in the obvious way.

Bounds \eqref{eq:Rhbound} and \eqref{eq:Qhbound} use the same seminorm $|D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Om)$, over $\varphi\in W$, so by \eqref{eq:Gh-repn} they combine to bound $\Gh$ in a dual norm.  Specifically, for an operator $\mathcal{A} \in H^{-1}(\Om)$ define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dualnormbv}
\|\mathcal{A}\|_{-2,\infty;\Om} = \sup\bigl\{\left<\mathcal{A},\varphi\right> : \varphi\in W,\ |D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Om)\le 1\bigr\}.
\end{equation}
Applying this to $\Gh$, \eqref{eq:Rhbound} and \eqref{eq:Qhbound} yield
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Ghbound}
  \|\Gh\|_{-2,\infty;\Om} \preceq\ \|h^2 R_\infty\|_{0,\infty;\Om} \ +\ \|h^2\nabla\sigma_h\|_{0,d;\Lamh},
\end{equation}
which is formula (3.10) in \cite{NSV03}.  As with the Galerkin functional itself, nothing in its derivation distinguishes lower from upper contact.


\section{Barriers} \label{sec:barriers}

Following \cite[\S 4]{NSV03}, we now bracket the exact solution between continuous \emph{barriers}, $u_\bot \le u \le u^\top$, built from the discrete solution $u_h$, the data $g,\psi_\bot,\psi_\top$, and a Galerkin correction $w$.

In fact, $w \in H_0^1(\Om)$ is defined as the Riesz representation of the Galerkin functional:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:w}
  \int_\Om \nabla w \cdot \nabla\varphi = \left<\Gh,\varphi\right> \qquad \text{for all } \varphi \in H_0^1(\Om).
\end{equation}
As in \cite[\S 4]{NSV03}, $w$ is continuous; Lemma~\ref{lem:holder} below establishes H\"older continuity of $w$ in the box-constrained case.


\subsection{Reduction to two pairings}

The next Lemma considers candidate barriers, with values of ``$c$'' to be determined.  The gradient of each barrier is $\nabla u_h + \nabla w$, and thus certain reductions apply, and we find sufficient conditions in terms of $\sigma$ and $\tilde\sigma_h$, so that the candidates are in fact barriers.  These conditions will be satisfied in Propositions \ref{prop:upperbarrier} and \ref{prop:lowerbarrier} below.

\begin{lemma}[Barrier criterion]
\label{lem:barriercrit}
If $c \ge \|g - \Ih g\|_{0,\infty;\dOm}$ is a constant then the functions
\begin{align}
u^\top &:= u_h + w + c \label{eq:candc} \\
u_\bot &:= u_h + w - c \notag
\end{align}
satisfy
\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)] If $v := (u - u^\top)^+$ then $v \ge 0$, $v \in H_0^1(\Om) \cap C(\bar\Om)$, and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:barrierenergy}
  \|\nabla v\|_{0,2;\Om}^2 = \left<\sigma,v\right> - \left<\tilde\sigma_h,v\right>.
\end{equation}
Thus if $\left<\sigma,v\right> \le 0$ and $\left<\tilde\sigma_h,v\right> \ge 0$ then $u \le u^\top$ in $\Om$.
\item[(b)] If $v := (u_\bot - u)^+$ then $v \ge 0$, $v \in H_0^1(\Om) \cap C(\bar\Om)$, and
\begin{equation}
\|\nabla v\|_{0,2;\Om}^2 = \left<\tilde\sigma_h,v\right> - \left<\sigma,v\right>.
\end{equation}
Thus if $\left<\sigma,v\right> \ge 0$ and $\left<\tilde\sigma_h,v\right> \le 0$ then $u_\bot \le u$ in $\Om$.
\end{itemize}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We prove (a); part (b) is the same computation with the roles of the two functions in $v$ exchanged and the sign of $c$ reversed.

Since $w \in H_0^1(\Om)$ is continuous it vanishes on $\dOm$, while $u = g$ and $u_h = \Ih g$ there.  Hence $(u - u^\top)|_{\dOm} = (g - \Ih g) - c \le 0$, by the hypothesis on $c$, so $v$ vanishes on $\dOm$.  Also $v \ge 0$ by definition, and $v$ is continuous because $u$, $u_h$, and $w$ are, so $v\in H_0^1(\Om) \cap C(\bar\Om)$.  For \eqref{eq:barrierenergy}, recall $\nabla v = \nabla(u-u^\top)$ almost everywhere on $\{v > 0\}$ and $\nabla v = 0$ almost everywhere elsewhere.  Therefore, using $\nabla u^\top = \nabla u_h + \nabla w$, and then using \eqref{eq:w} and \eqref{eq:Ghagain} as well,
\[
  \|\nabla v\|_{0,2;\Om}^2 = \int_\Om \nabla(u - u^\top)\cdot\nabla v
  = \left<\nabla(u-u_h),\nabla v\right> - \left<\Gh,v\right>
  = \left<\sigma - \tilde\sigma_h,\,v\right>.
\]
Finally, if the two pairings have the stated signs then $\|\nabla v\|_{0,2;\Om} = 0$, so $v$ is constant, and $v \in H_0^1(\Om)$ then forces $v \equiv 0$.  That is, $u \le u^\top$.
\end{proof}

In applying this Lemma we will use different $c$ for the two barriers, but each will satisfy $c \ge \|g - \Ih g\|_{0,\infty;\dOm}$.

Consider the upper barrier, so that we need $\left<\sigma,v\right> \le 0$ and $\left<\tilde\sigma_h,v\right> \ge 0$.  In either part of the Lemma the function $v$ is continuous and non-negative, so, $\sigma$ being a signed Radon measure (\S \ref{sec:setup}), the pairing is integration \cite[Theorem 6.9]{KinderlehrerStampacchia1980}, and the Jordan decomposition $\sigma = \mu_\bot - \mu_\top$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigmapairing}
  \left<\sigma,v\right> = \int_\Om v\,d\mu_\bot - \int_\Om v\,d\mu_\top \ \le\ \int_\Om v\,d\mu_\bot .
\end{equation}
So the upper-contact measure is harmless for the upper barrier, and it suffices that $v$ vanish on $\Cbot$, where $\mu_\bot$ is supported.  This is the only role of the lower obstacle here, and it is the mechanism \cite{NSV03} already uses.

To show $\left<\tilde\sigma_h,v\right> \ge 0$ for the upper barrier, recall that definition \eqref{eq:sigmatilde} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigmatildesplit}
  \left<\tilde\sigma_h,v\right> = \sum_{T\in\Thbot}\int_T \sigma_h v \ +\ \sum_{T\in\Thtop}\int_T \sigma_h v \ +\ \sum_{T\in\Thplus}\int_T \Ih(\sigma_h \Pih v),
\end{equation}
in which the first sum is already non-negative, because $\sigma_h \ge 0$ pointwise on each $T \in \Thbot$ (Corollary \ref{cor:sigmahsign}) and $v \ge 0$.  The box constraint is relevant in the second and third sums: $\sigma_h \le 0$ on $\Thtop$ elements, and $\sigma_h$ has no determined sign on $\Thplus$ elements, so both can contribute negatively.  (In the unilateral case of \cite{NSV03} this does not happen, since there $\sigma_h \ge 0$ on all of $\bar\Om$ and hence $\left<\tilde\sigma_h,v\right> \ge 0$ for every $v \ge 0$.)  The upper barrier must therefore contain a second mechanism, which forces $v$ to vanish wherever $\sigma_h < 0$ can influence \eqref{eq:sigmatildesplit}.

Proposition \ref{prop:upperbarrier} below will set $c$ so that these considerations apply and we can get $u\le u^\top$.  The lower barrier is symmetric in every respect.


\subsection{Star-dilated strict contact sets}

Recalling definition \eqref{eq:sigmah}, let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Nhpm}
  \Nh^+ := \{z \in \Nh \,:\, \sigma_h(z) > 0\}, \qquad
  \Nh^- := \{z \in \Nh \,:\, \sigma_h(z) < 0\}
\end{equation}
be nodes at which the original discrete residual $\sigma_h$ is strictly signed, and let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omegas}
  \ombot := \Uh(\Nh^+), \qquad \omtop := \Uh(\Nh^-)
\end{equation}
be their star-dilations, each a union of closed elements by definition \eqref{eq:star}.  By Proposition~\ref{prop:nodalsign} at interior nodes, and by the first two cases of \eqref{eq:sigmah-bnd} at boundary nodes, $\Nh^+ \subset \Nhbot$ and $\Nh^- \subset \Nhtop$; a strictly-signed node is active, and the sign says against which obstacle.

The barrier proofs use two consequences of \eqref{eq:omegas}.  Regarding $\sigma_h$ as a piecewise-linear function, first note that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omegacontains}
  \{\sigma_h < 0\} \subset \omtop \qquad \text{and} \qquad \{\sigma_h > 0\} \subset \ombot,
\end{equation}
because if $\sigma_h(x) < 0$ then, $\sigma_h$ being affine on any element $T$ containing $x$, some vertex $z$ of $T$ has $\sigma_h(z) < 0$, and then $T \subset \Uh(z) \subset \omtop$.  Second, $B(z) \subset \Uh(z) \subset \omtop$ for every $z \in \Nh^- \cap \Nhr$, by the construction of the balls in \S \ref{sec:discretization}, and symmetrically for $\ombot$.

\begin{remark}[Why the star, and not the bare set $\{\sigma_h<0\}$]
\label{rem:whystar}
The corresponding set in \cite[formula (4.6)]{NSV03} is not dilated.  There the third sum in \eqref{eq:sigmatildesplit} is handled instead by an argument which needs the global sign: if $v > 0$ at an interior point of an element $T$, then $\sigma_h$ vanishes at that point, and an affine function of one sign on $T$ which vanishes at an interior point of $T$ vanishes identically on $T$, hence at the vertices of $T$ as well.  Bilaterally that reasoning fails exactly where it is needed.  A node $z \in \Nh^-$ can be a vertex of a transition element $T \in \Thplus$ on which $\sigma_h$ changes sign, and then $v > 0$ at a point of $T$ where $\sigma_h \ge 0$ is no contradiction, so nothing forces $(\Pih v)(z) = 0$.  Dilating by one ring of elements restores the conclusion directly, at the cost of measuring the barrier term on a slightly larger set.
\end{remark}


\subsection{Defining the barrier functions}

\begin{proposition}[Upper barrier]
\label{prop:upperbarrier}
The function
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ubarupper}
  u^\top := u_h + w + \|w\|_{0,\infty;\Om} + \|g-\Ih g\|_{0,\infty;\dOm} + \|(\psilo-u_h)^+\|_{0,\infty;\Om} + \|(\psiup-u_h)^+\|_{0,\infty;\omtop}
\end{equation}
satisfies $u \le u^\top$ in $\Om$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $c$ denote the sum of the four constants in \eqref{eq:ubarupper}, so that $u^\top = u_h + w + c$ and $c \ge \|g - \Ih g\|_{0,\infty;\dOm}$.  Set $v := (u - u^\top)^+$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:barriercrit}(a) it suffices to show $\left<\sigma,v\right> \le 0$ and $\left<\tilde\sigma_h,v\right> \ge 0$.

Everything follows from two pointwise lower bounds for $u^\top$.  Because $w + \|w\|_{0,\infty;\Om} \ge 0$ and the remaining constants in \eqref{eq:ubarupper} are non-negative, we have:
\begin{align}
\label{eq:utopboundOm}
  u^\top &\ge\ u_h + (\psilo - u_h)^+ \ \ge\ \psilo \quad\text{in } \Om, \\
\label{eq:utopboundomtop}
  u^\top &\ge\ u_h + (\psiup - u_h)^+ \ \ge\ \psiup \quad\text{on } \omtop.
\end{align}

For the first pairing $\left<\sigma,v\right>$, bound \eqref{eq:utopboundOm} gives $\{v>0\} = \{u > u^\top\} \subset \{u > \psilo\} = \Om \setminus \Cbot$.  Thus $v = 0$ on $\Cbot$, which supports $\mu_\bot$, and \eqref{eq:sigmapairing} gives $\left<\sigma,v\right> \le \int_\Om v \,d\mu_\bot = 0$.

For the second pairing $\left<\tilde\sigma_h,v\right>$, bound \eqref{eq:utopboundomtop}, together with $u \le \psiup$ in $\Om$, gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:vzerotop}
  v \equiv 0 \quad \text{on } \omtop.
\end{equation}
It follows that each of the three sums in representation \eqref{eq:sigmatildesplit} is non-negative.  Here we look at these sums individually:
\renewcommand{\labelenumi}{(\roman{enumi})}
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $T \in \Thbot$ then $\sigma_h \ge 0$ pointwise on $T$ and $v \ge 0$, so $\int_T \sigma_h v \ge 0$.

\item If $T \in \Thtop$ then $\sigma_h \le 0$ is affine on $T$, so either $\sigma_h \equiv 0$ on $T$, and $\int_T \sigma_h v = 0$, or $\sigma_h(z) < 0$ at some vertex $z$ of $T$.  In the latter case $z \in \Nh^-$, hence $T \subset \Uh(z) \subset \omtop$, and $\int_T \sigma_h v = 0$ by \eqref{eq:vzerotop}.

\item If $T \in \Thplus$, let $z_0,\dots,z_d$ be its vertices.  Since $\Ih(\sigma_h \Pih v)$ is affine on $T$, its integral is $|T|$ times the average of its vertex values:
\[
  \int_T \Ih(\sigma_h \Pih v) = \frac{|T|}{d+1}\sum_{i=0}^d \sigma_h(z_i)\,(\Pih v)(z_i).
\]
A boundary vertex contributes nothing, because $\Pih v \in \Vhr$ vanishes there.  At an interior vertex $z_i$ we have $(\Pih v)(z_i) \ge 0$, by $v \ge 0$ and positivity of $\Pih$, so the $i$th term is non-negative unless $\sigma_h(z_i) < 0$.  But if $\sigma_h(z_i) < 0$ then $z_i \in \Nh^-$, so $B(z_i) \subset \omtop$, and then \eqref{eq:Pih} and \eqref{eq:vzerotop} give $(\Pih v)(z_i) = |B(z_i)|^{-1}\int_{B(z_i)} v = 0$.  Hence every term of the sum is non-negative.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent The conditions of Lemma \ref{lem:barriercrit}(a) hold, so $u\le u^\top$ in $\Om$.
\end{proof}

When using representation \eqref{eq:sigmatildesplit} in the above proof, the first two sums used the pointwise signs of $\sigma_h$ on wholly-active elements, while the third used only nodal values of $\sigma_h$ and the ball averages defining $\Pih$.  In particular no hypothesis relating $h$ to the geometry of the contact sets is needed, and the separation constant $r_0$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:separation} plays no role.

\begin{proposition}[Lower barrier]
\label{prop:lowerbarrier}
The function
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ubarlower}
  u_\bot := u_h + w - \|w\|_{0,\infty;\Om} - \|g-\Ih g\|_{0,\infty;\dOm} - \|(u_h-\psiup)^+\|_{0,\infty;\Om} - \|(u_h-\psilo)^+\|_{0,\infty;\ombot}
\end{equation}
satisfies $u_\bot \le u$ in $\Om$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The construction mirrors Proposition~\ref{prop:upperbarrier}, exchanging $\bot \leftrightarrow \top$ and reversing all signs.  Writing $c$ for the sum of the four constants, $u_\bot = u_h + w - c$ with $c \ge \|g-\Ih g\|_{0,\infty;\dOm}$, and $v := (u_\bot - u)^+$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:barriercrit}(b) asks for $\left<\sigma,v\right> \ge 0$ and $\left<\tilde\sigma_h,v\right> \le 0$.  The two pointwise upper bounds replacing \eqref{eq:utopboundOm} and \eqref{eq:utopboundomtop} are
\begin{align*}
  u_\bot &\le\ u_h - (u_h - \psiup)^+ \ \le\ \psiup \quad\text{in } \Om, \\
  u_\bot &\le\ u_h - (u_h - \psilo)^+ \ \le\ \psilo \quad\text{on } \ombot.
\end{align*}

The first of these inequalities gives $\{v>0\} \subset \Om \setminus \Ctop$, so $v$ vanishes on the support of $\mu_\top$ and \eqref{eq:sigmapairing} yields $\left<\sigma,v\right> = \int_\Om v \,d\mu_\bot \ge 0$.

The second of the above inequalities gives $v \equiv 0$ on $\ombot$, since $u \ge \psilo$ in $\Om$.  As in the upper barrier case, consider \eqref{eq:sigmatildesplit} term by term.  If $T \in \Thtop$ then $\sigma_h \le 0$ pointwise on $T$ and $v \ge 0$, so $\int_T \sigma_h v \le 0$.  If $T \in \Thbot$ then $\sigma_h \ge 0$ is affine on $T$, so either $\sigma_h \equiv 0$ on $T$, or $\sigma_h(z) > 0$ at some vertex $z$, in which case $z \in \Nh^+$ and $T \subset \Uh(z) \subset \ombot$, where $v$ vanishes; either way $\int_T\sigma_h v = 0$.  If $T \in \Thplus$ then, as before, $\int_T \Ih(\sigma_h\Pih v)$ is $|T|$ times the average $(d+1)^{-1}\sum_i \sigma_h(z_i)(\Pih v)(z_i)$, in which boundary vertices contribute nothing, interior vertices with $\sigma_h(z_i) \le 0$ contribute non-positively because $(\Pih v)(z_i) \ge 0$, and interior vertices with $\sigma_h(z_i) > 0$ have $B(z_i) \subset \ombot$ and hence $(\Pih v)(z_i) = 0$.  Thus $\left<\tilde\sigma_h,v\right> \le 0$, and \eqref{eq:ubarlower} follows.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Initial pointwise error estimate}

Subtracting $u_h$ from the two barriers gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:maxbarriers}
|u-u_h| \le \max \{|u_\bot-u_h|, |u^\top-u_h|\}
\end{equation}
pointwise, thus Propositions~\ref{prop:upperbarrier} and~\ref{prop:lowerbarrier} give
\begin{align}
\label{eq:barrierbound}
  \|u-u_h\|_{0,\infty;\Om} \ \le\ & 2\|w\|_{0,\infty;\Om} + \|g-\Ih g\|_{0,\infty;\dOm} \\
  &+ \|(\psilo-u_h)^+\|_{0,\infty;\Om} + \|(u_h-\psiup)^+\|_{0,\infty;\Om} \notag \\
  &+ \|(\psiup-u_h)^+\|_{0,\infty;\omtop} + \|(u_h-\psilo)^+\|_{0,\infty;\ombot}, \notag
\end{align}
the box-constrained form of \cite[formula (4.8)]{NSV03}.  (A maximum of the two lines of obstacle terms would do, but we prefer the symmetric expression.)  Besides $\|w\|_{0,\infty;\Om}$, every term on the right is computable from the discrete solution and the given data.

The four obstacle terms in \eqref{eq:barrierbound} come in two different kinds.  The two which are suprema over $\Om$ measure the failure of $u_h$ to be admissible for the continuum problem, and they are controlled by the obstacle interpolation errors: since $u_h \ge \psiloh$ and $u_h \le \psiuph$ in $\Om$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:violationbound}
  \|(\psilo-u_h)^+\|_{0,\infty;\Om} \le \|\psilo-\psiloh\|_{0,\infty;\Om}, \quad
  \|(u_h-\psiup)^+\|_{0,\infty;\Om} \le \|\psiup-\psiuph\|_{0,\infty;\Om},
\end{equation}
each therefore $O(h^2)$ for obstacles $\psilo,\psiloh \in W^{2,\infty}(\Om)$.

The other two obstacle terms in \eqref{eq:barrierbound}, suprema over $\omtop,\ombot$ respectively, are not interpolation errors.  They measure how far $u_h$ has detached from an obstacle on the region where the discrete residual asserts that it is attached, and they are the box-constrained analogue of the localized term in \cite[formula (4.6)]{NSV03}.

\begin{remark}[Optimal, non-computable choice]
\label{rem:optimalchoice}
As in \cite[Remark 4.3]{NSV03}, the proofs of Propositions~\ref{prop:upperbarrier} and~\ref{prop:lowerbarrier} go through with $\|(u-u_h)^+\|_{0,\infty;\omtop}$ and $\|(u_h-u)^+\|_{0,\infty;\ombot}$ in place of the last two terms, since only $u \le u^\top$ on $\omtop$, and $u_\bot \le u$ on $\ombot$, were used.  These are the optimal choices, and they are trivially efficient, but they are not computable.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The unilateral case]
\label{rem:unilateral}
If the upper obstacle is absent then $\sigma_h \ge 0$ throughout $\bar\Om$, so $\Nh^- = \emptyset$ and $\omtop = \emptyset$, and \eqref{eq:ubarupper} is exactly \cite[formula (4.2)]{NSV03}.  The lower barrier \eqref{eq:ubarlower} then reduces to \cite[formula (4.6)]{NSV03} except that our localized term is measured over $\ombot$ rather than over $\{\sigma_h>0\}$, which by \eqref{eq:omegacontains} is a subset.  As Remark~\ref{rem:whystar} explains, this one-ring enlargement is what replaces the global sign; we have not tried to remove it in the unilateral case.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Consequence for efficiency]
\label{rem:effring}
The local lower bounds of \cite[Prop.~6.3]{NSV03} for the localized term are stated for elements $T$ meeting $\{\sigma_h<0\}$, and already involve $\Uh(T)$ on the right-hand side.  Because our terms are measured over the star-dilated sets \eqref{eq:omegas}, the corresponding efficiency statement here will concern elements meeting $\ombot$ or $\omtop$, and might be expected to involve one further ring of elements.  In fact it does not; see Proposition~\ref{prop:effdetach} and Remark~\ref{rem:noextraring}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Full localization and barrier sets]
\label{rem:localization}
Our estimator localizes to the star-dilated sets \eqref{eq:omegas} and not to the contact sets themselves; see Remark~\ref{rem:unilateral}.  For the unilateral problem, \cite{NSV05} closes the corresponding gap, producing estimators which, apart from obstacle resolution, vanish identically within the full-contact set, and then uses the pointwise bounds to construct barrier sets, that is, computable regions containing the free boundary, under a stability condition there.  We regard the bilateral analogues of both refinements as open, and intend to pursue them; \S\S \ref{sec:localized} and \ref{sec:barriersets} say what each requires.  Note that the bilateral problem has two free boundaries, so bilateral barrier sets would have to be shown disjoint before they localize either one; Proposition~\ref{prop:separation} is the natural tool, since $r_0$ bounds $\operatorname{dist}(\Cbot,\Ctop)$ from below in terms of the data.
\end{remark}

Bounding $\|w\|_{0,\infty;\Om}$ is a question about the linear problem \eqref{eq:w} only, and it is taken up next.


\section{Pointwise estimates} \label{sec:pointwise}

Estimate \eqref{eq:barrierbound} is computable except for $\|w\|_{0,\infty;\Om}$, and $w$ solves the linear problem \eqref{eq:w}.  The obstacles enter that problem only as the data $\Gh$, and \S \ref{sec:estimator} has already put $\Gh$ into a form which does not distinguish them.  Consequently this section follows \cite[\S 5]{NSV03} without structural change, and the box constraint appears only in the readings $\ThC = \Thbot \cup \Thtop$ inside \eqref{eq:Rp} and $\Lamh = \Lamhbot \cup \Lamhtop$ in \eqref{eq:Qhbound}.  We write the argument out anyway, both because those readings have to be checked and because the section supplies the continuity of $w$ used in \S \ref{sec:barriers}.

The difficulty addressed here is a mismatch of orders.  What linear theory controls directly is a H\"older norm of $w$, and only by a first-order quantity, whereas $\|u-u_h\|_{0,\infty;\Om}$ must be bounded by a second-order estimator.  Following \cite[\S 5.1]{NSV03}, the resolution comes in three steps.  We first bound $w$ in $C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Om)$ by the first-order quantity.  We then bound a mollified point value $\left<\delta,w\right>$ by a second-order quantity, using a regularized Green's function.  Finally we combine the two, in such a way that the first-order term acquires a positive power of $h_{\min}$ and is thereby demoted to second order.


\subsection{A first-order bound on the Galerkin functional}

Bound \eqref{eq:Ghbound} controls $\Gh$ in the dual norm \eqref{eq:dualnormbv}, which is built on the seminorm $|D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Om)$.  The H\"older estimate needs instead the dual norm of $W_0^{1,p'}(\Om)$, built on $\|\nabla\varphi\|_{0,p';\Om}$, and this is where the first-order quantity arises.

\begin{lemma}[$L^p$ bound on $\Gh$]
\label{lem:Ghp}
Let $1 \le p \le \infty$, with dual exponent $p' = p/(p-1)$.  Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Ghp}
  \bigl|\left<\Gh,\varphi\right>\bigr| \ \preceq\ \Bigl(\|h R_p\|_{0,p;\Om} + \|h^2\nabla\sigma_h\|_{0,p;\Lamh}\Bigr)\,\|\nabla\varphi\|_{0,p';\Om}
\end{equation}
for all $\varphi \in W_0^{1,p'}(\Om)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Start from the representation formula \eqref{eq:Gh-repn}.  Write $\varrho_T := f + \sigma_h$ if $T\in\ThC$ and $\varrho_T := f$ if $T\in\Thplus$, matching the second term of \eqref{eq:Rp}.  For the residual part of \eqref{eq:Gh-repn}, apply H\"older's inequality on each side and element appearing in definition \eqref{eq:RhQh} of $\Rh$, and then the second and third estimates of \eqref{eq:Pihprops}, the latter two with exponent $p'$, so that $h_S^{1-1/p'} = h_S^{1/p}$:
\begin{align*}
  \bigl|\left<\Rh,\varphi-\Pih\varphi\right>\bigr|
  &\le \sum_{S\in\Sh} \bigl\|\jump{\partial_\nu u_h}\bigr\|_{0,p;S}\,\|\varphi-\Pih\varphi\|_{0,p';S}
     + \sum_{T\in\Th} \|\varrho_T\|_{0,p;T}\,\|\varphi-\Pih\varphi\|_{0,p';T} \\
  &\preceq \sum_{S\in\Sh} h_S^{1/p}\bigl\|\jump{\partial_\nu u_h}\bigr\|_{0,p;S}\,\|\nabla\varphi\|_{0,p';\Uh(S)}
     + \sum_{T\in\Th} h_T\|\varrho_T\|_{0,p;T}\,\|\nabla\varphi\|_{0,p';\Uh(T)}.
\end{align*}
In definition \eqref{eq:Rp} the side term of $R_p|_T$ carries the factor $h_T^{-1/p'}|T|^{-1/p}$, so, after a discrete H\"older inequality in the pair $(p,p')$ and the finite overlap of the patches $\Uh(T)$ and $\Uh(S)$, both sums are bounded by $\|hR_p\|_{0,p;\Om}\,\|\nabla\varphi\|_{0,p';\Om}$.

For the quadrature part, the mass-lumping bound already used for \eqref{eq:Qhbound}, but now with the H\"older pair $(p,p')$ in place of $(d,d')$, together with the first estimate of \eqref{eq:Pihprops} and the same finite overlap, gives
\[
  \bigl|\left<\Qh,\Pih\varphi\right>\bigr| \ \preceq\ \|h^2\nabla\sigma_h\|_{0,p;\Lamh}\,\|\nabla\Pih\varphi\|_{0,p';\Om}
  \ \preceq\ \|h^2\nabla\sigma_h\|_{0,p;\Lamh}\,\|\nabla\varphi\|_{0,p';\Om}.
\]
Adding the two bounds gives \eqref{eq:Ghp}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[H\"older continuity of $w$]
\label{lem:holder}
The Riesz representation $w$ of \eqref{eq:w} is H\"older continuous.  More precisely, for every $p > d$ there is $\alpha \in (0,1)$ such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:holder}
  \|w\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Om)} \ \preceq\ \|h R_p\|_{0,p;\Om} + \|h^2\nabla\sigma_h\|_{0,p;\Lamh},
\end{equation}
where the hidden constant depends on $\Om$ and $p$, and blows up as $p \downarrow d$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The classical H\"older estimate of De Giorgi and Nash, applied to \eqref{eq:w}, gives $\|w\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Om)} \preceq \|\Gh\|_{-1,p;\Om}$, where
\[
  \|\Gh\|_{-1,p;\Om} := \sup\bigl\{\left<\Gh,\varphi\right> \,:\, \varphi \in W_0^{1,p'}(\Om),\ \|\nabla\varphi\|_{0,p';\Om} \le 1\bigr\}
\]
is the norm of the dual space of $W_0^{1,p'}(\Om)$; see \cite[Theorem C.2]{KinderlehrerStampacchia1980}.  Now apply Lemma~\ref{lem:Ghp}.
\end{proof}

This justifies the continuity of $w$ used in \S \ref{sec:barriers}.  Note that the box constraint enters \eqref{eq:holder} only through $\ThC$ inside $R_p$, and through $\Lamh$; the linear theory behind the De Giorgi--Nash estimate knows nothing of the obstacles.


\subsection{Maximum norm of \texorpdfstring{$w$}{w}}

The right side of \eqref{eq:holder} is first order in $h$, so \eqref{eq:holder} alone is not enough.  Since $w$ is continuous and vanishes on $\dOm$, choose $x_0\in\Om$ with $|w(x_0)| = \|w\|_{0,\infty;\Om}$.  The uniform cone property of $\Om$ \cite[\S 4.7]{Adams1975} then provides a ball $B \subset \Om$ of radius $\rho = C h_{\min}^\beta$, with $\operatorname{dist}(x_0,B) \preceq \rho$, where $h_{\min} := \min_{T\in\Th} h_T$ and $\beta \ge 1$ is chosen below.  Let $\delta \in C_0^\infty(\Om)$ be a regularization of the Dirac mass at $x_0$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:delta}
  \operatorname{supp}\delta \subset B, \qquad \int_\Om \delta = 1, \qquad 0 \le \delta \preceq \rho^{-d}.
\end{equation}
Because $w$ is continuous and $\delta$ has unit mass, there is $x_1 \in B$ with $\left<\delta,w\right> = w(x_1)$, and then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:wsplit}
  \|w\|_{0,\infty;\Om} \ \le\ \bigl|\left<\delta,w\right>\bigr| + |w(x_0)-w(x_1)|.
\end{equation}
Since $|x_0 - x_1| \preceq \rho = C h_{\min}^\beta$, Lemma~\ref{lem:holder} bounds the second term of \eqref{eq:wsplit} by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:holderapplied}
  |w(x_0)-w(x_1)| \ \preceq\ h_{\min}^{\alpha\beta}\Bigl(\|hR_p\|_{0,p;\Om} + \|h^2\nabla\sigma_h\|_{0,p;\Lamh}\Bigr).
\end{equation}

The first term of \eqref{eq:wsplit} is handled by the regularized Green's function $G \in H_0^1(\Om)$, defined by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:green}
  \left<\nabla G,\nabla\varphi\right> = \left<\delta,\varphi\right> \qquad \text{for all } \varphi\in H_0^1(\Om),
\end{equation}
for which the a priori bound
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:greenbound}
  \|D^2 G\|_{0,1;\Om} + \|DG\|_{0,d/(d-1);\Om} \ \preceq\ |\log h_{\min}|^2
\end{equation}
holds on any polyhedral domain, proved in \cite{Nochetto1995} for $d=2$ and in \cite{Darietal2000} for $d=3$; the hidden constant depends on $\beta$ through $\rho$.  In particular $G \in W^{2,1}(\Om)\cap H_0^1(\Om) \subset W$, so $|D^2G|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Om) = \|D^2G\|_{0,1;\Om}$ and $G$ is admissible in both \eqref{eq:Rhbound} and \eqref{eq:Qhbound}.

\begin{lemma}[Mollified point value]
\label{lem:mollified}
The regularized Dirac mass \eqref{eq:delta} and the Riesz representation $w$ of \eqref{eq:w} satisfy
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mollified}
  \bigl|\left<\delta,w\right>\bigr| \ \preceq\ |\log h_{\min}|^2 \Bigl(\|h^2R_\infty\|_{0,\infty;\Om} + \|h^2\nabla\sigma_h\|_{0,d;\Lamh}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
where the hidden constant depends on $\beta$ through $\rho$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Taking $\varphi = w$ in \eqref{eq:green}, then $\varphi = G$ in \eqref{eq:w}, and then applying the representation formula \eqref{eq:Gh-repn},
\[
  \left<\delta,w\right> = \left<\nabla G,\nabla w\right> = \left<\Gh,G\right> = \left<\Rh,G-\Pih G\right> + \left<\Qh,\Pih G\right>.
\]
Bound \eqref{eq:Rhbound} controls the first term by $\|h^2R_\infty\|_{0,\infty;\Om}\|D^2G\|_{0,1;\Om}$.  The first inequality of \eqref{eq:Qhbound}, followed by the first estimate of \eqref{eq:Pihprops} with exponent $d' = d/(d-1)$, controls the second by $\|h^2\nabla\sigma_h\|_{0,d;\Lamh}\|DG\|_{0,d/(d-1);\Om}$.  Now apply \eqref{eq:greenbound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Pointwise estimate of $|w|$]
\label{prop:wbound}
The maximum norm of the Riesz representation $w$ of \eqref{eq:w} satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:wbound}
  \|w\|_{0,\infty;\Om} \ \preceq\ |\log h_{\min}|^2\Bigl(\|h^2R_\infty\|_{0,\infty;\Om} + \|h^2\nabla\sigma_h\|_{0,d;\Lamh}\Bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix $p > d$, let $\alpha$ be as in Lemma~\ref{lem:holder}, and choose $\beta = 1/\alpha$, so that $h_{\min}^{\alpha\beta} = h_{\min}$ in \eqref{eq:holderapplied}.  Given \eqref{eq:wsplit} and Lemma~\ref{lem:mollified}, it suffices to show that the right side of \eqref{eq:holderapplied} is then dominated by the right side of \eqref{eq:wbound}.  We may assume $\operatorname{diam}\Om \le 1$, since rescaling the geometry costs only a constant.

For the residual term, $R_p|_T \preceq R_\infty|_T$ pointwise on every $T$, uniformly in $p$: the volume terms of \eqref{eq:Rp} agree, while $\|\jump{\partial_\nu u_h}\|_{0,p;\partial T\setminus\dOm} \le |\partial T|^{1/p}\,\|\jump{\partial_\nu u_h}\|_{0,\infty;\partial T\setminus\dOm}$, and shape regularity turns the resulting factor $h_T^{-1/p'}|T|^{-1/p}|\partial T|^{1/p}$ into $h_T^{-1}$.  Hence, using $h_{\min}\le h_T$ and the boundedness of $\Om$,
\[
  h_{\min}\|hR_p\|_{0,p;\Om} \le \|h^2R_p\|_{0,p;\Om} \preceq \|h^2R_\infty\|_{0,\infty;\Om}.
\]

For the quadrature term, $\nabla\sigma_h$ is constant on each element, so $\|h^2\nabla\sigma_h\|_{0,p;\Lamh} \approx (\sum_T a_T^p)^{1/p}$ with $a_T := h_T^{2+d/p}|\nabla\sigma_h|_T$, the sum running over the elements of $\Lamh$; the case $p=d$ of the same identity has $a_T$ replaced by $b_T := h_T^{3}|\nabla\sigma_h|_T$.  Now $h_{\min} a_T \le b_T$, because $h_{\min} \le h_T \le h_T^{1-d/p}$ under $\operatorname{diam}\Om\le 1$, and $\ell^p \subset \ell^d$ with $(\sum_T c_T^p)^{1/p} \le (\sum_T c_T^d)^{1/d}$ because $p > d$.  Therefore
\[
  h_{\min}\|h^2\nabla\sigma_h\|_{0,p;\Lamh} \preceq \Bigl(\sum_T b_T^d\Bigr)^{1/d} \approx \|h^2\nabla\sigma_h\|_{0,d;\Lamh}. \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}


\subsection{Reliability}

Collecting \S \ref{sec:barriers} and Proposition~\ref{prop:wbound}, define the \emph{pointwise estimator}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Eh}
\begin{aligned}
  E_h := \ & C_0 |\log h_{\min}|^2 \|h^2 R_\infty\|_{0,\infty;\Om}
    && \text{localized residual} \\
  &+ C_1 |\log h_{\min}|^2 \|h^2\nabla\sigma_h\|_{0,d;\Lamh}
    && \text{localized quadrature} \\
  &+ \|(\psilo-u_h)^+\|_{0,\infty;\Om} + \|(u_h-\psiup)^+\|_{0,\infty;\Om}
    && \text{obstacle admissibility} \\
  &+ \|(\psiup-u_h)^+\|_{0,\infty;\omtop} + \|(u_h-\psilo)^+\|_{0,\infty;\ombot}
    && \text{localized detachment} \\
  &+ \|g-\Ih g\|_{0,\infty;\dOm}
    && \text{boundary datum.}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This is the box-constrained form of the estimator of \cite[\S 5.2]{NSV03}, which has one term of each of the last three kinds instead of two, two, and one.

\begin{theorem}[Reliability]
\label{thm:reliability}
Let $u$ solve \eqref{eq:VI} and let $u_h$ solve \eqref{eq:discVI}.  If $C_0$ and $C_1$ are twice the constants hidden in \eqref{eq:wbound} then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:reliability}
  \|u-u_h\|_{0,\infty;\Om} \ \le\ E_h.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Insert \eqref{eq:wbound} into the term $2\|w\|_{0,\infty;\Om}$ of \eqref{eq:barrierbound}, the factor two being absorbed by the choice of $C_0$ and $C_1$.  The five remaining terms of \eqref{eq:barrierbound} are precisely the last five terms of \eqref{eq:Eh}.
\end{proof}

Every term of $E_h$ is computable from $u_h$, the mesh, and the data.  The two star-dilated sets $\ombot,\omtop$ of \eqref{eq:omegas} are computable as well, being determined by the nodal signs of $\sigma_h$.

The estimator also controls the error in the residual, as in \cite[Theorem 5.4]{NSV03}.

\begin{proposition}[Residual estimate]
\label{prop:residualest}
$\|\sigma-\tilde\sigma_h\|_{-2,\infty;\Om} \preceq E_h$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\varphi \in W \cap W^{2,1}(\Om)$ with $|D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Om)\le 1$.  Integrating by parts in \eqref{eq:Ghagain}, and using $u - u_h = g - \Ih g$ on $\dOm$,
\[
  \left<\sigma-\tilde\sigma_h,\varphi\right> = -\left<u-u_h,\Delta\varphi\right> + \int_{\dOm}(g-\Ih g)\,\partial_\nu\varphi - \left<\Gh,\varphi\right>.
\]
Here $\|\Delta\varphi\|_{0,1;\Om} \le |D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Om) \le 1$, a trace estimate gives $\|\nabla\varphi\|_{0,1;\dOm} \preceq |D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Om)$, and $\left<\Gh,\varphi\right>$ is bounded by \eqref{eq:Ghbound}.  Taking the supremum over such $\varphi$,
\[
  \|\sigma-\tilde\sigma_h\|_{-2,\infty;\Om} \preceq \|u-u_h\|_{0,\infty;\Om} + \|g-\Ih g\|_{0,\infty;\dOm} + \|h^2R_\infty\|_{0,\infty;\Om} + \|h^2\nabla\sigma_h\|_{0,d;\Lamh}.
\]
Each term on the right is bounded by a multiple of $E_h$: the first by Theorem~\ref{thm:reliability}, the second directly, and the last two by the first two terms of \eqref{eq:Eh}, since $|\log h_{\min}|^2 \ge 1$ once $h_{\min}\le e^{-1}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[On the constants]
\label{rem:constants}
\cite[Theorem 5.4]{NSV03} states the sharper conclusion $\max\{\|u-u_h\|_{0,\infty;\Om},\|\sigma-\tilde\sigma_h\|_{-2,\infty;\Om}\} \le E_h$, with no hidden constant on either component.  Getting that from the proof above would require enlarging $C_0$ and $C_1$ once more, so that they also absorb \eqref{eq:Ghbound}, and absorbing the boundary trace term into $\|g-\Ih g\|_{0,\infty;\dOm}$.  We have kept the two statements separate rather than tune constants, since it is \eqref{eq:reliability} that the library uses.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Localization of the interior residual]
\label{rem:interiorloc}
By \eqref{eq:Rp}, the interior residual entering $E_h$ reads $|f+\sigma_h|$ on the active region $\Lamh$, and not $|f|$.  As in \cite[Remark 5.7]{NSV03}, it is the presence of $\sigma_h$ there which permits coarse meshes inside the contact region while $u$ is smooth, and in the box-constrained case this now applies to $\Lamhbot$ and $\Lamhtop$ alike.  The effect is a cancellation within one term, however, and not a vanishing of $E_h$; see Remark~\ref{rem:localization}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Logarithmic factor]
\label{rem:logfactor}
The factor $|\log h_{\min}|^2$ in \eqref{eq:Eh} comes from \eqref{eq:greenbound}, hence from linear theory, and it has nothing to do with the obstacles.  As \cite[Remark 5.10]{NSV03} observes, its power can be improved from $2$ to $1$ for smooth or convex domains.
\end{remark}


\section{Lower bounds} \label{sec:efficiency}

Theorem~\ref{thm:reliability} bounds the error by the estimator.  This section addresses the converse question, namely whether $E_h$ can overestimate the error, following \cite[\S 6]{NSV03}.  We call a quantity \emph{globally efficient} if it is bounded by the global error plus a global measure of data approximation, and \emph{locally efficient} if each local indicator is bounded by the error and the data approximation over a small patch surrounding the corresponding element.  Global efficiency is what justifies stopping an adaptive loop once the estimator is small, while local efficiency is what protects against over-refinement.

Three kinds of data approximation appear below, and the last two now come in two copies each, one per obstacle.  The first is the usual oscillation $\|h^2(f-\bar f)\|_{0,\infty;\Om}$ of the load, where $\bar f$ is the piecewise-constant function with $\bar f|_T := |T|^{-1}\int_T f$ for $T \in \Th$.  The second is the pair of \emph{obstacle approximations}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:obstacleapprox}
  \|(\psiloh - \psilo)^+\|_{0,\infty;\omega} \qquad\text{and}\qquad \|(\psiup - \psiuph)^+\|_{0,\infty;\omega}.
\end{equation}
The two positive parts point in opposite directions.  Each retains the part of the obstacle interpolation error which is invisible to the corresponding detachment term of \eqref{eq:Eh}, that term being measured against the continuum obstacle while the discrete solution only knows the discrete one.  Both quantities are bounded by $\|\psilo-\psiloh\|_{0,\infty;\omega}$ and $\|\psiup-\psiuph\|_{0,\infty;\omega}$ respectively, hence they are $O(h^2)$ for obstacles in $W^{2,\infty}$; see Remark~\ref{rem:smoothobstacles}.  The third is the pair of \emph{discrete obstacle oscillations}: for $\omega \subset \bar\Om$ and $v_h \in \Vh$ set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:jumposc}
  \bigl\|h\jump{\partial_\nu v_h}\bigr\|_{0,\infty;\omega}
  := \max\bigl\{ h_S \bigl\|\jump{\partial_\nu v_h}\bigr\|_{0,\infty;S} \,:\, S \in \Sh \text{ with } S \cap \operatorname{int}\omega \ne \emptyset \bigr\},
\end{equation}
and use this with $v_h = \psiloh$ and with $v_h = \psiuph$.

Efficiency statements are local, so we need a local form of the dual seminorm \eqref{eq:dualnormbv}.  For $\omega \subset \bar\Om$ and $\rho \in W^*$ put
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:localdual}
  \|\rho\|_{-2,\infty;\omega} := \sup\bigl\{\left<\rho,\varphi\right> \,:\, \varphi \in W,\ |D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\omega) \le 1,\ \operatorname{supp}\varphi \subset \omega\bigr\},
\end{equation}
which reduces to \eqref{eq:dualnormbv} when $\omega = \bar\Om$.  Every test function used in this section has support in the interior of $\Om$, so no boundary terms will arise from integration by parts.  Finally, two patches around an element $T\in\Th$ appear, its star $\Uh(T)$ from \eqref{eq:star} and the smaller
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Tstar}
  T^* := \bigcup\{T' \in \Th \,:\, T' \text{ shares at least one side with } T\} \ \subset\ \Uh(T),
\end{equation}
and we write $\omega_S$ for the union of the two elements meeting along an interior side $S \in \Sh$.


\subsection{A local bound on the Galerkin functional}

Both efficiency arguments below test $\Gh$ against a locally-supported function, so we first record the local counterpart of \cite[formula (3.11)]{NSV03}.  It is a direct consequence of the second form \eqref{eq:Ghagain} of the Galerkin functional, and, like \eqref{eq:Ghagain} itself, it does not distinguish the obstacles.

\begin{lemma}[Local bound on $\Gh$]
\label{lem:Ghlocal}
Let $\omega \subset \Om$ be open and let $\varphi \in W$ have support a compact subset of $\omega$.  Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Ghlocal}
  \bigl|\left<\Gh,\varphi\right>\bigr| \ \le\ \Bigl(\|u-u_h\|_{0,\infty;\omega} + \|\sigma-\tilde\sigma_h\|_{-2,\infty;\omega}\Bigr)\,|D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\omega).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:Ghagain}, $\left<\Gh,\varphi\right> = \left<\nabla(u-u_h),\nabla\varphi\right> - \left<\sigma-\tilde\sigma_h,\varphi\right>$, and the second pairing is at most $\|\sigma-\tilde\sigma_h\|_{-2,\infty;\omega}|D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\omega)$ by definition \eqref{eq:localdual}.  For the first, mollify: since $\operatorname{supp}\varphi$ is a compact subset of $\omega$, the mollifications $\varphi_\eps$ lie in $C_0^\infty(\omega)$ for small $\eps$, satisfy $|D^2\varphi_\eps|_{\mathrm{var}}(\omega) \le |D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\omega)$, and converge to $\varphi$ in $H_0^1(\Om)$, while $\Delta\varphi_\eps \rightharpoonup \Delta\varphi$ weakly-$*$ as measures.  Since $u - u_h$ is continuous,
\[
  \left<\nabla(u-u_h),\nabla\varphi\right> = \lim_{\eps\to 0} \left<\nabla(u-u_h),\nabla\varphi_\eps\right>
  = -\lim_{\eps\to 0} \int_\omega (u-u_h)\,\Delta\varphi_\eps,
\]
with no boundary term at any stage, and $|\Delta\varphi_\eps|(\omega) \le |D^2\varphi_\eps|_{\mathrm{var}}(\omega)$ finishes the proof.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Efficiency of the localized residual}

The lower bound for the localized residual uses bubble functions, in the manner of Verf\"urth \cite{Verfurth1996} and Nochetto \cite{Nochetto1995}.  The construction has to be compatible with the averaging operator $\Pih$ of \S \ref{sec:discretization}, in the sense that the bubbles must not disturb $\tilde\sigma_h$ on the transition elements $\Thplus$, and \cite[Lemma 6.1]{NSV03} arranges this by shrinking the balls $B(z)$ in \eqref{eq:Pih} so that they avoid the barycenters of all elements and sides.  Shrinking the balls preserves $B(z) \subset \Uh(z)$, so it changes neither \eqref{eq:Pihprops} and \eqref{eq:PihBV} nor any statement of \S\S \ref{sec:estimator}--\ref{sec:barriers}; only the constants move.  The obstacles play no part in the construction, so we state the result and refer to \cite[Lemma 6.1]{NSV03} for its proof.

\begin{lemma}[Retracted bubble functions]
\label{lem:bubbles}
There is $\delta \in (0,1)$, depending only on the shape-regularity constant $\gamma^*$, such that if the balls in \eqref{eq:Pih} are replaced by $B(z;\delta\rho_z)$, where $\rho_z := \max\{r > 0 : B(z;r) \subset \Uh(z)\}$, then for every $T \in \Th$ and every $S \in \Sh$ there are bubble functions $b_T, b_S \in W^{2,1}(\Om) \cap H_0^1(\Om) \subset W$ with $0 \le b_T, b_S \le 1$, with $\operatorname{supp} b_T$ a compact subset of $\operatorname{int} T$ and $\operatorname{supp} b_S$ a compact subset of $\operatorname{int}\omega_S$, and with the following properties.  For all $\varphi \in L^1(\Om)$ and all $\varphi_T \in \mathbb{P}_1(T)$,
\begin{align}
\label{eq:bubbleT}
  \Pih(\varphi\, b_T) &= 0, \\
  \|\varphi_T\|_{0,\infty;T} &\preceq \sup\Bigl\{ \textstyle\int_T \varphi_T\, \zeta_T\, b_T \,:\, \zeta_T \in \mathbb{P}_1(T),\ \|\zeta_T\|_{0,1;T} \le 1 \Bigr\}, \notag \\
  |D^2(\varphi_T b_T)|_{\mathrm{var}}(T) &\preceq h_T^{-2}\,\|\varphi_T\|_{0,1;T}, \notag
\end{align}
and
\begin{align}
\label{eq:bubbleS}
  \Pih(\varphi\, b_S) &= 0, \\
  1 &\preceq |S|^{-1}\textstyle\int_S b_S, \notag \\
  |D^2 b_S|_{\mathrm{var}}(\omega_S) \preceq h_S^{-1}|S|, &\qquad \|b_S\|_{0,1;\omega_S} \preceq h_S |S|. \notag
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

The point of \eqref{eq:bubbleT} and \eqref{eq:bubbleS} is that the transition elements drop out of $\tilde\sigma_h$ when it is tested against a bubble.  Indeed, in definition \eqref{eq:sigmatilde} the $\Thplus$ sum contains $\Pih$ applied to the test function, so it vanishes, while the $\ThC$ sum is an integral over $\Lamh$.  Thus, for every $\varphi \in L^\infty(\Om)$, every $T\in\Th$, and every $S\in\Sh$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:bubblepairing}
  \left<\tilde\sigma_h, \varphi\, b_T\right> = \int_{\Lamh} \sigma_h\, \varphi\, b_T
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \left<\tilde\sigma_h, \varphi\, b_S\right> = \int_{\Lamh} \sigma_h\, \varphi\, b_S,
\end{equation}
which is \cite[formula (6.4)]{NSV03}: the discrete non-contact region contributes nothing.  In particular the first identity reads $\int_T \sigma_h \varphi b_T$ if $T \in \ThC$, because $\operatorname{supp} b_T \subset \operatorname{int} T$, and it reads $0$ if $T \in \Thplus$, because then $\operatorname{int} T$ does not meet $\Lamh$.  Note that \eqref{eq:bubblepairing} is blind to which obstacle is active on a given element of $\ThC$; the whole of this subsection is.

\begin{proposition}[Efficiency I: localized residual]
\label{prop:effresidual}
The localized residual $\|h^2R_\infty\|_{0,\infty;\Om}$, the boundary datum term $\|g-\Ih g\|_{0,\infty;\dOm}$, and both obstacle admissibility terms of \eqref{eq:Eh} are locally and globally efficient.  In particular, for every $T \in \Th$,
\begin{align}
\label{eq:effresidual}
  \|h^2 R_\infty\|_{0,\infty;T} &+ \|g - \Ih g\|_{0,\infty;\dOm \cap T} + \|(\psilo-u_h)^+\|_{0,\infty;T} + \|(u_h-\psiup)^+\|_{0,\infty;T} \\
  &\preceq\ \|u-u_h\|_{0,\infty;T^*} + \|\sigma-\tilde\sigma_h\|_{-2,\infty;T^*} + \|h^2(f-\bar f)\|_{0,\infty;T^*}. \notag
\end{align}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
1.  The three terms other than the residual are immediate.  On $\dOm$ we have $u = g$ and $u_h = \Ih g$, so $g - \Ih g = u - u_h$ there.  Because $u \in \mathcal{K}$ satisfies $\psilo \le u \le \psiup$ in $\Om$, we also have the two pointwise bounds $(\psilo - u_h)^+ \le (u-u_h)^+$ and $(u_h - \psiup)^+ \le (u_h-u)^+$, and each is at most $\|u-u_h\|_{0,\infty;T}$ on $T$.  This is the only place where the box constraint enters the proof, and the two obstacles enter symmetrically.

2.  Next we bound the interior residual.  Let $T \in \Th$ and set $\varrho_T := f + \sigma_h$ if $T \in \ThC$ and $\varrho_T := f$ if $T \in \Thplus$, as in \eqref{eq:Rp}, and let $\bar\varrho_T$ be the same expression with $\bar f$ in place of $f$.  In both cases $\bar\varrho_T \in \mathbb{P}_1(T)$, since $\sigma_h$ is affine on $T$ and $\bar f$ is constant there; no sign of $\sigma_h$ is used.  Choose $\zeta_T$ by the second line of \eqref{eq:bubbleT}, so that
\[
  \|\bar\varrho_T\|_{0,\infty;T} \preceq \int_T \bar\varrho_T\,\zeta_T b_T, \qquad \|\zeta_T\|_{0,1;T} \le 1.
\]
By \eqref{eq:bubblepairing}, in either case $\int_T (\bar\varrho_T - \bar f) \zeta_T b_T = \left<\tilde\sigma_h,\zeta_T b_T\right>$, so that
\[
  \int_T \bar\varrho_T \,\zeta_T b_T = \left<f + \tilde\sigma_h, \zeta_T b_T\right> + \int_T (\bar f - f)\,\zeta_T b_T = \left<\Gh,\zeta_T b_T\right> + \int_T (\bar f - f)\,\zeta_T b_T,
\]
the second equality by definition \eqref{eq:Gh} and $\left<\nabla u_h,\nabla(\zeta_T b_T)\right> = 0$, which holds because $\nabla u_h$ is constant on $T$ while $\zeta_T b_T$ vanishes near $\partial T$.  Now apply Lemma~\ref{lem:Ghlocal} with $\omega = \operatorname{int} T$ and the third line of \eqref{eq:bubbleT} to the first term, and $0 \le b_T \le 1$ with $\|\zeta_T\|_{0,1;T}\le 1$ to the second.  Adding and subtracting $f$ on the left then gives the local estimate
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:effvolume}
  h_T^2\|\varrho_T\|_{0,\infty;T} \ \preceq\ \|u-u_h\|_{0,\infty;T} + \|\sigma-\tilde\sigma_h\|_{-2,\infty;T} + h_T^2\|f-\bar f\|_{0,\infty;T},
\end{equation}
in which the patch is $T$ itself.

3.  Now the jump residual.  Let $S \in \Sh$ and recall that $J_S := \jump{\partial_\nu u_h}$ is constant on $S$.  Taking $\alpha_S := \operatorname{sign}(J_S)/|S|$, the second line of \eqref{eq:bubbleS} gives $\|J_S\|_{0,\infty;S} \preceq \int_S J_S \alpha_S b_S$ while $\|\alpha_S\|_{0,1;S} = 1$.  Since $\alpha_S b_S$ is supported in $\operatorname{int}\omega_S$, identity \eqref{eq:byparts} leaves only the side $S$, so definition \eqref{eq:Gh} and \eqref{eq:bubblepairing} give
\[
  \int_S J_S\,\alpha_S b_S = -\left<\nabla u_h, \nabla(\alpha_S b_S)\right>
  = \left<\Gh, \alpha_S b_S\right> - \int_{\omega_S} \bigl(f + \sigma_h \mathbb{1}_{\Lamh}\bigr)\,\alpha_S b_S,
\]
where $\mathbb{1}_{\Lamh}$ is the characteristic function of the discrete contact region.  Lemma~\ref{lem:Ghlocal} and the last line of \eqref{eq:bubbleS}, together with $|\alpha_S||S| = 1$, bound the two terms, and we obtain
\[
  h_S\|J_S\|_{0,\infty;S} \ \preceq\ \|u-u_h\|_{0,\infty;\omega_S} + \|\sigma-\tilde\sigma_h\|_{-2,\infty;\omega_S} + h_S^2\bigl\|f + \sigma_h \mathbb{1}_{\Lamh}\bigr\|_{0,\infty;\omega_S}.
\]
On each of the two elements of $\omega_S$ the last integrand is exactly the $\varrho_T$ of step 2, so \eqref{eq:effvolume} applies there and yields
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:effjump}
  h_S\|J_S\|_{0,\infty;S} \ \preceq\ \|u-u_h\|_{0,\infty;\omega_S} + \|\sigma-\tilde\sigma_h\|_{-2,\infty;\omega_S} + \|h^2(f-\bar f)\|_{0,\infty;\omega_S}.
\end{equation}
Note that the patch is still only $\omega_S$; this is because step 2 is local to a single element.

4.  Finally, by \eqref{eq:Rp} with $p = \infty$, $\|h^2R_\infty\|_{0,\infty;T} \le h_T\|\jump{\partial_\nu u_h}\|_{0,\infty;\partial T\setminus\dOm} + h_T^2\|\varrho_T\|_{0,\infty;T}$.  Bound the first part by \eqref{eq:effjump} over the sides $S \subset \partial T$, using $h_S \le h_T$ and $\omega_S \subset T^*$, and the second by \eqref{eq:effvolume}.  Combining with step 1 gives \eqref{eq:effresidual}.  Global efficiency follows by taking the maximum over $T \in \Th$, because a test function admissible in $\|\cdot\|_{-2,\infty;T^*}$ is admissible in $\|\cdot\|_{-2,\infty;\bar\Om}$, so the local seminorms \eqref{eq:localdual} are dominated by the global one.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Efficiency of the localized detachment terms}

The two remaining terms of $E_h$ are the localized detachment terms $\|(u_h-\psilo)^+\|_{0,\infty;\ombot}$ and $\|(\psiup-u_h)^+\|_{0,\infty;\omtop}$.  As \cite[\S 6.2]{NSV03} explains for the unilateral case, and as Remark~\ref{rem:optimalchoice} recalls here, these computable terms replace the non-computable optimal choices $\|(u_h-u)^+\|_{0,\infty;\ombot}$ and $\|(u-u_h)^+\|_{0,\infty;\omtop}$, which are trivially efficient.  What has to be shown is that the substitution costs only obstacle approximation.

The mechanism is a discrete quadratic-growth statement, the discrete counterpart of \cite[formula (2.9)]{Baiocchi1977}: a nonnegative piecewise-linear function which vanishes at an interior node is controlled on the whole star of that node by the jumps of its normal derivative.  We state it as in \cite[Lemma 6.4]{NSV03}, whose proof concerns $\Vh$ alone and involves no obstacle.

\begin{lemma}[Discrete growth around a minimum node]
\label{lem:growth}
Let $v_h \in \Vh$ satisfy $v_h \ge 0$ in $\Om$ and $v_h(z) = 0$ for some interior node $z \in \Nhr$.  Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:growth}
  \|v_h\|_{0,\infty;\Uh(z)} \ \preceq\ \bigl\|h\jump{\partial_\nu v_h}\bigr\|_{0,\infty;\Uh(z)},
\end{equation}
where the hidden constant is bounded by the number of elements in $\Uh(z)$.
\end{lemma}

Applied with $v_h = u_h - \psiloh \ge 0$ or $v_h = \psiuph - u_h \ge 0$, both nonnegative because $u_h \in \Kh$, this gives the bilateral analogue of \cite[Proposition 6.3]{NSV03}.  The hypothesis takes a slightly cleaner form here than in \cite{NSV03}, because $\ombot$ and $\omtop$ are unions of elements by construction: an element $T$ satisfies $T \subset \ombot$ if and only if $T$ has a vertex in $\Nh^+$, and likewise for $\omtop$ and $\Nh^-$.

\begin{proposition}[Efficiency II: localized detachment]
\label{prop:effdetach}
Let $T \in \Th$ with $T \subset \ombot$.
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] If $T \subset \{u_h = \psiloh\}$ then $\|(u_h-\psilo)^+\|_{0,\infty;T} = \|(\psiloh-\psilo)^+\|_{0,\infty;T}$.
\item[(ii)] Otherwise $T \cap \{u_h > \psiloh\} \ne \emptyset$, and then
\begin{align}
\label{eq:effdetach}
  \|(u_h-\psilo)^+\|_{0,\infty;T} \ \preceq\ & \|u-u_h\|_{0,\infty;\Uh(T)} + \|\sigma-\tilde\sigma_h\|_{-2,\infty;\Uh(T)} + \|h^2(f-\bar f)\|_{0,\infty;\Uh(T)} \\
  & + \|(\psiloh-\psilo)^+\|_{0,\infty;T} + \bigl\|h\jump{\partial_\nu \psiloh}\bigr\|_{0,\infty;\Uh(T)}. \notag
\end{align}
\end{itemize}
The symmetric statements hold for $T \subset \omtop$, with $(\psiup - u_h)^+$ in place of $(u_h-\psilo)^+$, the set $\{u_h = \psiuph\}$ in place of $\{u_h = \psiloh\}$, and $(\psiup - \psiuph)^+$ and $\jump{\partial_\nu \psiuph}$ in place of $(\psiloh-\psilo)^+$ and $\jump{\partial_\nu \psiloh}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Statement (i) is an identity, since $u_h = \psiloh$ on $T$.  For (ii), note first that $u_h \ge \psiloh$ always, so the failure of (i) is exactly $T \cap \{u_h > \psiloh\} \ne \emptyset$.  Since $T \subset \ombot$ there is a vertex $z$ of $T$ with $\sigma_h(z) > 0$, and then $z \in \Nhbot$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:nodalsign} and \eqref{eq:sigmah-bnd}, so $u_h(z) = \psiloh(z)$.  Moreover $z$ is an interior node: if $z$ were a boundary node then $\sigma_h(z) > 0$ would require the first case of \eqref{eq:sigmah-bnd}, hence $T \subset \Uh(z) \subset \{u_h = \psiloh\}$, which is excluded.

Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:growth} to $v_h := u_h - \psiloh$, which is nonnegative and vanishes at $z$.  Since $T \subset \Uh(z) \subset \Uh(T)$, and since $\jump{\partial_\nu v_h} = \jump{\partial_\nu u_h} - \jump{\partial_\nu \psiloh}$,
\[
  \|u_h - \psiloh\|_{0,\infty;T} \ \preceq\ \bigl\|h\jump{\partial_\nu u_h}\bigr\|_{0,\infty;\Uh(T)} + \bigl\|h\jump{\partial_\nu \psiloh}\bigr\|_{0,\infty;\Uh(T)}.
\]
If $S \in \Sh$ meets $\operatorname{int}\Uh(T)$ then both elements adjacent to $S$ lie in $\Uh(T)$, because $\Uh(T)$ is a union of closed elements and a relative-interior point of $S$ in $\operatorname{int}\Uh(T)$ has a full neighbourhood there.  Hence \eqref{eq:effjump} bounds the first term by the first line of \eqref{eq:effdetach}, with patches contained in $\Uh(T)$.  Finally the pointwise inequality $(u_h - \psilo)^+ \le |u_h - \psiloh| + (\psiloh-\psilo)^+$, obtained by writing $u_h - \psilo = (u_h-\psiloh) + (\psiloh-\psilo)$, completes the estimate.

For $T \subset \omtop$ the argument is the same with signs exchanged.  A vertex $z$ of $T$ has $\sigma_h(z) < 0$, hence $z \in \Nhtop$ and $u_h(z) = \psiuph(z)$, and $z$ is interior because otherwise the second case of \eqref{eq:sigmah-bnd} would force $T \subset \{u_h = \psiuph\}$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:growth} applies to $v_h := \psiuph - u_h \ge 0$, and the concluding pointwise inequality is $(\psiup - u_h)^+ \le |\psiuph - u_h| + (\psiup - \psiuph)^+$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Smooth obstacles]
\label{rem:smoothobstacles}
If $\psilo \in W^{2,\infty}(\Uh(T))$ then, exactly as in \cite[Remark 6.5]{NSV03},
\[
  \|(\psiloh-\psilo)^+\|_{0,\infty;T} + \bigl\|h\jump{\partial_\nu \psiloh}\bigr\|_{0,\infty;\Uh(T)} \ \preceq\ h_T^2\,\|D^2\psilo\|_{0,\infty;\Uh(T)},
\]
by the standard interpolation estimates $\|\psiloh - \psilo\|_{0,\infty;T} \preceq h_T^2\|D^2\psilo\|_{0,\infty;T}$ and $\|h\nabla(\psiloh-\psilo)\|_{0,\infty;\Uh(T)} \preceq h_T^2\|D^2\psilo\|_{0,\infty;\Uh(T)}$, the latter used through $\jump{\partial_\nu\psiloh} = \jump{\partial_\nu(\psiloh-\psilo)}$.  The same holds for $\psiup$.  Thus both localized detachment terms are locally and globally efficient for obstacles in $W^{2,\infty}$, to the same second order as the rest of $E_h$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Elements which meet both dilated sets]
\label{rem:bothsides}
Nothing prevents $T \subset \ombot \cap \omtop$, which happens exactly when $T$ has one vertex in $\Nh^+$ and another in $\Nh^-$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:effdetach} then applies twice, each time through a different vertex, and the two conclusions do not interact.  Such a $T$ necessarily belongs to $\Thplus$, since its two distinguished vertices carry $u_h = \psiloh$ and $u_h = \psiuph$ respectively, values which differ by \eqref{eq:disc-nondeg}.  As in \S \ref{sec:barriers}, no relation between $h$ and the separation distance $r_0$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:separation} is needed.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The star dilation is free here]
\label{rem:noextraring}
Remark~\ref{rem:effring} anticipated that measuring the detachment terms over the dilated sets \eqref{eq:omegas}, rather than over $\{\sigma_h>0\}$ and $\{\sigma_h<0\}$ as \cite{NSV03} does, would cost an extra ring of elements in the efficiency statement.  It does not.  Regarding $\sigma_h$ as piecewise linear, $\ombot = \Uh(\{\sigma_h>0\})$, because an element on which $\sigma_h$ is somewhere positive has a vertex where $\sigma_h$ is positive, and conversely.  So the elements $T \subset \ombot$ treated by Proposition~\ref{prop:effdetach} are exactly the elements $T$ with $T \cap \{\sigma_h > 0\} \ne \emptyset$ treated by \cite[Proposition 6.3]{NSV03}, and the patch on the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:effdetach} is the same $\Uh(T)$ as theirs.  The reason is that their local bound is already stated over all of $T$, and not over $T \cap \{\sigma_h>0\}$; the dilation adds nothing beyond what the element-wise maximum was taking anyway.
\end{remark}


\subsection{Efficiency of the quadrature estimator}

The remaining term of $E_h$ is the quadrature estimator $\|h^2\nabla\sigma_h\|_{0,d;\Lamh}$, which arises from the mass lumping in definition \eqref{eq:sigmah} of $\sigma_h$.  That lumping is what makes the nodal complementarity of Proposition~\ref{prop:nodalsign} available in the first place, so this term is the price of the whole approach.

\begin{proposition}[Efficiency III: quadrature estimator]
\label{prop:effquad}
The quadrature estimator is locally efficient: for every $T \in \ThC$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:effquad}
  \|h^2\nabla\sigma_h\|_{0,d;T} \ \preceq\ \|u-u_h\|_{0,\infty;T} + \|\sigma-\tilde\sigma_h\|_{-2,\infty;T} + \|h^2(f-\bar f)\|_{0,\infty;T}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $\bar f$ is constant on $T$ we may write $\nabla\sigma_h = \nabla(\bar f + \sigma_h)$, and $\bar f + \sigma_h$ is affine on $T$, so an inverse estimate and $|T|^{1/d} \approx h_T$ give
\begin{align*}
  h_T^2\|\nabla\sigma_h\|_{0,d;T} &= h_T^2\,|T|^{1/d}\,\bigl|\nabla(\bar f+\sigma_h)\bigr|
  \ \preceq\ h_T\,|T|^{1/d}\,\|\bar f + \sigma_h\|_{0,\infty;T} \\
  &\preceq\ h_T^2\,\|f + \sigma_h\|_{0,\infty;T} + h_T^2\|f - \bar f\|_{0,\infty;T}.
\end{align*}
Since $T \in \ThC$, $f + \sigma_h$ is the $\varrho_T$ of step 2 in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:effresidual}, so \eqref{eq:effvolume} applies.
\end{proof}

As \cite[\S 6.3]{NSV03} observes, the local bound \eqref{eq:effquad} does not obviously imply global efficiency, because passing to global quantities requires summing the left-hand side while the right-hand side is a maximum.  This is a feature of the $L^d$ accumulation in the quadrature term, and it is unrelated to the box constraint: \eqref{eq:effquad} holds on $\Thbot$ and $\Thtop$ elements alike, and its proof, like that of \eqref{eq:effvolume}, never uses a sign of $\sigma_h$.  In practice the marking strategy has to account for the different accumulation, as discussed in \cite[\S 7.1]{NSV03}.


\subsection{Summary}

Collecting Propositions~\ref{prop:effresidual}, \ref{prop:effdetach}, and \ref{prop:effquad}, every term of the estimator $E_h$ in \eqref{eq:Eh} is locally efficient, up to the load oscillation $\|h^2(f-\bar f)\|_{0,\infty;\cdot}$, the obstacle approximations \eqref{eq:obstacleapprox}, and the discrete obstacle oscillations \eqref{eq:jumposc}.  All of these are second order for data in $W^{2,\infty}$, by Remark~\ref{rem:smoothobstacles}.  Global efficiency follows for every term except the quadrature estimator, exactly as in the unilateral case.  The logarithmic factor $|\log h_{\min}|^2$ multiplying the first two terms of \eqref{eq:Eh} is of course not recovered by these lower bounds; as Remark~\ref{rem:logfactor} notes, it comes from linear theory and not from the obstacles.

It is worth recording where the bilateral case actually cost something.  In \S \ref{sec:barriers} it cost the star dilation \eqref{eq:omegas} and a second localized term in the estimator.  Here it cost almost nothing: the residual and quadrature lower bounds go through verbatim because their proofs use only that $\sigma_h$ is affine on each element of $\ThC$, never its sign, and the detachment lower bound is a pair of mirror-image applications of Lemma~\ref{lem:growth}, one per obstacle, which by Remark~\ref{rem:bothsides} do not interfere even on an element which touches both contact sets.  The asymmetry between the two halves of the theory is thus entirely in the barriers: reliability needed the sign of $\sigma_h$, and efficiency does not.



\section{A fully localized estimator} \label{sec:localized}

This section is not yet written.  Its subject is \cite{NSV05}, the successor of \cite{NSV03}, whose estimator vanishes identically inside the discrete full-contact set, apart from obstacle resolution, rather than merely localizing to the dilated sets \eqref{eq:omegas}; Remark~\ref{rem:localization} explains the gap being closed.  The machinery is nodal and star-based rather than element-based: a nodal multiplier $s_z$, a set $C_h$ of full-contact nodes cut out by sign conditions on $f$ and on the facet jumps over the star of $z$, and a star-based residual indicator.
