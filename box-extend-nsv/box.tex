\section{Introduction and goals} \label{sec:intro}

The VIAMR library (\href{https://github.com/StefanoFochesatto/viamr}{github.com/StefanoFochesatto/viamr}) implements a variety of adaptive mesh refinement (AMR) and mesh adaptation algorithms for variational inequalities (VIs).  Some of the implemented methods incorporate heuristic strategies and extend AMR for VIs to highly-nonlinear and/or degenerate problems.  See the main paper for the general setting.

However, for classical obstacle problems, namely unilateral problems where the operator is the (weak) Laplacian and the corresponding PDE is the Poisson equation, the library also implements element marking using the fully-rigorous \emph{a posteriori} estimator from Nochetto, Siebert, \& Veeser \cite{NSV03}.  For that estimator, reliability (error $\preceq$ estimator) and efficiency (estimator $\preceq$ error) are proven in the $L^\infty$ pointwise norm.  Such precision is generally not available for nonlinear and/or degenerating operators, and in any case it is not proven for the partly-heuristic methods in the library.

This Appendix extends \cite{NSV03}'s proof of reliability and efficiency to the bilateral case, that is, with continuous lower \emph{and upper} bounds.  The sequence of topics here, and much of the notation, generally follows the original.  Note that the operator must still be the ordinary linear Laplacian; this is not changed.

We are not aware of any pointwise \emph{a posteriori} theory for the bilateral problem, which is why we write this out.  The nearest results are of two kinds.  First, the same authors sharpened \cite{NSV03} in \cite{NSV05}, where the estimator is \emph{fully localized}, in that it vanishes identically on the full-contact set and not merely on a subset of it, and where the pointwise bounds are converted into \emph{barrier sets}, that is, computable regions which are proven to contain the free boundary under a stability (nondegeneracy) condition.  Both refinements are for one obstacle; we return to their bilateral analogues in Remark~\ref{rem:localization}.  Second, Ot\'arola, Rankin, \& Salgado \cite{Otarolaetal2019} prove reliable and efficient maximum-norm \emph{a posteriori} bounds for a problem with genuine box constraints, using the machinery of \cite{NSV03}.  However, their constrained variable is the control in a linear-quadratic optimal control problem, and it is determined by a pointwise projection of the adjoint state.  There is consequently no free boundary of the present kind, no contact-set localization, and no barrier construction; the reduction in \S \ref{sec:barriers} has no analogue there.  Our constrained variable is the solution of the VI itself.


\section{Continuum problem and setup} \label{sec:setup}

\subsection{The variational inequality, and its residual}  Let $\Om$ be a bounded, polyhedral, not necessarily convex domain in $\mathbb{R}^d$
with $d \in \{2,3\}$.  Suppose
$\psilo, \psiup \in H^1(\Om) \cap C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Om)$, $0 < \alpha \le 1$, are
lower and upper obstacles, respectively, satisfying the non-degeneracy assumption
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:nondeg}
  \psilo < \psiup \qquad \text{on } \bar\Om.
\end{equation}
Finally, let $f \in L^\infty(\Om)$ be a source function and $g \in H^1(\Om) \cap C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Om)$ be Dirichlet boundary data satisfying the compatibility condition
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:compat}
  \psilo \le g \le \psiup \qquad \text{on } \dOm.
\end{equation}


\begin{remark}[Non-degeneracy]
Strict separation \eqref{eq:nondeg} rules out ``pinched'' points \cite{BuelerFarrell2024} where the two obstacles touch, and thus both constraints are simultaneously active.  Thus the analysis below is free of an extra degenerate case, but our VIAMR library implementation must still cope with near-pinching, since active sets are decided against a numerical tolerance.
\end{remark}

Let $\mathcal{K}$ be the non-empty, closed, convex, \emph{box-constrained} subset of $H^1(\Om)$ defined by the bilateral obstacles:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:K}
  \mathcal{K} := \{v \in H^1(\Om) \mid \psilo \le v \le \psiup \text{ a.e.\ in } \Om,
    \ v = g \text{ on } \dOm\}.
\end{equation}
The fixed boundary condition $v=g$ is in the trace sense.  The continuous VI problem reads:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:VI}
  u \in \mathcal{K}: \qquad
  \left< \nabla u, \nabla(v - u)\right> \ge \left< f, v - u\right>
  \qquad \text{for all } v \in \mathcal{K}.
\end{equation}
This is the immediate generalization of problem (1.1) of \cite{NSV03}, from a one-sided constraint to the bilateral case.  We state the VI in form \eqref{eq:VI}, matching common conventions elsewhere in the literature \cite{BuelerFarrell2024,Falk1974,GraeserKornhuber2009,KinderlehrerStampacchia1980}, and in the library itself, and it is obviously equivalent to the reversed form ``$\left< \nabla u, \nabla(u - v)\right> \le \left< f, u - v\right>$'' used in \cite[formula (1.1)]{NSV03}.

It is well-known that \eqref{eq:VI} admits a unique H\"older continuous solution $u \in \mathcal{K}$ \cite{KinderlehrerStampacchia1980}.
%% TODO(citation): NSV03 attributes Hölder continuity of u, in the
%% unilateral case, to Frehse & Mosco (1982); find and verify a
%% citation that actually covers the box-constrained/double-obstacle case
%% before this goes further, rather than asserting one.

Let $\sigma \in H^{-1}(\Om) = \mathring H^1(\Om)^*$ be the \emph{residual functional}
defined by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigma}
  \left< \sigma, \varphi\right> = \left< \nabla u, \nabla \varphi\right> - \left< f, \varphi\right>
  \qquad \text{for all } \varphi \in \mathring H^1(\Om).
\end{equation}

Again, definition \eqref{eq:sigma} switches the sign relative to \cite[formula (1.2)]{NSV03}.  However, our sign choice, consistent across the VIAMR library, matches the traditional choice for \emph{unilateral} complementarity, that is, with this sign choice we expect $u-\psi_\bot\ge 0, \sigma\ge 0, (u-\psi_\bot) \sigma = 0$ as the nonlinear complementarity form of the unilateral problem.  Our sign choice also matches more of the literature, e.g.~the standard textbook \cite{KinderlehrerStampacchia1980}.  In any case, the reader should remember our bilateral sign convention as given by \eqref{eq:sigmasign} below.

Consider what happens when the solution contacts the obstacle, creating sets where the inequality is active.  Unlike the unilateral case, $\sigma$ no longer has a fixed sign globally.  Write $\Cbot := \{x\,:\,u(x) = \psilo(x)\}$ and $\Ctop := \{x\,:\,u(x) = \psiup(x)\}$ for the closed
\emph{lower-} and \emph{upper-contact} (active) \emph{sets}, subsets of $\bar\Om$.  Because a general element of $H^{-1}(\Om)$ cannot be restricted to a closed set of measure zero, we first state the sign of $\sigma$ on open sets:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigmasignopen}
  \sigma \ge 0 \text{ on } \Om \setminus \Ctop \qquad \text{and} \qquad \sigma \le 0 \text{ on } \Om \setminus \Cbot,
\end{equation}
in the distributional sense, that is, $\left<\sigma,\varphi\right> \ge 0$ for every $\varphi \ge 0$ compactly supported in $\Om \setminus \Ctop$, and $\left<\sigma,\varphi\right> \le 0$ for every $\varphi \ge 0$ compactly supported in $\Om \setminus \Cbot$.

The proof of \eqref{eq:sigmasignopen} is by taking a smooth and locally-supported test function $\varphi\in \mathring H^1(\Om)$, with $\varphi\ge 0$ compactly supported in $\Om \setminus \Ctop$, or $\varphi\le 0$ compactly supported in $\Om \setminus \Cbot$, respectively, and substituting $v=u+\eps \varphi$ into \eqref{eq:VI}, for sufficiently small $\eps>0$, such that $v\in\mathcal{K}$.

Now, the non-degeneracy assumption \eqref{eq:nondeg} says $\Cbot$ and $\Ctop$ are disjoint, so the two open sets in \eqref{eq:sigmasignopen} cover $\Om$.  A distribution with a sign on an open set is a signed measure there, so $\sigma$ is a signed Radon measure \cite{EvansGariepy2015}.  Furthermore, on the open \emph{non-contact set} (inactive set) $\{\psilo < u < \psiup\}$, which lies in both of the sets in \eqref{eq:sigmasignopen}, the two bounds give $\sigma = 0$.  Thus $\sigma$ admits the Jordan decomposition $\sigma = \mu_{\bot} - \mu_{\top}$ into nonnegative Radon measures supported on $\Cbot$ and $\Ctop$ respectively.  In particular, $\sigma$ can now be restricted to the contact sets themselves, and \eqref{eq:sigmasignopen} becomes
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigmasign}
  \sigma \ge 0 \text{ on } \Cbot \qquad \text{and} \qquad \sigma \le 0 \text{ on } \Ctop.
\end{equation}


\subsection{Separation of the bilateral active (contact) sets}  While the non-degeneracy assumption $\psilo < \psiup$ implies that the two contact sets $\Cbot, \Ctop$ are disjoint, it does not by itself say how far apart they are.  We quantify the gap here because it is the natural scale at which one expects the discrete classification of \S \ref{sec:discretization} to begin tracking the true contact sets.  The resulting separation bound $r_0$ is \emph{not} needed for any result below, though the uniform gap $\delta_{\min}$ defined next certainly is.

For the continuum problem, compactness of $\bar\Om$ allows us to quantify the separation of the contact sets, as follows.  First, let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:deltamin}
  \delta_{\min} := \min_{\bar\Om} (\psiup - \psilo),
\end{equation}
equivalently the largest constant with $\psilo + \delta_{\min} \le \psiup$ on $\bar\Om$.  The minimum is attained, and positive, by nondegeneracy \eqref{eq:nondeg}, continuity of $\psiup - \psilo$, and compactness of $\bar\Om$.

Let $L^\top \ge 0$ be the H\"older seminorm of $\psiup$ on $\bar\Om$, i.e.\ the least constant with $|\psiup(x) - \psiup(y)| \le L^\top |x-y|^\alpha$ for all $x,y \in \bar\Om$.  This value is finite since $\psiup \in C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Om)$, but note it can be zero, related to the division convention below.  We assume, as does \cite{NSV03} for the unilateral problem, that the solution $u$ is H\"older continuous on $\bar\Om$ with some exponent $\beta \in (0,1]$ and seminorm $L_u \ge 0$.

\begin{proposition}[Separation of the contact sets]
\label{prop:separation}
If $\Cbot, \Ctop$ are each non-empty
then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:r0}
  \operatorname{dist}(\Cbot, \Ctop) \ \ge\ r_0
  := \min\left\{ \left(\frac{\delta_{\min}}{2 L^\top}\right)^{1/\alpha},
                 \left(\frac{\delta_{\min}}{2 L_u}\right)^{1/\beta} \right\}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

Regarding possible division by zero in \eqref{eq:r0}, we make the convention that a term is omitted from the minimum if its
denominator vanishes, i.e.\ if $\psiup$ or $u$ is constant.  That is, we regard the quotients as positive infinity in such cases.

\begin{proof}
Let $x \in \Cbot$ and $y \in \Ctop$ be arbitrary, and set $r := |x-y|$.  Since $u(x) = \psilo(x)$,
\begin{equation}
  \delta_{\min} \le \psiup(x) - \psilo(x) = \psiup(x) - u(x).
\end{equation}
Insert $\psiup(y) = u(y)$ and use the two H\"older bounds:
\begin{equation}
  \delta_{\min} \le \bigl(\psiup(x) - \psiup(y)\bigr) + \bigl(u(y) - u(x)\bigr)
  \le L^\top r^\alpha + L_u r^\beta.
\end{equation}
If $r < r_0$, then
$L^\top r^\alpha < \delta_{\min}/2$ and $L_u r^\beta < \delta_{\min}/2$, by definition of $r_0$,  so $L^\top r^\alpha + L_u r^\beta < \delta_{\min}$, a contradiction. Thus
$r \ge r_0$, and $\operatorname{dist}(\Cbot,\Ctop) \ge r_0$ follows.
\end{proof}

Separation hypotheses of this kind are expected in the bilateral setting.  For the displacement obstacle problem of clamped Kirchhoff plates, a fourth-order bilateral problem, Brenner et al.~\cite{Brenneretal2012} assume that the two obstacles are separated from each other and from the boundary values, and under that hypothesis obtain $L^\infty$ convergence rates and approximation of the coincidence set, though \emph{a priori} in both cases.  Here, by contrast, $\delta_{\min}$ in \eqref{eq:deltamin} and $r_0$ in \eqref{eq:r0} are computed from the data rather than assumed.

Proposition~\ref{prop:separation} gives an explicit, data-determined lower bound $r_0$ on the spatial separation of the two contact sets  $\Cbot, \Ctop$.  The lower bound $r_0$ is controlled by the data functions $f,g,\psilo,\psiup$ through the regularity theory \cite{KinderlehrerStampacchia1980}.  As it turns out, \S \ref{sec:discretization} does \emph{not} need to impose $h < c\,r_0$ as a hypothesis for the estimator to be well-defined and correct.  Instead, $r_0$'s role is to bound the width of the transition band so that the discretization's classification tracks the true contact sets closely.


\section{Discretization} \label{sec:discretization}

Let $\{\Th\}$ be a sequence of conforming partitions of $\bar\Om$ into \emph{closed} simplices, namely triangles or tetrahedra in dimensions $d=2,3$, respectively.  Let $\Nh$ be the node set of $\Th$ and $\Nhr \subset \Nh$ the interior nodes.  Finally, let $\Sh$ be the interior sides, i.e.~the interior edges or faces in $d=2,3$.

Given a (closed) simplex $T$, $h_T$ is its diameter and $\rho_T$ is the maximal radius of a ball contained in $T$.  We assume shape-regularity, namely a bound $h_T/\rho_T \le \gamma^*$ for all $T \in \Th$, with $\gamma^*$ independent of $h$.  We also write $h$ for the piecewise-constant \emph{mesh-size function} with $h|_T = h_T$, and $h_S$ for the diameter of a side $S$; expressions like $\|h^2 R\|_{0,\infty;\Om}$ below are to be read with this function.

For $\omega \subset \bar\Om$ any subset, let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:star}
\Uh(\omega) := \bigcup\{T \in \Th : T \cap \omega \ne \emptyset\}.
\end{equation}
This closed set is a (finite) union of the closed simplices $T$.  Note that ``$T \cap \omega \ne \emptyset$'' only requires $T$ to meet $\omega$ in a single point, so for example $\Uh(T)$ for a simplex $T$ includes every simplex with a shared vertex, edge, or face.  For a node $z \in \Nh$, its \emph{star} \cite{Langeetal2016} is $\Uh(z) := \Uh(\{z\}) = \operatorname{supp} \phi_h^z= \overline{\{x\,:\,\phi_h^z(x) > 0\}}$.

Let $\Vh$ be the space of continuous piecewise-linear finite elements over $\Th$, with
$\Vhr := \Vh \cap \mathring H^1(\Om)$ the subspace with zero boundary values.  We denote the nodal basis of $\Vh$ by $(\phi_h^z)_{z \in \Nh}$.  Let $\Ih$ be the Lagrange interpolation operator onto $\Vh$, specifically $\Ih:C(\bar\Om) \to \Vh$.  As in \cite{NSV03} we write $\left< \cdot,\cdot\right>_h$ for the lumped $L_2$ scalar product,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lumped}
\left< v_h, w_h\right>_h := \int_\Om \Ih(v_h w_h), \qquad v_h, w_h \in \Vh.
\end{equation}
Also as in \cite{NSV03}, in what follows $C$ denotes different constants depending only on $\gamma^*$, $\Om$, and $d$, and $\preceq$ indicates $\le$ up to such a constant.


\subsection{Monotonicity of nodal interpolation, and \texorpdfstring{$\Kh$}{Kh}}

Let us start with two properties special to affine functions and piecewise-\emph{linear} interpolation, which need no acuteness or mesh-fineness assumption on $\Th$.  They do not hold generally for higher-order elements.

\begin{lemma}[Vanishing affine functions on simplices]
\label{lem:affine}
If $w$ is affine on a simplex $T$, and if $w \ge 0$ at every vertex of $T$, then $w\ge 0$ everywhere on $T$.  Furthermore, $w = 0$ somewhere in $T$ if and only if $w = 0$ at some vertex of $T$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Most parts are obvious affine function properties.  For ($\Rightarrow$) direction of the equivalence claim, write a point $x \in T$ in barycentric coordinates, $x = \sum_i \lambda_i x_i$, $\lambda_i \ge 0$, $\sum_i \lambda_i = 1$, so $w(x) = \sum_i \lambda_i w(x_i)$.  If $w(x_i) > 0$ for every vertex $x_i$, then $w(x) > 0$ for every $x \in T$ (some $\lambda_i > 0$ always, and all terms are non-negative with at least one strictly positive).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Order-preservation of $I_h$]
\label{lem:monotone}
If $w, \zeta \in C(\bar\Om)$ satisfy $w \le \zeta$ on $\bar\Om$, then
$\Ih w \le \Ih \zeta$ on $\bar\Om$, for any mesh $\Th$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By linearity of $\Ih$, $\Ih \zeta - \Ih w = \Ih(\zeta - w)$, and $\zeta - w \ge 0$ at every node. On each $T \in \Th$, $\Ih(\zeta-w)$ is affine, hence $\Ih(\zeta-w) \ge 0$ on $T$, for every $T$, by the Lemma \ref{lem:affine}.
\end{proof}

Set $\psiloh := \Ih \psilo$ and $\psiuph := \Ih \psiup$.  Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:monotone} to $\psilo \le \psiup$ gives only $\psiloh \le \psiuph$.  Applying it instead to the uniform-gap form of \eqref{eq:deltamin}, and using that $\Ih$ reproduces constants, gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:disc-nondeg}
  \psiuph - \psiloh \ \ge\ \delta_{\min} \ >\ 0 \qquad \text{on } \bar\Om.
\end{equation}
Note this holds for \emph{every} mesh, not just fine ones. The compatibility condition \eqref{eq:compat} gives a second consequence, $\psiloh \le \Ih g \le \psiuph$ on $\dOm$, but here Lemma~\ref{lem:monotone} cannot be applied directly: $\psilo \le g \le \psiup$ is assumed only on $\dOm$, not on all of $\bar\Om$.  Instead we apply the Lemma face by face.  Each boundary face $F$ of a simplex is itself a simplex, whose vertices are nodes, and the trace of $\Ih w$ on $F$ depends only on the values of $w$ at those vertices.  Hence the Lemma, applied on $F$ to functions given on $F$ alone, yields the comparison there, and $\dOm$ is the union of such faces.

Thus we define the discrete constraint set just as in \eqref{eq:K}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Kh}
  \Kh := \{v_h \in \Vh \mid \psiloh \le v_h \le \psiuph \text{ in } \Om,
    \ v_h = \Ih g \text{ on } \dOm\},
\end{equation}
This set is closed and convex, and it is non-empty: choose $v_h \in \Vh$ nodally, equal to $\Ih g$ at boundary nodes and anywhere in the non-empty interval $[\psiloh(z),\psiuph(z)]$ at interior nodes, and Lemma~\ref{lem:affine} promotes these nodal inequalities to all of $\bar\Om$.  As noted by \cite{NSV03} for the unilateral case, $\Kh$ is in general not a subset of $\mathcal{K}$ defined by \eqref{eq:K}, so in this sense our approximation is non-conforming.

The discrete problem corresponding to \eqref{eq:VI} reads
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:discVI}
  u_h \in \Kh: \qquad
  \left< \nabla u_h, \nabla(v_h - u_h)\right> \ge \left< f, v_h - u_h\right>
  \qquad \text{for all } v_h \in \Kh,
\end{equation}
which again admits a unique solution.


\subsection{The discrete residual and its sign}

For interior nodes $z \in \Nhr$, define $\sigma_h \in \Vh$ nodally by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigmah}
  \left< \sigma_h, \phi_h^z \right>_h
  = \left< \nabla u_h, \nabla \phi_h^z \right> - \left< f, \phi_h^z \right>,
\end{equation}
whence $\sigma_h(z) = \bigl(\int_\Om \phi_h^z\bigr)^{-1}
\bigl(\left< \nabla u_h, \nabla \phi_h^z\right> - \left< f, \phi_h^z\right>\bigr)$.  This is \cite[formula (2.1)]{NSV03}, but here with the same sign convention as for $\sigma$ itself (see \eqref{eq:sigma}).  Note that while $\sigma \in H^{-1}(\Om)$ is a quite general object, generally a measure, $\sigma_h$ simply assigns a real value for each interior node in the mesh.  (On the other hand, the reader might be wise to regard $\sigma_h$ as a collection of delta measures at points.)  Definition \eqref{eq:sigmah} is extended to boundary nodes below.
% also, \eqref{eq:sigmah} is the Galerkin (weak-form) instance of the strong-form nodal residual underlying \cite{BuelerFarrell2024}'s mixed complementarity classification in their \S 7, and it matches the convention already used by \texttt{viamr}'s \texttt{nsv03mark()}

Because the constraints in \eqref{eq:Kh} compare piecewise-linear functions, admissibility is decided at the nodes alone.  Indeed, for $v_h \in \Vh$ the differences $v_h - \psiloh$ and $\psiuph - v_h$ are affine on each $T \in \Th$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:affine} promotes nodal inequalities to pointwise ones and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:nodalKh}
  v_h \in \Kh \quad\iff\quad v_h = \Ih g \text{ on } \dOm \ \text{ and } \ \psiloh(z) \le v_h(z) \le \psiuph(z) \ \text{ for all } z \in \Nh.
\end{equation}
This is the same observation already used above to see that $\Kh \ne \emptyset$.  It has a strong consequence for $\sigma_h$.

\begin{proposition}[Nodal complementarity]
\label{prop:nodalsign}  Let $\Nhbot := \{z \in \Nh : u_h(z) = \psiloh(z)\}$ and $\Nhtop := \{z \in \Nh : u_h(z) = \psiuph(z)\}$ be the \emph{nodal active sets}, the discrete lower- and upper-contact sets; they are disjoint by \eqref{eq:disc-nondeg}.  Let $z \in \Nhr$ be an interior node.  Then
\begin{align}
\label{eq:nodalcomp}
  z \in \Nhbot \ &\implies\ \sigma_h(z) \ge 0, \\
  z \in \Nhtop \ &\implies\ \sigma_h(z) \le 0, \notag \\
  z \notin \Nhbot \cup \Nhtop \ &\implies\ \sigma_h(z) = 0. \notag
\end{align}
Combining these, $\sigma_h(z) > 0$ implies $z \in \Nhbot$, and $\sigma_h(z) < 0$ implies $z \in \Nhtop$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $t \in \mathbb{R}$ set $v_h := u_h + t \phi_h^z \in \Vh$.  Since $z$ is an interior node, $\phi_h^z$ vanishes on $\dOm$, so $v_h = u_h = \Ih g$ there.  At any node $z' \ne z$ we have $\phi_h^z(z') = 0$, hence $v_h(z') = u_h(z')$, which satisfies the bounds in \eqref{eq:nodalKh} because $u_h \in \Kh$; and $v_h(z) = u_h(z) + t$.  So, by \eqref{eq:nodalKh},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tfeasible}
  v_h \in \Kh \quad\iff\quad \psiloh(z) - u_h(z) \ \le\ t \ \le\ \psiuph(z) - u_h(z).
\end{equation}
This is a condition at the single node $z$; no hypothesis about the rest of $z$'s star is involved.  Testing \eqref{eq:discVI} with any such $v_h$, and using \eqref{eq:sigmah}, gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tsigma}
  t\,\sigma_h(z) \ \ge\ 0 \qquad \text{for every feasible } t.
\end{equation}

Suppose $z \in \Nhbot$, so $u_h(z) = \psiloh(z)$.  Then $\psiuph(z) - u_h(z) \ge \delta_{\min} > 0$ by \eqref{eq:disc-nondeg}, so every $t \in (0,\delta_{\min}]$ is feasible in \eqref{eq:tfeasible}; dividing \eqref{eq:tsigma} by $t > 0$ preserves the inequality and gives $\sigma_h(z) \ge 0$.  Symmetrically, if $z \in \Nhtop$ then every $t \in [-\delta_{\min},0)$ is feasible, and dividing by $t < 0$ \emph{reverses} the inequality, giving $\sigma_h(z) \le 0$.  Finally, if $z$ lies in neither set then $\psiloh(z) < u_h(z) < \psiuph(z)$, so all $t$ of sufficiently small modulus are feasible, of either sign; both preceding conclusions then apply and $\sigma_h(z) = 0$.  The three cases are exhaustive, and the first two are mutually exclusive, because $\Nhbot \cap \Nhtop = \emptyset$ by \eqref{eq:disc-nondeg}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What replaces the unilateral global sign]
\label{rem:nodalglobal}
In the unilateral setting of \cite{NSV03}, in our sign convention, $\sigma_h \ge 0$ holds \emph{throughout} $\bar\Om$: raising $u_h$ by a non-negative multiple of any nodal basis function can never violate a one-sided lower bound, so \eqref{eq:tsigma} is available with $t > 0$ at every interior node.  With two obstacles that fails, and $\sigma_h$ has no global sign.  Proposition~\ref{prop:nodalsign} is what takes its place: a \emph{nodal} statement, which is precisely the discrete form of the complementarity structure \eqref{eq:sigmasign}.  Note that $\sigma_h$ vanishes at every strictly inactive interior node, so the nodal values of $\sigma_h$ can be non-zero only on the nodal active sets $\Nhbot \cup \Nhtop$.  (This is not a pointwise statement: $\sigma_h$ is piecewise linear, so it is generally non-zero throughout any element having an active vertex.)
\end{remark}


\subsection{Thinned/eroded element-wise active sets}

Extending \cite{NSV03}, for elements we split $\Th$ into three parts:
\begin{align}
\label{eq:Thsplit}
  \Thbot &:= \{T \in \Th : \Uh(T) \subset \{u_h = \psiloh\}\}, \\
  \Thtop &:= \{T \in \Th : \Uh(T) \subset \{u_h = \psiuph\}\}, \notag \\
  \Thplus &:= \Th \setminus (\Thbot \cup \Thtop). \notag
\end{align}
Then define $\Lamhbot$ and $\Lamhtop$ as the corresponding unions of elements,
\begin{equation}
\Lamhbot = \bigcup_{T \in \Thbot} T, \qquad \Lamhtop = \bigcup_{T \in \Thtop} T,
\end{equation}
which are also discrete lower- and upper-contact active sets, but now element-wise.

The element sets $\Thbot$ and $\Thtop$ are exactly what the \texttt{VIAMR.thinelemactive()} method marks.  An element is marked only if it \emph{and every element sharing a node with it} is active.  This erodes, by one element ring, the more obvious element-wise active set.  In fact, the simpler method \texttt{VIAMR.elemactive()} marks an element active if all its nodes are active, i.e.~they are in $\Nhbot,\Nhtop$ respectively.  The thinning/erosion is precisely the content of ``$\Uh(T) \subset \{u_h = \psiloh\}$'' because $\Uh(T)$ \emph{is} $T$'s ring of neighbors (every $T' \in \Th$ meeting $T$ in a node).  In any case, the relevant library calls are
\begin{align*}
\Thbot &= \texttt{thinelemactive(uh, psiloh, boxside="lower")} \\
\Thtop &= \texttt{thinelemactive(uh, psiuph, boxside="upper")}.
\end{align*}

\begin{remark}[The library tests a centroid value, against a tolerance]
\label{rem:activetol}
Both \texttt{elemactive()} and \texttt{thinelemactive()} decide activity by evaluating the gap at a single DG0 degree of freedom, the cell centroid, and comparing to a tolerance \texttt{activetol}, rather than by testing every vertex.  For piecewise-linear $u_h,\psiloh$ the centroid value of $u_h - \psiloh$ is the average of the vertex values, and this gap is non-negative because $u_h \in \Kh$, so at zero tolerance the two tests agree.  At a positive tolerance the centroid test is weaker: a triangle with vertex gaps $0,0,3\,\texttt{activetol}/2$ has centroid gap $\texttt{activetol}/2$ and is marked active, though one of its vertices is not.  The identifications above are therefore exact in the limit $\texttt{activetol}\to 0$.
\end{remark}

The element-wise sets promote the nodal signs of Proposition \ref{prop:nodalsign} to pointwise ones (over points in the elements).  Let $T \in \Thbot$ and let $z$ be a vertex of $T$, thus a lower-active node.  If $z$ is an interior node then $\sigma_h(z) \ge 0$ by Proposition \ref{prop:nodalsign}.  If instead $z$ is a boundary node then, because every element containing $z$ meets $T$, we have $\Uh(z) \subset \Uh(T) \subset \{u_h = \psiloh\}$, and the boundary-node definition \eqref{eq:sigmah-bnd} below gives $\sigma_h(z) = \hat\sigma_h(z)^+ \ge 0$.  Either way $\sigma_h \ge 0$ at each vertex of $T$, whence $\sigma_h \ge 0$ throughout $T$ by Lemma \ref{lem:affine}; symmetrically $\sigma_h \le 0$ on each $T \in \Thtop$.  On $\Thplus$ elements no pointwise sign is claimed.

No separation hypothesis, and no relation between $h$ and the geometry of the contact sets, enters any of this; see Remark \ref{rem:nodalglobal}.  Proposition~\ref{prop:separation}'s constant $r_0$ therefore plays no role in the correctness of what follows.

However, $r_0$ still matters for \emph{sharpness}.  One expects that once $h \lesssim r_0$, $\Thplus$ separates the discrete free boundaries, rather than swallowing-up contact elements that should have been classified into $\Thbot$ or $\Thtop$, but we do not prove this.


\subsection{The residual at boundary nodes}

At a boundary node $z \in \Nh \setminus \Nhr$, $\phi_h^z$ does not vanish on $\dOm$, and $v_h = \Ih g$ is fixed there by discrete admissibility \eqref{eq:Kh}, so the stationarity argument of Proposition~\ref{prop:nodalsign} cannot be applied.  In fact,  we define $\sigma_h(z)$ at a boundary node by the following diagnostic construction, similar to \cite[\S 2.1]{NSV03}.

Because $\phi_h^z$ does not vanish on $\dOm$ for a boundary node, the bracket in \eqref{eq:sigmah} is no longer the whole residual: integrating $\left<\nabla u_h,\nabla\phi_h^z\right>$ by parts elementwise picks up an extra boundary flux term, $\left<\!\left<\partial_n u_h,\phi_h^z\right>\!\right> := \int_{\dOm} (\partial_n u_h)\,\phi_h^z$, where $\partial_n$ is the outward-normal derivative, taken elementwise. Write
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigmahat}
  \hat\sigma_h(z) := \Bigl(\int_\Om \phi_h^z\Bigr)^{-1}
  \Bigl[\left<\nabla u_h,\nabla\phi_h^z\right> - \left< f,\phi_h^z\right> - \left<\!\left<\partial_n u_h,\phi_h^z\right>\!\right>\Bigr],
  \qquad z \in \Nh\setminus\Nhr,
\end{equation}
for this boundary-corrected nodal residual, normalized exactly as in \eqref{eq:sigmah}.

Since \eqref{eq:sigmahat} is not derived from a stationarity argument using VI \eqref{eq:discVI}, $\hat\sigma_h(z)$ carries no guaranteed sign, but \cite{NSV03} handles this by keeping only the part which has the sign which convention \eqref{eq:sigmasign} demands, and discarding the rest.  We extend the same device to both obstacles, defining, for $z \in \Nh\setminus\Nhr$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigmah-bnd}
  \sigma_h(z) := \begin{cases}
    \hat\sigma_h(z)^+, & \text{if } \Uh(z) \subset \{u_h = \psiloh\}, \\
    -\hat\sigma_h(z)^-, & \text{if } \Uh(z) \subset \{u_h = \psiuph\}, \\
    0, & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $v^+ := \max\{v,0\}$ and $v^- := -\min\{v,0\}$ are the positive and negative parts of $v$.  Since $\hat\sigma_h(z)^+ \ge 0$ and $-\hat\sigma_h(z)^- \le 0$ by construction, \eqref{eq:sigmah-bnd} satisfies the discrete version of \eqref{eq:sigmasign} for boundary nodes by fiat.

Thus, at a boundary node whose star lies wholly in one active set, we keep the correctly-signed part of $\hat\sigma_h(z)$ and zero out the rest; at any other boundary node, including one whose star meets both contact types or neither, $\sigma_h(z)$ is simply set to zero.  Up to the sign flip already noted, the first case of \eqref{eq:sigmah-bnd} is exactly \cite[formula (2.2)]{NSV03}, and the second case, for upper-contact boundary nodes, is appropriately symmetric.


\subsection{Positivity-preserving interpolation}

We record here the ball-averaging interpolation operator of Chen \& Nochetto \cite{ChenNochetto2000}, since the Galerkin-functional and barrier constructions to come both use it.  The construction does not depend on the obstacles at all, so it is unchanged for the box-constrained case; we simply state it following \cite[\S 2.2]{NSV03}, and cite \cite{ChenNochetto2000} for proofs.  We call a linear operator $A$ \emph{positive} if $\varphi \ge 0$ implies $A\varphi \ge 0$.

To define the interpolation operator $\Pih : L_1(\Om) \to \Vhr$, fix the mesh $\Th$ first, and then, for each $z \in \Nhr$, fix a ball $B(z) \subset \Uh(z)$, i.e.~centered at $z$ and contained in its star, whose radius is comparable to the local mesh size near $z$.  On a graded or adapted mesh the radius will vary from node to node; $B(z)$ is generally \emph{not} one ball translated throughout $\Om$.  The precise choice of radius, needed only for \eqref{eq:Pihprops} below to hold uniformly in $h$, does not concern us here; see \cite[\S 6]{NSV03}.

For $\varphi \in L_1(\Om)$, set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Pih}
  \Pih\varphi(z) := \frac{1}{|B(z)|}\int_{B(z)}\varphi, \qquad z \in \Nhr.
\end{equation}
Positivity is immediate: the average of a non-negative function over a ball is non-negative.  Linearity is also clear because the balls $B(z)$ only depend on the mesh, not the input $\varphi$.  Note $\Pih$ is not a projection: $\Pih^2 \ne \Pih$ in general.

Write $\|\cdot\|_{0,p;\omega}$ for the $L^p(\omega)$ norm over $\omega \subset \bar\Om$.  Chen \& Nochetto \cite{ChenNochetto2000} show that, for every $1\le p\le\infty$, $T\in\Th$, and interior side $S\in\Sh$, and for $\varphi \in \mathring W^{1,p}(\Om)$,
\begin{align}
\label{eq:Pihprops}
  \|\nabla\Pih\varphi\|_{0,p;T} &\preceq \|\nabla\varphi\|_{0,p;\Uh(T)}, \\
  \|\varphi - \Pih\varphi\|_{0,p;T} &\preceq h_T\|\nabla\varphi\|_{0,p;\Uh(T)}, \notag \\
  \|\varphi - \Pih\varphi\|_{0,p;S} &\preceq h_S^{1-1/p}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{0,p;\Uh(S)}. \notag
\end{align}
If in addition $\varphi \in \mathring W^{1,1}(\Om)$ and $\nabla\varphi \in BV(\Om)^d$, then also
\begin{align}
\label{eq:PihBV}
  \|\varphi-\Pih\varphi\|_{0,1;T} &\preceq h_T^2\, |D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Uh(T)), \\
  \|\varphi-\Pih\varphi\|_{0,1;S} &\preceq h_S\, |D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Uh(S)), \notag
\end{align}
where $|D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}} := \sum_{i=1}^d |D\partial_i\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}$ sums the variation measures of the components of $\nabla\varphi$, and $|D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\omega) = \|D^2\varphi\|_{0,1;\omega}$ whenever $\varphi \in W^{2,1}(\Om)$ and $\omega \subset \Om$ is measurable.  We will need the properties \eqref{eq:Pihprops} and \eqref{eq:PihBV} in following sections.


\section{A posteriori estimator} \label{sec:estimator}

\subsection{The Galerkin functional}

Following \cite[\S 3]{NSV03}, write $\Thzero := \Thbot \cup \Thtop$ for the union of both wholly-active element sets.  Note that from \eqref{eq:Thsplit} we can now write $\Thplus = \Th \setminus \Thzero$.  Define $\tilde\sigma_h \in H^{-1}(\Om)$ by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigmatilde}
  \left<\tilde\sigma_h,\varphi\right> := \sum_{T\in\Thzero}\int_T \sigma_h\varphi + \sum_{T\in\Thplus}\int_T \Ih(\sigma_h\Pih\varphi), \qquad \varphi \in \mathring H^1(\Om).
\end{equation}
This is exactly \cite[formula (3.1)]{NSV03}: on wholly-active elements $\tilde\sigma_h$ agrees with $\sigma_h$ itself; on the other elements $\Thplus$, which are inactive or transitional, the test function is first replaced by its positivity-preserving interpolant $\Pih\varphi$ before pairing with $\sigma_h$.  Nothing in \eqref{eq:sigmatilde} distinguishes lower from upper contact, so we have kept \cite{NSV03}'s definition.

The \emph{Galerkin functional} $\Gh \in H^{-1}(\Om)$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Gh}
  \left<\Gh,\varphi\right> := -\left<\nabla u_h,\nabla\varphi\right> + \left< f + \tilde\sigma_h,\varphi\right>, \qquad \varphi \in \mathring H^1(\Om).
\end{equation}
This is \cite[formula (3.3)]{NSV03}, but with $\tilde\sigma_h$ entering with the opposite sign, consistent because our $\sigma_h$ is the negative of theirs.

Eliminating $f$, definition \eqref{eq:sigma} of $\sigma$ gives the equivalent form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Ghagain}
\left<\Gh,\varphi\right> = \left<\nabla(u-u_h),\nabla\varphi\right> - \left<\sigma-\tilde\sigma_h,\varphi\right>.
\end{equation}
Thus $\Gh$ measures two mismatches at once, between $u$ and $u_h$, and between the exact residual $\sigma$ and its discrete surrogate $\tilde\sigma_h$.

Consider the application of $\Gh$ to a piecewise-linear function $\varphi_h$.  The following Lemma can be read as saying that the result would be zero if $\sigma_h$ and $\tilde \sigma_h$ were computing the same thing, and if the inner products $\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>$ and $\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>_h$ were the same.  Because these statements are not true, we ``almost'' have orthogonality.

\begin{lemma}[Almost Galerkin orthogonality]
\label{lem:almostGO}
For all $\varphi_h \in \Vhr$,
\begin{align}
\label{eq:almostGO}
  \left< \Gh, \varphi_h\right> &= \left<\tilde\sigma_h,\varphi_h\right> - \left< \sigma_h,\varphi_h\right>_h \\
  &= \sum_{T\in\Thzero}\int_T \bigl(\sigma_h\varphi_h - \Ih(\sigma_h\varphi_h)\bigr)
  \ +\ \sum_{T\in\Thplus}\int_T \Ih\bigl(\sigma_h(\Pih\varphi_h - \varphi_h)\bigr). \notag
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $\varphi_h\in\Vhr$, \eqref{eq:sigmah} gives $\left<\nabla u_h,\nabla\varphi_h\right> = \left< f,\varphi_h\right> + \left<\sigma_h,\varphi_h\right>_h$: this is $\sigma_h$'s defining relation at interior nodes, and it holds at \emph{every} $z\in\Nhr$ unconditionally, so by linearity it holds for every $\varphi_h \in \Vhr$, not just those supported where a star is wholly active.  Substituting into \eqref{eq:Gh},
\[
  \left<\Gh,\varphi_h\right> = -\bigl(\left< f,\varphi_h\right> + \left<\sigma_h,\varphi_h\right>_h\bigr) + \left< f,\varphi_h\right> + \left<\tilde\sigma_h,\varphi_h\right> = \left<\tilde\sigma_h,\varphi_h\right> - \left<\sigma_h,\varphi_h\right>_h,
\]
the first equality.  For the second, $\left<\sigma_h,\varphi_h\right>_h = \sum_{T\in\Th}\int_T \Ih(\sigma_h\varphi_h)$, since $\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>_h$ is by definition $\int_\Om \Ih(\cdot)$ and the integral is additive over $\Th$.  Subtracting this from \eqref{eq:sigmatilde} and collecting terms, the $\Thzero$ part gives $\int_T \bigl(\sigma_h\varphi_h - \Ih(\sigma_h\varphi_h)\bigr)$, and the $\Thplus$ part gives $\int_T \bigl(\Ih(\sigma_h\Pih\varphi_h) - \Ih(\sigma_h\varphi_h)\bigr) = \int_T \Ih\bigl(\sigma_h(\Pih\varphi_h-\varphi_h)\bigr)$ by linearity of $\Ih$.
\end{proof}

Every step above uses only the set-theoretic split $\Th = \Thzero \sqcup \Thplus$ and the unconditional definition \eqref{eq:sigmah} of $\sigma_h$ at interior nodes.  These definitions do not care whether the unified (and thinned) active elements $\Thzero$ are from a lower or upper obstacle.

In the following Lemma, $\Rh$ is a residual while $\Qh$ is a quadrature error.

\begin{lemma}[Representation formula for $\Gh$]
\label{lem:Gh-repn}
For each interior side $S \in \Sh$, let $T^-,T^+ \in \Th$ be the two elements meeting along $S$, fix a unit normal $\nu$ pointing from $T^-$ into $T^+$, and write
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:jump}
  \jump{\partial_\nu u_h} := \partial_\nu u_h|_{T^+} - \partial_\nu u_h|_{T^-}
\end{equation}
for the jump across $S$.  This sign convention matters below, though it does not affect the estimator itself, in which only $|\jump{\partial_\nu u_h}|$ appears.  Define $\Rh \in H^{-1}(\Om)$, and $\Qh \in \Vhr^*$ a linear functional on the discrete space, by
\begin{align}
\label{eq:RhQh}
  \left<\Rh,\varphi\right> &:= \sum_{S\in\Sh}\int_S \jump{\partial_\nu u_h}\,\varphi + \sum_{T\in\Thzero}\int_T (f+\sigma_h)\varphi + \sum_{T\in\Thplus}\int_T f\varphi, \\
  \left<\Qh,\varphi_h\right> &:= \sum_{T\in\Thzero}\int_T \bigl(\sigma_h\varphi_h - \Ih(\sigma_h\varphi_h)\bigr). \notag
\end{align}
Then, for $\varphi \in \mathring H^1(\Om)$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Gh-repn}
  \left<\Gh,\varphi\right> = \left<\Rh,\varphi-\Pih\varphi\right> + \left<\Qh,\Pih\varphi\right>.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Split $\left<\Gh,\varphi\right> = \left<\Gh,\varphi-\Pih\varphi\right> + \left<\Gh,\Pih\varphi\right>$, and evaluate each term.

For $\left<\Gh,\varphi-\Pih\varphi\right>$, use definition \eqref{eq:Gh} with $\varphi$ replaced by $\varphi - \Pih\varphi \in \mathring H^1(\Om)$.  Integrate $\left<\nabla u_h,\nabla(\varphi-\Pih\varphi)\right>$ by parts elementwise: $u_h$ is affine on each $T$, so no interior element term survives, leaving only the interior jump terms.  (There is no boundary term since $\varphi-\Pih\varphi$ vanishes on $\dOm$.)  Concretely, for any $v \in \mathring H^1(\Om)$ the two elements along $S$ contribute $\partial_\nu u_h|_{T^-}$ and $-\partial_\nu u_h|_{T^+}$, their outward normals at $S$ being $\nu$ and $-\nu$, so with convention \eqref{eq:jump}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:byparts}
  \left<\nabla u_h,\nabla v\right> = -\sum_{S\in\Sh}\int_S \jump{\partial_\nu u_h}\,v,
\end{equation}
and the leading minus sign in \eqref{eq:Gh} turns this into the $+\sum_S$ appearing in $\Rh$.  For $\left<\tilde\sigma_h,\varphi-\Pih\varphi\right>$, use definition \eqref{eq:sigmatilde}.  On $\Thzero$, $\tilde\sigma_h=\sigma_h$ directly, while on $\Thplus$, $\tilde\sigma_h$ pairs against $\Pih$ applied to the test function, and by linearity of $\Pih$, $\Pih(\varphi-\Pih\varphi) = \Pih\varphi - \Pih^2\varphi$.  Altogether,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Gh-first}
  \left<\Gh,\varphi-\Pih\varphi\right> = \left<\Rh,\varphi-\Pih\varphi\right> \ +\ \sum_{T\in\Thplus}\int_T \Ih\bigl(\sigma_h(\Pih\varphi-\Pih^2\varphi)\bigr).
\end{equation}

For $\left<\Gh,\Pih\varphi\right>$, apply Lemma~\ref{lem:almostGO} with $\varphi_h=\Pih\varphi\in\Vhr$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Gh-second}
  \left<\Gh,\Pih\varphi\right> = \left<\Qh,\Pih\varphi\right> \ -\ \sum_{T\in\Thplus}\int_T \Ih\bigl(\sigma_h(\Pih\varphi-\Pih^2\varphi)\bigr).
\end{equation}

Adding \eqref{eq:Gh-second} and \eqref{eq:Gh-first} and cancelling leaves \eqref{eq:Gh-repn}.
\end{proof}

As with Lemma~\ref{lem:almostGO}, this derivation only used the $\Th = \Thzero \sqcup \Thplus$ split, $\sigma_h$'s definition, and linearity of $\Pih$ and $\Ih$; which obstacle is active on $\Thzero$ is not relevant.  The cancellation is exactly why $\tilde\sigma_h$, rather than $\sigma_h$ itself, appears in \eqref{eq:Gh}.  If one used $\sigma_h$ in place of $\tilde\sigma_h$ it would leave an uncancelled $\Thplus$ term in \eqref{eq:Gh-repn}, one that does not shrink at the rate needed for a second-order estimator; see the discussion after \cite[formula (3.6)]{NSV03}.


\subsection{Localized residual and quadrature estimators}

Following \cite[\S\S 4--5]{NSV03}, our next task is to bound $\Gh$ in a dual space larger than $\mathring H^1(\Om)$ itself.  As in \cite[\S 3.2]{NSV03}, let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:W}
  W := \{\varphi \in \mathring H^1(\Om) : \nabla\varphi \in BV(\Om)^d\},
\end{equation}
equipped with the seminorm $\varphi \mapsto |D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Om)$ already used in \eqref{eq:PihBV}.  This space is slightly larger than $W^{2,1}(\Om) \cap \mathring H^1(\Om)$, because $\nabla\varphi \in W^{1,1}(\Om)^d$ implies $\nabla\varphi \in BV(\Om)^d$ but not conversely, and it still contains the discrete space $\Vhr$; both facts matter below.

For $1 \le p \le \infty$, with dual exponent $p' = p/(p-1)$, define the \emph{localized residual} $R_p$, element-wise over $T\in\Th$, by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Rp}
  R_p|_T := h_T^{-1/p'}\,|T|^{-1/p}\,\bigl\|\jump{\partial_\nu u_h}\bigr\|_{0,p;\partial T \setminus \dOm}
  \ +\ \begin{cases} |f + \sigma_h|, & T \in \Thzero, \\ |f|, & T \in \Thplus. \end{cases}
\end{equation}
This is \cite[formula (3.7)]{NSV03}, again with the sign of $\sigma_h$ flipped: the first term is the usual interior-flux-jump contribution over $\partial T$, and the second is the residual magnitude $|f+\sigma_h|$ on wholly-active elements, matching the volume density of $\Rh$ in \eqref{eq:RhQh}, or the bare source term $|f|$ elsewhere.  Nothing here refers to the nature of the active obstacle, so \eqref{eq:Rp} is unchanged from the unilateral case.

By the BV-approximation properties \eqref{eq:PihBV} of $\Pih$, and because shape-regularity gives the patches $\Uh(T)$, $\Uh(S)$ finite overlap, so that $\sum_{T\in\Th} |D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Uh(T)) \preceq |D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Om)$ and likewise over sides, definition \eqref{eq:RhQh} of $\Rh$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Rhbound}
  \bigl|\left<\Rh,\varphi-\Pih\varphi\right>\bigr| \ \preceq\ \|h^2 R_\infty\|_{0,\infty;\Om}\, |D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Om)
\end{equation}
for $\varphi \in W$.  This is \cite[formula (3.8)]{NSV03}.

The second term $\left<\Qh,\Pih\varphi\right>$ in the representation formula \eqref{eq:Gh-repn} accounts for the effect of quadrature.  It too is independent of obstacle type, and it already arises for an unconstrained linear problem.  For $v_h,w_h$ affine on $T$ the mass-lumping error satisfies $\bigl|\int_T (v_h w_h - \Ih(v_h w_h))\bigr| \preceq h_T^2 \int_T |\nabla v_h| |\nabla w_h|$; applying this with $v_h = \sigma_h$ and $w_h = \Pih\varphi$, summing over $T \in \Thzero$, and using H\"older with the conjugate pair $(d,d')$ gives the first bound below.  Then applying the first bound in \eqref{eq:Pihprops} together with the Sobolev-type inequality $\|\nabla\varphi\|_{0,d';\Om} \preceq |D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Om)$, valid for $\varphi \in W$, gives the second.  Altogether we have \cite[formula (3.9)]{NSV03}, namely
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Qhbound}
  \bigl|\left<\Qh,\Pih\varphi\right>\bigr| \ \preceq\ \|h^2\nabla\sigma_h\|_{0,d;\Lamh}\, \|\nabla\Pih\varphi\|_{0,d';\Om}
  \ \preceq\ \|h^2\nabla\sigma_h\|_{0,d;\Lamh}\, |D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Om),
\end{equation}
where $\Lamh := \Lamhbot \cup \Lamhtop = \bigcup_{T\in\Thzero} T$ is the union of both active regions.  We have generalized from the unilateral contact set \cite[formula (2.4)]{NSV03} to the bilateral case in the obvious way.

Both \eqref{eq:Rhbound} and \eqref{eq:Qhbound} control their left sides by the same seminorm $|D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Om)$, so they combine into a bound on $\Gh$ in the corresponding dual norm,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Ghdualnorm}
  \|\Gh\|_{-2,\infty;\Om} := \sup\bigl\{\left<\Gh,\varphi\right> : \varphi\in W,\ |D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Om)\le 1\bigr\}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, inserting \eqref{eq:Rhbound} and \eqref{eq:Qhbound} into the representation formula \eqref{eq:Gh-repn} gives the upper bound
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Ghbound}
  \|\Gh\|_{-2,\infty;\Om} \ \preceq\ \|h^2 R_\infty\|_{0,\infty;\Om} \ +\ \|h^2\nabla\sigma_h\|_{0,d;\Lamh}.
\end{equation}
Bound \eqref{eq:Ghbound} is \cite[formula (3.10)]{NSV03}.  As with the Galerkin functional itself, nothing in its derivation distinguishes lower from upper contact; the same bound controls $\Gh$ whether the active elements $T\in\Thzero$ come from $\Thbot$ or $\Thtop$.


\section{Barriers} \label{sec:barriers}

Following \cite[\S 4]{NSV03}, we now bracket the exact solution between continuous \emph{barriers}, $u_\bot \le u \le u^\top$, built from the discrete solution, the data, and a correction $w$ which solves a linear problem.

Let $w \in \mathring H^1(\Om)$ be the Riesz representation of the Galerkin functional $\Gh$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:w}
  \int_\Om \nabla w \cdot \nabla\varphi = \left<\Gh,\varphi\right> \qquad \text{for all } \varphi \in \mathring H^1(\Om).
\end{equation}
As in \cite[\S 4]{NSV03} we assume throughout this section that $w$ is continuous.  This is a statement about the linear problem \eqref{eq:w}, in which the obstacles enter only through the data $\Gh$, so \cite[Lemma 5.1]{NSV03} applies here to justify H\"older continuity of $w$.

\subsection{Reduction to two pairings}

For each candidate barrier below the gradient is $\nabla u_h + \nabla w$ and certain reductions apply.

\begin{lemma}[Barrier criterion]
\label{lem:barriercrit}
If $c \ge \|g - \Ih g\|_{0,\infty;\dOm}$ is any constant then the functions
\begin{align}
u^\top &:= u_h + w + c \\
u_\bot &:= u_h + w - c
\end{align}
satisfy
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] If $v := (u - u^\top)^+$ then $v \ge 0$, $v \in \mathring H^1(\Om) \cap C(\bar\Om)$, and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:barrierenergy}
  \|\nabla v\|_{0,2;\Om}^2 = \left<\sigma,v\right> - \left<\tilde\sigma_h,v\right>.
\end{equation}
Thus if $\left<\sigma,v\right> \le 0$ and $\left<\tilde\sigma_h,v\right> \ge 0$ then $u \le u^\top$ in $\Om$.
\item[(ii)] If $v := (u_\bot - u)^+$ then $v \ge 0$, $v \in \mathring H^1(\Om) \cap C(\bar\Om)$, and
\begin{equation}
\|\nabla v\|_{0,2;\Om}^2 = \left<\tilde\sigma_h,v\right> - \left<\sigma,v\right>.
\end{equation}
Thus if $\left<\sigma,v\right> \ge 0$ and $\left<\tilde\sigma_h,v\right> \le 0$ then $u_\bot \le u$ in $\Om$.
\end{itemize}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We prove (i); part (ii) is the same computation with the roles of the two functions in $v$ exchanged and the sign of $c$ reversed.

Since $w \in \mathring H^1(\Om)$ is continuous it vanishes on $\dOm$, while $u = g$ and $u_h = \Ih g$ there.  Hence $(u - u^\top)|_{\dOm} = (g - \Ih g) - c \le 0$, by the hypothesis on $c$, so $v$ vanishes on $\dOm$.  Also $v \ge 0$ by definition, and $v$ is continuous because $u$, $u_h$, and $w$ are.

For \eqref{eq:barrierenergy}, recall $\nabla v = \nabla(u-u^\top)$ almost everywhere on $\{v > 0\}$ and $\nabla v = 0$ almost everywhere elsewhere.  Therefore, using $\nabla u^\top = \nabla u_h + \nabla w$ and then \eqref{eq:w} and \eqref{eq:Ghagain},
\[
  \|\nabla v\|_{0,2;\Om}^2 = \int_\Om \nabla(u - u^\top)\cdot\nabla v
  = \left<\nabla(u-u_h),\nabla v\right> - \left<\Gh,v\right>
  = \left<\sigma - \tilde\sigma_h,\,v\right>.
\]
Finally, if the two pairings have the stated signs then $\|\nabla v\|_{0,2;\Om} = 0$, so $v$ is constant, and $v \in \mathring H^1(\Om)$ then forces $v \equiv 0$.  That is, $u \le u^\top$.
\end{proof}

This Lemma isolates what $c$ must accomplish.  In applying this Lemma we will use different $c$ for the two barriers, but each will satisfy $c \ge \|g - \Ih g\|_{0,\infty;\dOm}$, thus absorbing the boundary interpolation error.

Consider the upper barrier, so that we need $\left<\sigma,v\right> \le 0$ and $\left<\tilde\sigma_h,v\right> \ge 0$.  In either part of the Lemma the function $v$ is continuous and non-negative, so, $\sigma$ being a signed Radon measure (\S \ref{sec:setup}), the pairing is integration \cite[Theorem 6.9]{KinderlehrerStampacchia1980}, and the Jordan decomposition $\sigma = \mu_\bot - \mu_\top$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigmapairing}
  \left<\sigma,v\right> = \int_\Om v\,d\mu_\bot - \int_\Om v\,d\mu_\top \ \le\ \int_\Om v\,d\mu_\bot .
\end{equation}
So the upper-contact measure is harmless for the upper barrier, and it suffices that $v$ vanish on $\Cbot$, where $\mu_\bot$ is supported.  This is the only role of the lower obstacle here (and it is the mechanism \cite{NSV03} already uses).

To show $\left<\tilde\sigma_h,v\right> \ge 0$ for the upper barrier, recall that definition \eqref{eq:sigmatilde} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigmatildesplit}
  \left<\tilde\sigma_h,v\right> = \sum_{T\in\Thbot}\int_T \sigma_h v \ +\ \sum_{T\in\Thtop}\int_T \sigma_h v \ +\ \sum_{T\in\Thplus}\int_T \Ih(\sigma_h \Pih v),
\end{equation}
in which the first sum is already non-negative, because $\sigma_h \ge 0$ pointwise on each $T \in \Thbot$ (\S \ref{sec:discretization}) and $v \ge 0$.  The box constraint is relevant in the second and third sums: $\sigma_h \le 0$ on $\Thtop$ elements, and $\sigma_h$ has no sign at all on $\Thplus$ elements, so both can contribute negatively.  (In the unilateral case of \cite{NSV03} this does not happen, since there $\sigma_h \ge 0$ on all of $\bar\Om$ and hence $\left<\tilde\sigma_h,v\right> \ge 0$ for every $v \ge 0$ (Remark~\ref{rem:nodalglobal}).)  The upper barrier must therefore contain a second mechanism, which forces $v$ to vanish wherever $\sigma_h < 0$ can influence \eqref{eq:sigmatildesplit}.

Proposition \ref{prop:upperbarrier} will set $c$ so that these considerations apply and we can get $u\le u^\top$.  The lower barrier is symmetric in every respect.


\subsection{Star-dilated strict contact sets}

Let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Nhpm}
  \Nh^+ := \{z \in \Nh \,:\, \sigma_h(z) > 0\}, \qquad
  \Nh^- := \{z \in \Nh \,:\, \sigma_h(z) < 0\}
\end{equation}
be the nodes at which the discrete residual is strictly signed, and let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omegas}
  \ombot := \Uh(\Nh^+), \qquad \omtop := \Uh(\Nh^-)
\end{equation}
be their star-dilations, each a union of closed elements by \eqref{eq:star}.  By Proposition~\ref{prop:nodalsign} at interior nodes, and by the first two cases of \eqref{eq:sigmah-bnd} at boundary nodes, $\Nh^+ \subset \Nhbot$ and $\Nh^- \subset \Nhtop$; a strictly-signed node is active, and the sign says against which obstacle.  The barrier proofs use two consequences of \eqref{eq:omegas}.  First,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:omegacontains}
  \{\sigma_h < 0\} \subset \omtop \qquad \text{and} \qquad \{\sigma_h > 0\} \subset \ombot,
\end{equation}
because if $\sigma_h(x) < 0$ then, $\sigma_h$ being affine on any element $T$ containing $x$, some vertex $z$ of $T$ has $\sigma_h(z) < 0$, and then $T \subset \Uh(z) \subset \omtop$.  Second, $B(z) \subset \Uh(z) \subset \omtop$ for every $z \in \Nh^- \cap \Nhr$, by the construction of the balls in \S \ref{sec:discretization}, and symmetrically for $\ombot$.

\begin{remark}[Why the star, and not the bare set $\{\sigma_h<0\}$]
\label{rem:whystar}
The corresponding set in \cite[formula (4.6)]{NSV03} is not dilated.  There the third sum in \eqref{eq:sigmatildesplit} is handled instead by an argument which needs the global sign: if $v > 0$ at an interior point of an element $T$, then $\sigma_h$ vanishes at that point, and an affine function of one sign on $T$ which vanishes at an interior point of $T$ vanishes identically on $T$, hence at the vertices of $T$ as well.  Bilaterally that reasoning fails exactly where it is needed.  A node $z \in \Nh^-$ can be a vertex of a transition element $T \in \Thplus$ on which $\sigma_h$ changes sign, and then $v > 0$ at a point of $T$ where $\sigma_h \ge 0$ is no contradiction, so nothing forces $(\Pih v)(z) = 0$.  Dilating by one ring of elements restores the conclusion directly, at the cost of measuring the barrier term on a slightly larger set.
\end{remark}


\subsection{Upper barrier}

\begin{proposition}[Upper barrier]
\label{prop:upperbarrier}
The function
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ubarupper}
  u^\top := u_h + w + \|w\|_{0,\infty;\Om} + \|g-\Ih g\|_{0,\infty;\dOm} + \|(\psilo-u_h)^+\|_{0,\infty;\Om} + \|(\psiup-u_h)^+\|_{0,\infty;\omtop}
\end{equation}
satisfies $u \le u^\top$ in $\Om$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $c$ denote the sum of the four constants in \eqref{eq:ubarupper}, so that $u^\top = u_h + w + c$ and $c \ge \|g - \Ih g\|_{0,\infty;\dOm}$.  Set $v := (u - u^\top)^+$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:barriercrit}(i) it suffices to show $\left<\sigma,v\right> \le 0$ and $\left<\tilde\sigma_h,v\right> \ge 0$.

Everything follows from two pointwise lower bounds for $u^\top$.  Because $w + \|w\|_{0,\infty;\Om} \ge 0$ and the remaining constants in \eqref{eq:ubarupper} are non-negative,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:utopbounds}
  u^\top \ \ge\ u_h + (\psilo - u_h)^+ \ \ge\ \psilo \quad\text{in } \Om,
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  u^\top \ \ge\ u_h + (\psiup - u_h)^+ \ \ge\ \psiup \quad\text{on } \omtop.
\end{equation}

For the first pairing, the first bound in \eqref{eq:utopbounds} gives $\{v>0\} = \{u > u^\top\} \subset \{u > \psilo\} = \Om \setminus \Cbot$.  Thus $v = 0$ on $\Cbot$, which supports $\mu_\bot$, and \eqref{eq:sigmapairing} gives $\left<\sigma,v\right> \le \int_\Om v \,d\mu_\bot = 0$.

For the second pairing, the second bound in \eqref{eq:utopbounds}, together with $u \le \psiup$ in $\Om$, gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:vzerotop}
  v \equiv 0 \quad \text{on } \omtop.
\end{equation}
We show that each of the three sums in \eqref{eq:sigmatildesplit} is non-negative.

If $T \in \Thbot$ then $\sigma_h \ge 0$ pointwise on $T$ and $v \ge 0$, so $\int_T \sigma_h v \ge 0$.

If $T \in \Thtop$ then $\sigma_h \le 0$ is affine on $T$, so either $\sigma_h \equiv 0$ on $T$, and $\int_T \sigma_h v = 0$, or $\sigma_h(z) < 0$ at some vertex $z$ of $T$.  In the latter case $z \in \Nh^-$, hence $T \subset \Uh(z) \subset \omtop$, and $\int_T \sigma_h v = 0$ by \eqref{eq:vzerotop}.

If $T \in \Thplus$, let $z_0,\dots,z_d$ be its vertices.  Since $\Ih(\sigma_h \Pih v)$ is affine on $T$, its integral is $|T|$ times the average of its vertex values:
\[
  \int_T \Ih(\sigma_h \Pih v) = \frac{|T|}{d+1}\sum_{i=0}^d \sigma_h(z_i)\,(\Pih v)(z_i).
\]
A boundary vertex contributes nothing, because $\Pih v \in \Vhr$ vanishes there.  At an interior vertex $z_i$ we have $(\Pih v)(z_i) \ge 0$, by $v \ge 0$ and positivity of $\Pih$, so the $i$th term is non-negative unless $\sigma_h(z_i) < 0$.  But if $\sigma_h(z_i) < 0$ then $z_i \in \Nh^-$, so $B(z_i) \subset \omtop$, and then \eqref{eq:Pih} and \eqref{eq:vzerotop} give $(\Pih v)(z_i) = |B(z_i)|^{-1}\int_{B(z_i)} v = 0$.  Hence every term of the sum is non-negative.
\end{proof}

The first two sums used the pointwise signs of $\sigma_h$ on wholly-active elements, from \S\ref{sec:discretization}, while the third used only nodal values of $\sigma_h$ and the ball averages defining $\Pih$.  In particular no hypothesis relating $h$ to the geometry of the contact sets is needed, and the separation constant $r_0$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:separation} again plays no role.

\subsection{Lower barrier}

\begin{proposition}[Lower barrier]
\label{prop:lowerbarrier}
The function
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ubarlower}
  u_\bot := u_h + w - \|w\|_{0,\infty;\Om} - \|g-\Ih g\|_{0,\infty;\dOm} - \|(u_h-\psiup)^+\|_{0,\infty;\Om} - \|(u_h-\psilo)^+\|_{0,\infty;\ombot}
\end{equation}
satisfies $u_\bot \le u$ in $\Om$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The construction mirrors Proposition~\ref{prop:upperbarrier}, exchanging $\bot \leftrightarrow \top$ and reversing all signs.  Writing $c$ for the sum of the four constants, $u_\bot = u_h + w - c$ with $c \ge \|g-\Ih g\|_{0,\infty;\dOm}$, and $v := (u_\bot - u)^+$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:barriercrit}(ii) asks for $\left<\sigma,v\right> \ge 0$ and $\left<\tilde\sigma_h,v\right> \le 0$.  The two pointwise upper bounds replacing \eqref{eq:utopbounds} are
\[
  u_\bot \ \le\ u_h - (u_h - \psiup)^+ \ \le\ \psiup \quad\text{in } \Om,
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  u_\bot \ \le\ u_h - (u_h - \psilo)^+ \ \le\ \psilo \quad\text{on } \ombot.
\]
The first gives $\{v>0\} \subset \Om \setminus \Ctop$, so $v$ vanishes on the support of $\mu_\top$ and \eqref{eq:sigmapairing} becomes $\left<\sigma,v\right> = \int_\Om v \,d\mu_\bot \ge 0$.  The second gives $v \equiv 0$ on $\ombot$, since $u \ge \psilo$ in $\Om$.

Now consider \eqref{eq:sigmatildesplit} term by term.  If $T \in \Thtop$ then $\sigma_h \le 0$ pointwise on $T$ and $v \ge 0$, so $\int_T \sigma_h v \le 0$.  If $T \in \Thbot$ then $\sigma_h \ge 0$ is affine on $T$, so either $\sigma_h \equiv 0$ on $T$, or $\sigma_h(z) > 0$ at some vertex $z$, in which case $z \in \Nh^+$ and $T \subset \Uh(z) \subset \ombot$, where $v$ vanishes; either way $\int_T\sigma_h v = 0$.  If $T \in \Thplus$ then, as before, $\int_T \Ih(\sigma_h\Pih v)$ is $|T|/(d+1)$ times $\sum_i \sigma_h(z_i)(\Pih v)(z_i)$, in which boundary vertices contribute nothing, interior vertices with $\sigma_h(z_i) \le 0$ contribute non-positively because $(\Pih v)(z_i) \ge 0$, and interior vertices with $\sigma_h(z_i) > 0$ have $B(z_i) \subset \ombot$ and hence $(\Pih v)(z_i) = 0$.  Therefore $\left<\tilde\sigma_h,v\right> \le 0$.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Partial pointwise error estimate}

Subtracting $u_h$ from the two barriers, Propositions~\ref{prop:upperbarrier} and~\ref{prop:lowerbarrier} give
\begin{align}
\label{eq:barrierbound}
  \|u-u_h\|_{0,\infty;\Om} \ \le\ & 2\|w\|_{0,\infty;\Om} + \|g-\Ih g\|_{0,\infty;\dOm} \\
  &+ \|(\psilo-u_h)^+\|_{0,\infty;\Om} + \|(u_h-\psiup)^+\|_{0,\infty;\Om} \notag \\
  &+ \|(\psiup-u_h)^+\|_{0,\infty;\omtop} + \|(u_h-\psilo)^+\|_{0,\infty;\ombot}, \notag
\end{align}
the box-constrained form of \cite[formula (4.8)]{NSV03}.  (A maximum of the two lines of obstacle terms would do, but we prefer the symmetric expression.)  Besides $\|w\|_{0,\infty;\Om}$, every term on the right is computable from the discrete solution and the given data.

The four obstacle terms come in two different kinds.  The two on the middle line measure the failure of $u_h$ to be admissible for the continuum problem, and they are controlled by the obstacle interpolation errors: since $u_h \ge \psiloh$ and $u_h \le \psiuph$ in $\Om$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:violationbound}
  \|(\psilo-u_h)^+\|_{0,\infty;\Om} \le \|\psilo-\psiloh\|_{0,\infty;\Om}
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \|(u_h-\psiup)^+\|_{0,\infty;\Om} \le \|\psiup-\psiuph\|_{0,\infty;\Om},
\end{equation}
each therefore $O(h^2)$ for an obstacle in $W^{2,\infty}(\Om)$.  The two on the last line are not interpolation errors.  They measure how far $u_h$ has detached from an obstacle on the region where the discrete residual asserts that it is attached, and they are the box-constrained analogue of the localized term in \cite[formula (4.6)]{NSV03}.

\begin{remark}[Optimal, non-computable choice]
\label{rem:optimalchoice}
As in \cite[Remark 4.3]{NSV03}, the proofs of Propositions~\ref{prop:upperbarrier} and~\ref{prop:lowerbarrier} go through with $\|(u-u_h)^+\|_{0,\infty;\omtop}$ and $\|(u_h-u)^+\|_{0,\infty;\ombot}$ in place of the last two terms, since only $u \le u^\top$ on $\omtop$, and $u_\bot \le u$ on $\ombot$, were used.  These are the optimal choices, and they are trivially efficient, but they are not computable.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The unilateral case]
\label{rem:unilateral}
If the upper obstacle is absent then $\sigma_h \ge 0$ throughout $\bar\Om$, so $\Nh^- = \emptyset$ and $\omtop = \emptyset$, and \eqref{eq:ubarupper} is exactly \cite[formula (4.2)]{NSV03}.  The lower barrier \eqref{eq:ubarlower} then reduces to \cite[formula (4.6)]{NSV03} except that our localized term is measured over $\ombot$ rather than over $\{\sigma_h>0\}$, which by \eqref{eq:omegacontains} is a subset.  As Remark~\ref{rem:whystar} explains, this one-ring enlargement is what replaces the global sign; we have not tried to remove it in the unilateral case.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Consequence for efficiency]
\label{rem:effring}
The local lower bounds of \cite[Prop.~6.3]{NSV03} for the localized term are stated for elements $T$ meeting $\{\sigma_h<0\}$, and already involve $\Uh(T)$ on the right-hand side.  Because our terms are measured over the star-dilated sets \eqref{eq:omegas}, the corresponding efficiency statement here will concern elements meeting $\ombot$ or $\omtop$, and should be expected to involve one further ring of elements.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Full localization and barrier sets]
\label{rem:localization}
Our estimator localizes to the star-dilated sets \eqref{eq:omegas} and not to the contact sets themselves; see Remark~\ref{rem:unilateral}.  For the unilateral problem, \cite{NSV05} closes the corresponding gap, producing estimators which, apart from obstacle resolution, vanish identically within the full-contact set, and then uses the pointwise bounds to construct barrier sets, that is, computable regions containing the free boundary, under a stability condition there.  We regard the bilateral analogues of both refinements as open, and intend to pursue them.  Note that the bilateral problem has two free boundaries, so bilateral barrier sets would have to be shown disjoint before they localize either one; Proposition~\ref{prop:separation} is the natural tool, since $r_0$ bounds $\operatorname{dist}(\Cbot,\Ctop)$ from below in terms of the data.
\end{remark}

Bounding $\|w\|_{0,\infty;\Om}$ is a question about the linear problem \eqref{eq:w} only, and it is taken up next.
