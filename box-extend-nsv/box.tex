\section{Introduction and goals} \label{sec:intro}

The VIAMR library (\href{https://github.com/StefanoFochesatto/viamr}{github.com/StefanoFochesatto/viamr}) implements adaptive mesh refinement (AMR) and mesh adaptation algorithms for variational inequalities (VIs).  Some of the implemented methods incorporate heuristic strategies and extend AMR for VIs to highly-nonlinear and/or degenerate problems.  See the main paper for the general setting.

However, for classical obstacle problems, namely unilateral problems where the operator is the (weak) Laplacian and the corresponding PDE is the Poisson equation, the library also implements element marking using the fully-rigorous \emph{a posteriori} estimator from Nochetto, Siebert, \& Veeser \cite{NSV03}.  For that estimator reliability (estimator gives upper bound on error) and efficiency (lower bound as well) are proven in the $L^\infty$ pointwise norm, a degree of precision which is generally not available for nonlinear and/or degenerating operators.

This Appendix extends their proof of reliability and efficiency to the bilateral case, that is, with continuous lower \emph{and upper} bounds.  (Note that the operator must still be the ordinary linear Laplacian; this is not changed.)  The sequence of topics here, and much of the notation, generally follows that in \cite{NSV03}.


\section{Continuum problem and setup} \label{sec:setup}

\subsection{The variational inequality, and its residual}  Let $\Om$ be a bounded, polyhedral, not necessarily convex domain in $\mathbb{R}^d$
with $d \in \{1,2,3\}$.  Suppose
$\psilo, \psiup \in H^1(\Om) \cap C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Om)$, $0 < \alpha \le 1$, are
lower and upper obstacles, respectively, satisfying the non-degeneracy assumption
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:nondeg}
  \psilo < \psiup \qquad \text{in } \Om.
\end{equation}
Finally, let $f \in L^\infty(\Om)$ be a source function and $g \in H^1(\Om) \cap C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Om)$ be Dirichlet boundary data satisfying the compatibility condition
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:compat}
  \psilo \le g \le \psiup \qquad \text{on } \dOm.
\end{equation}


\begin{remark}[Non-degeneracy]
Strict separation \eqref{eq:nondeg} rules out ``pinched'' points \cite{BuelerFarrell2024} where the two obstacles touch, and thus both constraints are simultaneously active.  Thus the analysis below is free of an extra degenerate case, but our VIAMR library implementation must still cope with near-pinching, since active sets are decided against a numerical tolerance.
\end{remark}

Let $\mathcal{K}$ be the non-empty, closed, convex, \emph{box-constrained} subset of $H^1(\Om)$ defined by the bilateral obstacles:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:K}
  \mathcal{K} := \{v \in H^1(\Om) \mid \psilo \le v \le \psiup \text{ a.e.\ in } \Om,
    \ v = g \text{ on } \dOm\}.
\end{equation}
The fixed boundary condition $v=g$ is in the trace sense.  The continuous VI problem reads:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:VI}
  u \in \mathcal{K}: \qquad
  \left< \nabla u, \nabla(v - u)\right> \ge \left< f, v - u\right>
  \qquad \text{for all } v \in \mathcal{K}.
\end{equation}
This is the immediate generalization of problem (1.1) of \cite{NSV03}, from a one-sided constraint to the bilateral case.  We state the VI in form \eqref{eq:VI}, matching common conventions elsewhere in the literature \cite{BuelerFarrell2024,Falk1974,GraeserKornhuber2009,KinderlehrerStampacchia1980}, and in the library itself, and it is obviously equivalent to the reversed form ``$\left< \nabla u, \nabla(u - v)\right> \le \left< f, u - v\right>$'' used in \cite[formula (1.1)]{NSV03}.

It is well-known that \eqref{eq:VI} admits a unique H\"older continuous solution $u \in \mathcal{K}$ \cite{KinderlehrerStampacchia1980}.
%% TODO(citation): NSV03 attributes Hölder continuity of u, in the
%% unilateral case, to Frehse & Mosco (1982); find and verify a
%% citation that actually covers the box-constrained/double-obstacle case
%% before this goes further, rather than asserting one.

Let $\sigma \in H^{-1}(\Om) = \mathring H^1(\Om)^*$ be the \emph{residual functional}
defined by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigma}
  \left< \sigma, \varphi\right> = \left< \nabla u, \nabla \varphi\right> - \left< f, \varphi\right>
  \qquad \text{for all } \varphi \in \mathring H^1(\Om).
\end{equation}

Again, definition \eqref{eq:sigma} switches the sign relative to \cite[formula (1.2)]{NSV03}.  However, our sign choice, consistent across the VIAMR library, matches the traditional for \emph{unilateral} complementarity ($a \ge 0$, $b \ge 0$, $ab = 0$), and it matches more of the literature.  In any case, the reader should remember our sign convention as given by \eqref{eq:sigmasign} below.  Also, the discrete counterpart $\sigma_h$ appears below in \S \ref{sec:discretization}.

Consider what happens when the solution contacts the obstacle, creating sets where the inequality is active.  Unlike the unilateral case, $\sigma$ no longer has a fixed sign globally.  Write $\Cbot := \{u = \psilo\}$ and $\Ctop := \{u = \psiup\}$ for the closed
lower- and upper-contact \emph{active sets}.  Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigmasign}
  \sigma \ge 0 \text{ on } \Cbot \qquad \text{and} \qquad \sigma \le 0 \text{ on } \Ctop,
\end{equation}
while $\sigma = 0$ on the open non-contact set $\{\psilo < u < \psiup\}$.  The proof is by taking a smooth and locally-supported test function $\phi\in \mathring H^1(\Omega)$, with $\phi\ge 0$ compactly supported in $\Om \setminus \Ctop$, or $\phi\le 0$ compactly supported in $\Om \setminus \Cbot$, respectively, and substituting $v=u+\eps \phi$ into \eqref{eq:VI}, for sufficiently small $\eps>0$, such that $v\in\mathcal{K}$.  Thus the non-degeneracy assumption \eqref{eq:nondeg} implies that $\sigma$ is a signed Radon measure \cite{EvansGariepy2015} with a corresponding splitting: $\sigma$ admits the Jordan decomposition $\sigma = \mu_{\bot} - \mu_{\top}$ into nonnegative Radon measures supported on $\Cbot$ and $\Ctop$ respectively.


\subsection{Separation of the bilateral active (contact) sets}  While the non-degeneracy assumption $\psilo < \psiup$ implies that the two contact sets $\Cbot, \Ctop$ are disjoint, it does not by itself say how far apart they are.  This is important because, upon discretization (\S \ref{sec:discretization}), a mesh star (definition \eqref{eq:star} below, or e.g.~\cite{Langeetal2016}) touching \emph{both} contact sets would break the simple sign argument which we will need for the discrete residual $\sigma_h$ introduced below.  For the argument to follow, we need to control how much of the true contact set the discretization can recognize as such, and compactness of $\bar\Om$ allows us to quantify the separation of the contact sets, as follows.

Let $[\psiup]_\alpha$ denote the H\"older seminorm of $\psiup$ on $\bar\Om$, i.e.\ the least $L^\top \ge 0$ with $|\psiup(x) - \psiup(y)| \le L^\top |x-y|^\alpha$ for all $x,y \in \bar\Om$.  This value is finite since $\psiup \in C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Om)$, but note it can be zero, related to the division convention below.

\begin{proposition}[Separation of the contact sets]
\label{prop:separation}
Assume, as \cite{NSV03} does for the unilateral problem, that the solution $u$ is H\"older continuous on $\bar\Om$ with some exponent $\beta \in (0,1]$ and seminorm $L_u \ge 0$.  Let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:deltamin}
  \delta_{\min} := \min_{\bar\Om} (\psiup - \psilo) > 0,
\end{equation}
which is attained and positive by compactness of $\bar\Om$ and continuity
and non-degeneracy of $\psiup - \psilo$. If $\Cbot, \Ctop$ are both non-empty
then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:r0}
  \operatorname{dist}(\Cbot, \Ctop) \ \ge\ r_0
  := \min\left\{ \left(\frac{\delta_{\min}}{2 L^\top}\right)^{1/\alpha},
                 \left(\frac{\delta_{\min}}{2 L_u}\right)^{1/\beta} \right\}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

Regarding possible division by zero in \eqref{eq:r0}, we make the convention that a term is omitted from the minimum if its
denominator vanishes, i.e.\ if $\psiup$ or $u$ is constant.  That is, we regard the quotients as positive infinity in such cases.

\begin{proof}
Let $x \in \Cbot$ and $y \in \Ctop$ be arbitrary, and set $r := |x-y|$.  Since $u(x) = \psilo(x)$,
\begin{equation}
  \delta_{\min} \le \psiup(x) - \psilo(x) = \psiup(x) - u(x).
\end{equation}
Insert $\psiup(y) = u(y)$ and use the two H\"older bounds:
\begin{equation}
  \delta_{\min} \le \bigl(\psiup(x) - \psiup(y)\bigr) + \bigl(u(y) - u(x)\bigr)
  \le L^\top r^\alpha + L_u r^\beta.
\end{equation}
If $r < r_0$, then
$L^\top r^\alpha < \delta_{\min}/2$ and $L_u r^\beta < \delta_{\min}/2$, by definition of $r_0$,  so $L^\top r^\alpha + L_u r^\beta < \delta_{\min}$, a contradiction. Thus
$r \ge r_0$, and $\operatorname{dist}(\Cbot,\Ctop) \ge r_0$ follows.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:separation} gives an explicit, data-determined lower bound $r_0$ on the spatial separation of the two contact sets; $r_0$ is controlled by $f,\psilo,\psiup,g$ through the regularity theory.  As it turns out, \S \ref{sec:discretization} does \emph{not} need to impose $h < c\,r_0$ as a hypothesis for the estimator to be well-defined and correct.  Instead, $r_0$'s role is to bound the width of the transition band so that the discretization's classification tracks the true contact sets closely.


\section{Discretization} \label{sec:discretization}

Let $\{\Th\}$ be a sequence of conforming partitions of $\bar\Om$ into \emph{closed} simplices, namely intervals, triangles, or tetrahedra in dimensions $d=1,2,3$, respectively.  Let $\Nh$ be the node set of $\Th$, $\Nhr \subset \Nh$ the interior nodes, and $\Sh$ the interior sides.

Given a (closed) simplex $T$, $h_T$ is its diameter and $\rho_T$ is the maximal radius of a ball contained in $T$.  We assume shape-regularity, namely a bound $h_T/\rho_T \le \gamma^*$ for all $T \in \Th$, with $\gamma^*$ independent of $h$.

For $\omega \subset \bar\Om$ any subset, let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:star}
\Uh(\omega) := \bigcup\{T \in \Th : T \cap \omega \ne \emptyset\}.
\end{equation}
This closed set is a (finite) union of the closed simplices $T$.  Note that $T \cap \omega \ne \emptyset$ only requires $T$ to meet $\omega$ in a single point, so for example $\Uh(T)$ for a simplex $T$ includes every simplex with a shared vertex, edge, or face.  The star of a node \cite{Langeetal2016} is $\Uh(z) := \Uh(\{z\}) = \operatorname{supp} \varphi_h^z$ for $z \in \Nh$, which is the closure of $\{\varphi_h^z > 0\}$.

Let $\Vh$ be the space of continuous piecewise-linear finite elements over $\Th$, with
$\Vhr := \Vh \cap \mathring H^1(\Om)$.  We denote the nodal basis of $\Vh$ by $(\varphi_h^z)_{z \in \Nh}$.  Let $\Ih$ be the Lagrange interpolation operator onto $\Vh$, specifically $\Ih:C(\bar\Om) \to \Vh$.  As in \cite{NSV03} we write $\left< \cdot,\cdot\right>_h$ for the lumped $L_2$ scalar product,
\begin{equation}
\left< \varphi_h, \psi_h\right>_h := \int_\Om \Ih(\varphi_h \psi_h)
\end{equation}
Also as in \cite{NSV03}, in what follows $C$ denotes different constants depending only on $\gamma^*$, $\Om$, and $d$, and $\preceq$ indicates $\le$ up to such a constant.


\subsection{Monotonicity of nodal interpolation, and \texorpdfstring{$\Kh$}{Kh}}

Let us start with two properties special to affine functions and piecewise-\emph{linear} interpolation, which need no acuteness or mesh-fineness assumption on $\Th$.  They do not hold generally for higher-order elements.

\begin{lemma}[Vanishing affine functions on simplices]
\label{lem:affine}
If $w$ is affine on a simplex $T$, and if $w \ge 0$ at every vertex of $T$, then $w\ge 0$ everywhere on $T$.  Furthermore, $w = 0$ somewhere in $T$ if and only if $w = 0$ at some vertex of $T$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Most parts are obvious affine function properties.  For ($\Rightarrow$) direction of the equivalence claim, write a point $x \in T$ in barycentric coordinates, $x = \sum_i \lambda_i x_i$, $\lambda_i \ge 0$, $\sum_i \lambda_i = 1$, so $w(x) = \sum_i \lambda_i w(x_i)$.  If $w(x_i) > 0$ for every vertex $x_i$, then $w(x) > 0$ for every $x \in T$ (some $\lambda_i > 0$ always, and all terms are non-negative with at least one strictly positive).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Order-preservation of $I_h$]
\label{lem:monotone}
If $\phi, \zeta \in C(\bar\Om)$ satisfy $\phi \le \zeta$ on $\bar\Om$, then
$\Ih \phi \le \Ih \zeta$ on $\bar\Om$, for any mesh $\Th$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By linearity of $\Ih$, $\Ih \zeta - \Ih \phi = \Ih(\zeta - \phi)$, and $\zeta - \phi \ge 0$ at every node. On each $T \in \Th$, $\Ih(\zeta-\phi)$ is affine, hence $\Ih(\zeta-\phi) \ge 0$ on $T$, for every $T$, by the Lemma \ref{lem:affine}.
\end{proof}

Set $\psiloh := \Ih \psilo$ and $\psiuph := \Ih \psiup$. Applying
Lemma~\ref{lem:monotone} to $\psilo \le \psiup$ and \eqref{eq:nondeg} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:disc-nondeg}
  \psiuph - \psiloh \ \ge\ \delta_{\min} \ >\ 0 \qquad \text{on } \bar\Om.
\end{equation}
Note this holds for \emph{every} mesh, not just fine ones. Applying it again to
the compatibility condition $\psilo \le g \le \psiup$ on $\dOm$
\eqref{eq:compat} gives $\psiloh \le \Ih g \le \psiuph$ on $\dOm$.

Thus we define the discrete constraint set just as in \eqref{eq:K}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Kh}
  \Kh := \{v_h \in \Vh \mid \psiloh \le v_h \le \psiuph \text{ in } \Om,
    \ v_h = \Ih g \text{ on } \dOm\},
\end{equation}
and $\Kh$ is non-empty, closed, and convex.  As noted by \cite{NSV03} for the unilateral case, $\Kh$ is in general not a subset of $\mathcal{K}$ defined by \eqref{eq:K}, so in this sense our approximation is non-conforming.  The discrete problem corresponding to \eqref{eq:VI} reads
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:discVI}
  u_h \in \Kh: \qquad
  \left< \nabla u_h, \nabla(v_h - u_h)\right> \ge \left< f, v_h - u_h\right>
  \qquad \text{for all } v_h \in \Kh,
\end{equation}
which again admits a unique solution.


\subsection{The discrete residual and its sign}

For interior nodes $z \in \Nhr$, define $\sigma_h \in \Vh$ nodally by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigmah}
  \left< \sigma_h, \varphi_h^z \right>_h
  = \left< \nabla u_h, \nabla \varphi_h^z \right> - \left< f, \varphi_h^z \right>,
\end{equation}
whence $\sigma_h(z) = \bigl(\int_\Om \varphi_h^z\bigr)^{-1}
\bigl(\left< \nabla u_h, \nabla \varphi_h^z\right> - \left< f, \varphi_h^z\right>\bigr)$.  This is exactly \cite[formula (2.1)]{NSV03}, with the same sign flip as for $\sigma$ itself.
% also, \eqref{eq:sigmah} is the Galerkin (weak-form) instance of the strong-form nodal residual underlying \cite{BuelerFarrell2024}'s mixed complementarity classification in their \S 7, and it matches the convention already used by \texttt{viamr}'s \texttt{nsvmark()}

Let $\Nhbot := \{z \in \Nh : u_h(z) = \psiloh(z)\}$ and $\Nhtop := \{z \in \Nh : u_h(z) = \psiuph(z)\}$ be the \emph{nodal active sets}, the discrete lower- and upper-contact sets; they are disjoint by \eqref{eq:disc-nondeg}.

Extending \cite{NSV03}, for elements we split $\Th$ into three parts:
\begin{align}
\label{eq:Thsplit}
  \Thbot &:= \{T \in \Th : \Uh(T) \subset \{u_h = \psiloh\}\}, \\
  \Thtop &:= \{T \in \Th : \Uh(T) \subset \{u_h = \psiuph\}\}, \\
  \Thplus &:= \Th \setminus (\Thbot \cup \Thtop).
\end{align}
Then define $\Lamhbot$ and $\Lamhtop$ as the corresponding unions of elements,
\begin{equation}
\Lamhbot = \bigcup_{T \in \Thbot} T, \qquad \Lamhtop = \bigcup_{T \in \Thtop} T,
\end{equation}
which are also discrete lower- and upper-contact active sets, but now element-wise.

\begin{remark}[``Thin'' active sets]
\label{rem:thinactive}
The element sets $\Thbot$ and $\Thtop$ are exactly what the \texttt{VIAMR.thinelemactive()} method marks.  An element is marked only if it \emph{and every element sharing a node with it} is active.  This erodes, by one element ring, the seemingly-strict element-wise active set, namely method \texttt{elemactive()}, which marks an element active if all its nodes are active, i.e.~in $\Nhbot,\Nhtop$ respectively.  That erosion is precisely the content of ``$\Uh(T) \subset \{u_h = \psiloh\}$'', for example, because $\Uh(T)$ \emph{is} $T$'s ring of neighbors (every $T' \in \Th$ meeting $T$ in a node).  Concretely, the calls are
\begin{align*}
\Thbot &= \texttt{thinelemactive(uh, psiloh, boxside="lower")} \\
\Thtop &= \texttt{thinelemactive(uh, psiuph, boxside="upper")}
\end{align*}
using the \texttt{boxside} kwarg.  The original implementation of the unilateral \cite{NSV03} marking already needed the one-ring erosion in \texttt{thinelemactive()}.  Proposition~\ref{prop:nodalsign} below is the rigorous estimator-side justification of this ``safely interior'' erosion; it is not merely a heuristic.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Nodal residual sign in active cases]
\label{prop:nodalsign}
If $z \in \Nhr$ and $\Uh(z) \subset \{u_h = \psiloh\}$, i.e.\ $z$'s star is wholly lower-active, then $\sigma_h(z) \ge 0$.  Symmetrically, if $\Uh(z) \subset \{u_h = \psiuph\}$ then $\sigma_h(z) \le 0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $\Uh(z) \subset \{u_h = \psiloh\}$.  Since $\psiuph - \psiloh \ge \delta_{\min} > 0$ on $\bar\Om$ by \eqref{eq:disc-nondeg}, and because $u_h = \psiloh$ throughout $\Uh(z)$, also $\psiuph - u_h \ge \delta_{\min} > 0$ throughout $\Uh(z)$.  In particular, $\Uh(z) \cap \Nhtop = \emptyset$.

Now fix $t \in [0, \delta_{\min}/\max_{\Uh(z)} \varphi_h^z]$ and set $v_h := u_h + t \varphi_h^z \in \Vh$. Since $\varphi_h^z \ge 0$ and $u_h = \psiloh$ on $\Uh(z)$, $v_h \ge \psiloh$ there, and $v_h = u_h \ge \psiloh$ elsewhere.  By the choice of $t$, $v_h \le u_h + \delta_{\min} \le \psiuph$ on $\Uh(z)$, and $v_h = u_h \le \psiuph$ elsewhere, so $v_h \in \Kh$.  Testing \eqref{eq:discVI} with this $v_h$ and dividing by $t \ge 0$ gives $\left< \nabla u_h, \nabla \varphi_h^z\right> \ge \left< f, \varphi_h^z\right>$, which implies $\sigma_h(z) \ge 0$ by \eqref{eq:sigmah}.

The upper case is symmetric, testing with $t \le 0$.
\end{proof}

Observe that no separation hypothesis is needed in Proposition \ref{prop:nodalsign}.  This is important because we need to extend the \cite{NSV03} estimator using $\sigma_h$'s sign over elements in $\Thbot$ or $\Thtop$.  For a node whose star meets \emph{both} contact types, thus necessarily an element of $\Thplus$ by \eqref{eq:Thsplit}, the perturbation argument above breaks.  In fact, moving $t>0$ toward the free lower direction can collide with an upper-contact point elsewhere in the star, for any $t>0$, however small.  However, in the estimator, for $\Thplus$ elements we will never call on $\sigma_h$'s sign to begin with (see the forthcoming efficiency/lower-bounds section), so no separation hypothesis is needed for correctness.

Proposition~\ref{prop:separation}'s separation constant $r_0$ still matters for \emph{sharpness}: once $h \lesssim r_0$, $\Thplus$ shrinks to a thin transition band tracking the true free boundary, rather than swallowing up contact elements that should have been classified into $\Thbot$ or $\Thtop$.


\subsection{The residual at boundary nodes}

At a boundary node $z \in \Nh \setminus \Nhr$, $\phi_h^z$ does not vanish on $\dOm$, and $v_h = \Ih g$ is fixed there by \eqref{eq:Kh}, so the stationarity argument of Proposition~\ref{prop:nodalsign} gives nothing.  Instead, $\sigma_h(z)$ at a boundary node must be defined by the following diagnostic construction, similar to \cite[\S 2.1]{NSV03}.

Because $\phi_h^z$ does not vanish on $\dOm$ for a boundary node, the bracket in \eqref{eq:sigmah} is no longer the whole residual: integrating $\left<\nabla u_h,\nabla\phi_h^z\right>$ by parts elementwise picks up an extra boundary flux term, $\left<\!\left<\partial_n u_h,\phi_h^z\right>\!\right> := \int_{\dOm} (\partial_n u_h)\,\phi_h^z$, where $\partial_n$ is the outward-normal derivative, taken elementwise. Write
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Rbnd}
  R_h(z) := \left<\nabla u_h,\nabla\phi_h^z\right> - \left< f,\phi_h^z\right> - \left<\!\left<\partial_n u_h,\phi_h^z\right>\!\right>, \qquad z \in \Nh\setminus\Nhr,
\end{equation}
for this boundary-corrected residual bracket, in our sign convention. Since \eqref{eq:Rbnd} is not derived from a stationarity argument, it carries no guaranteed sign, but \cite{NSV03} handles this by keeping only the part of the bracket that has the sign \eqref{eq:sigmasign} demands, and discarding the rest.

We extend the same device to both obstacles, defining, for $z \in \Nh\setminus\Nhr$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigmah-bnd}
  \sigma_h(z) := \begin{cases}
    R_h(z)^+ / \int_\Om \phi_h^z, & \text{if } \Uh(z) \subset \{u_h = \psiloh\}, \\
    -R_h(z)^- / \int_\Om \phi_h^z, & \text{if } \Uh(z) \subset \{u_h = \psiuph\}, \\
    0, & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $v^+ := \max\{v,0\}$ and $v^- := -\min\{v,0\}$ are the positive and negative parts of $v$. Since $R_h(z)^+ \ge 0$ and $-R_h(z)^- \le 0$ by construction, \eqref{eq:sigmah-bnd} satisfies \eqref{eq:sigmasign} by fiat.

Thus, at a boundary node whose star lies wholly in one active set, we keep the correctly-signed part of $R_h(z)$ and zero out the rest; at any other boundary node, including one whose star meets both contact types or neither, $\sigma_h(z)$ is simply set to zero.  Up to the sign flip already noted, the first case of \eqref{eq:sigmah-bnd} is exactly \cite[formula (2.2)]{NSV03}, and the second case, for upper-contact boundary nodes, is appropriately symmetric.


\subsection{Positivity-preserving interpolation}

We record here the ball-averaging interpolation operator of Chen \& Nochetto \cite{ChenNochetto2000}, since the Galerkin-functional and barrier constructions to come both use it.  The construction does not depend on the obstacles at all, so it is unchanged for the box-constrained case; we simply state it following \cite[\S 2.2]{NSV03}, and citing \cite{ChenNochetto2000} for proofs.  We call a linear operator $A$ \emph{positive} if $\varphi \ge 0$ implies $A\varphi \ge 0$.

To define the interpolation operator $\Pih : L_1(\Om) \to \Vhr$, fix the mesh $\Th$ first, and then, for each $z \in \Nhr$, fix a ball $B(z) \subset \Uh(z)$, centered at $z$, whose radius is comparable to the local mesh size near $z$ (since it is contained in the star).  On a graded or adapted mesh this radius varies from node to node; $B(z)$ is generally \emph{not} one ball translated throughout $\Om$.  The precise choice of radius, needed only for \eqref{eq:Pihprops} below to hold uniformly in $h$, does not concern us here; see \cite[\S 6]{NSV03}.  For $\varphi \in L_1(\Om)$, set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Pih}
  \Pih\varphi(z) := \frac{1}{|B(z)|}\int_{B(z)}\varphi.
\end{equation}
Positivity is immediate: an average of a non-negative function over a ball is non-negative.  Linearity is also clear because the balls $B(z)$ only depend on the mesh, not the input $\varphi$.  Note $\Pih$ is not a projection: $\Pih^2 \ne \Pih$ in general.

Write $\|\cdot\|_{0,p;\omega}$ for the $L^p(\omega)$ norm over $\omega \subset \bar\Om$.  Chen \& Nochetto \cite{ChenNochetto2000} show that, for every $1\le p\le\infty$, $T\in\Th$, and $S\in\Sh$ (interior edges/faces), and for $\varphi \in \mathring W^{1,p}(\Om)$,
\begin{align}
\label{eq:Pihprops}
  \|\nabla\Pih\varphi\|_{0,p;T} &\preceq \|\nabla\varphi\|_{0,p;\Uh(T)}, \\
  \|\varphi - \Pih\varphi\|_{0,p;T} &\preceq h_T\|\nabla\varphi\|_{0,p;\Uh(T)}, \notag \\
  \|\varphi - \Pih\varphi\|_{0,p;S} &\preceq h_S^{1-1/p}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{0,p;\Uh(S)}. \notag
\end{align}
If in addition $\varphi \in \mathring W^{1,1}(\Om)$ and $\nabla\varphi \in BV(\Om)^d$, then also
\begin{align}
\label{eq:PihBV}
  \|\varphi-\Pih\varphi\|_{0,1;T} &\preceq h_T^2\, |D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Uh(T)), \\
  \|\varphi-\Pih\varphi\|_{0,1;S} &\preceq h_S\, |D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Uh(S)), \notag
\end{align}
where $|D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}} := \sum_{i=1}^d |D\partial_i\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}$ sums the variation measures of the components of $\nabla\varphi$, and $|D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\omega) = \|D^2\varphi\|_{0,1;\omega}$ whenever $\varphi \in W^{2,1}(\Om)$ and $\omega \subset \Om$ is measurable.

We will need properties \eqref{eq:Pihprops} and \eqref{eq:PihBV} in the following sections.


\section{A posteriori estimator} \label{sec:estimator}

\subsection{The Galerkin functional}

Following \cite[\S 3]{NSV03}, write $\Thzero := \Thbot \cup \Thtop$ for the union of both wholly-active element sets, matching their notation (``$T_h^0$'').  Note that from \eqref{eq:Thsplit} we can now write $\Thplus = \Th \setminus \Thzero$.  Define $\tilde\sigma_h \in H^{-1}(\Om)$ by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigmatilde}
  \left<\tilde\sigma_h,\varphi\right> := \sum_{T\in\Thzero}\int_T \sigma_h\varphi + \sum_{T\in\Thplus}\int_T \Ih(\sigma_h\Pih\varphi), \qquad \varphi \in \mathring H^1(\Om).
\end{equation}
This is exactly \cite[formula (3.1)]{NSV03}: on wholly-active elements $\tilde\sigma_h$ agrees with $\sigma_h$ itself; on the other elements $\Thplus$, which are inactive or transitional, the test function is first replaced by its positivity-preserving interpolant $\Pih\varphi$ before pairing with $\sigma_h$.  Nothing in \eqref{eq:sigmatilde} distinguishes lower from upper contact, so we have kept \cite{NSV03}'s definition.

The \emph{Galerkin functional} $\Gh \in H^{-1}(\Om)$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Gh}
  \left<\Gh,\varphi\right> := -\left<\nabla u_h,\nabla\varphi\right> + \left< f - \tilde\sigma_h,\varphi\right>, \qquad \varphi \in \mathring H^1(\Om).
\end{equation}
This is exactly \cite[formula (3.3)]{NSV03}.  Note that

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Ghagain}
\left<\Gh,\varphi\right> = \left<\nabla(u-u_h),\nabla\varphi\right> - \left<\sigma+\tilde\sigma_h,\varphi\right>
\end{equation}
by definition \eqref{eq:sigma} of $\sigma$.

Consider the application of $\Gh$ to a piecewise-linear function $\varphi_h$.  The following Lemma can be read as saying that the result would be zero if $\sigma_h$ and $\tilde \sigma_h$ were computing the same thing, and if the inner products $\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>$ and $\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>_h$ were the same.  Of course, these statements are not true, thus the ``almost'' in the Lemma's name.

\begin{lemma}[Almost Galerkin orthogonality]
\label{lem:almostGO}
For all $\varphi_h \in \Vhr$,
\begin{align}
\label{eq:almostGO}
  \left< \Gh, \varphi_h\right> &= \left< \sigma_h,\varphi_h\right>_h - \left<\tilde\sigma_h,\varphi_h\right> \\
  &= \sum_{T\in\Thzero}\int_T \Ih(\sigma_h\varphi_h) - \sigma_h\varphi_h
  \ +\ \sum_{T\in\Thplus}\int_T \Ih\bigl(\sigma_h(\varphi_h - \Pih\varphi_h)\bigr). \notag
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $\varphi_h\in\Vhr$, \eqref{eq:sigmah} gives $\left<\nabla u_h,\nabla\varphi_h\right> = \left< f,\varphi_h\right> - \left<\sigma_h,\varphi_h\right>_h$: this is $\sigma_h$'s defining relation at interior nodes, and it holds at \emph{every} $z\in\Nhr$ unconditionally, so by linearity it holds for every $\varphi_h \in \Vhr$, not just those supported where a star is wholly active.  Substituting into \eqref{eq:Gh},
\[
  \left<\Gh,\varphi_h\right> = -\bigl(\left< f,\varphi_h\right> - \left<\sigma_h,\varphi_h\right>_h\bigr) + \left< f,\varphi_h\right> - \left<\tilde\sigma_h,\varphi_h\right> = \left<\sigma_h,\varphi_h\right>_h - \left<\tilde\sigma_h,\varphi_h\right>,
\]
the first equality.  For the second, $\left<\sigma_h,\varphi_h\right>_h = \sum_{T\in\Th}\int_T \Ih(\sigma_h\varphi_h)$, since $\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>_h$ is by definition $\int_\Om \Ih(\cdot)$ and the integral is additive over $\Th$.  Subtracting \eqref{eq:sigmatilde} and collecting terms, the $\Thzero$ part gives $\int_T \Ih(\sigma_h\varphi_h) - \sigma_h\varphi_h$, and the $\Thplus$ part gives $\int_T \Ih(\sigma_h\varphi_h) - \Ih(\sigma_h\Pih\varphi_h) = \int_T \Ih\bigl(\sigma_h(\varphi_h-\Pih\varphi_h)\bigr)$ by linearity of $\Ih$.
\end{proof}

Every step above uses only the set-theoretic split $\Th = \Thzero \sqcup \Thplus$ and the unconditional definition \eqref{eq:sigmah} of $\sigma_h$ at interior nodes.  These definitions do not care whether the unified (and thinned) active elements $\Thzero$ are from a lower or upper obstacle.

\begin{lemma}[Representation formula for $\Gh$]
\label{lem:Gh-repn}
For each interior edge/face $S \in \Sh$, fix a unit normal $\nu$ and write $\jump{\partial_\nu u_h}$ for the jump across $S$.  Define $\Rh \in H^{-1}(\Om)$ and $\Qh \in \Vhr$ by
\begin{align}
\label{eq:RhQh}
  \left<\Rh,\varphi\right> &:= \sum_{S\in\Sh}\int_S \jump{\partial_\nu u_h}\,\varphi + \sum_{T\in\Thzero}\int_T (f-\sigma_h)\varphi + \sum_{T\in\Thplus}\int_T f\varphi, \\
  \left<\Qh,\varphi_h\right> &:= \sum_{T\in\Thzero}\int_T \Ih(\sigma_h\varphi_h) - \sigma_h\varphi_h. \notag
\end{align}
Then, for $\varphi \in \mathring H^1(\Om)$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Gh-repn}
  \left<\Gh,\varphi\right> = \left<\Rh,\varphi-\Pih\varphi\right> + \left<\Qh,\Pih\varphi\right>.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Split $\left<\Gh,\varphi\right> = \left<\Gh,\varphi-\Pih\varphi\right> + \left<\Gh,\Pih\varphi\right>$, and evaluate each term.

For $\left<\Gh,\varphi-\Pih\varphi\right>$, use definition \eqref{eq:Gh} with $\varphi$ replaced by $\varphi - \Pih\varphi \in \mathring H^1(\Om)$.  Integrate $\left<\nabla u_h,\nabla(\varphi-\Pih\varphi)\right>$ by parts elementwise: $u_h$ is affine on each $T$, so no interior element term survives, leaving only the interior jump terms.  (There is no boundary term since $\varphi-\Pih\varphi$ vanishes on $\dOm$.)  For $\left<\tilde\sigma_h,\varphi-\Pih\varphi\right>$, use definition \eqref{eq:sigmatilde}.  On $\Thzero$, $\tilde\sigma_h=\sigma_h$ directly, while on $\Thplus$, $\tilde\sigma_h$ pairs against $\Pih$ applied to the test function, and by linearity of $\Pih$, $\Pih(\varphi-\Pih\varphi) = \Pih\varphi - \Pih^2\varphi$.  Altogether,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Gh-first}
  \left<\Gh,\varphi-\Pih\varphi\right> = \left<\Rh,\varphi-\Pih\varphi\right> \ -\ \sum_{T\in\Thplus}\int_T \Ih\bigl(\sigma_h(\Pih\varphi-\Pih^2\varphi)\bigr).
\end{equation}

For $\left<\Gh,\Pih\varphi\right>$, apply Lemma~\ref{lem:almostGO} with $\varphi_h=\Pih\varphi\in\Vhr$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Gh-second}
  \left<\Gh,\Pih\varphi\right> = \left<\Qh,\Pih\varphi\right> \ +\ \sum_{T\in\Thplus}\int_T \Ih\bigl(\sigma_h(\Pih\varphi-\Pih^2\varphi)\bigr).
\end{equation}

Adding \eqref{eq:Gh-second} and \eqref{eq:Gh-first} and cancelling leaves \eqref{eq:Gh-repn}.
\end{proof}

As with Lemma~\ref{lem:almostGO}, this derivation only used the $\Th = \Thzero \sqcup \Thplus$ split, $\sigma_h$'s definition, and linearity of $\Pih$ and $\Ih$; which obstacle is active on $\Thzero$ is not relevant.  The cancellation is exactly why $\tilde\sigma_h$, rather than $\sigma_h$ itself, appears in \eqref{eq:Gh}.  If one used $\sigma_h$ in place of $\tilde\sigma_h$ it would leave an uncancelled $\Thplus$ term in \eqref{eq:Gh-repn}, one that does not shrink at the rate needed for a second-order estimator; see the discussion after \cite[formula (3.6)]{NSV03}.


\subsection{Localized residual and quadrature estimators}

Bounding $\Gh$ pointwise will be carried-out in later sections.  We will follow \cite[\S\S 4--5]{NSV03}, so we first need to bound $\Gh$ in a dual space larger than $\mathring H^1(\Om)$ itself.

Following \cite[\S 3.2]{NSV03}, let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:W}
  W := \{\varphi \in \mathring H^1(\Om) : \nabla\varphi \in BV(\Om)^d\},
\end{equation}
equipped with the seminorm $\varphi \mapsto |D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Om)$ already used in \eqref{eq:PihBV}.  This space is slightly larger than $\mathring W^{1,2}(\Om) \cap \mathring H^1(\Om)$, but it still contains the discrete space $\Vhr$; both facts matter below.

For $1 \le p \le \infty$, with dual exponent $p' = p/(p-1)$, define the \emph{localized residual} $R_p$, element-wise over $T\in\Th$, by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Rp}
  R_p|_T := h_T^{-1/p'}\,|T|^{-1/p}\,\bigl\|\jump{\partial_\nu u_h}\bigr\|_{0,p;\partial T \setminus \dOm}
  \ +\ \begin{cases} |f - \sigma_h|, & T \in \Thzero, \\ |f|, & T \in \Thplus. \end{cases}
\end{equation}
This is exactly \cite[formula (3.7)]{NSV03}: the first term is the usual interior-flux-jump contribution over $\partial T$, and the second is the residual magnitude $|f-\sigma_h|$ on wholly-active elements, or the bare source term $|f|$ elsewhere.  Nothing here refers to the nature of the active obstacle, so \eqref{eq:Rp} is unchanged from the unilateral case.

By the BV-approximation properties \eqref{eq:PihBV} of $\Pih$ and additivity of $|D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}$ over $\Th$, definition \eqref{eq:RhQh} of $\Rh$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Rhbound}
  \bigl|\left<\Rh,\varphi-\Pih\varphi\right>\bigr| \ \preceq\ \|h^2 R_\infty\|_{0,\infty;\Om}\, |D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Om)
\end{equation}
for $\varphi \in W$.  This is \cite[formula (3.8)]{NSV03}.

The second term $\left<\Qh,\Pih\varphi\right>$ in the representation formula \eqref{eq:Gh-repn} accounts for the effect of quadrature.  It too is independent of obstacle type, and it already arises for an unconstrained linear problem.  Applying the first bound in \eqref{eq:Pihprops} together with the Sobolev-type inequality $\|\nabla\varphi\|_{0,d';\Om} \preceq |D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Om)$, valid for $\varphi \in W$, gives \cite[formula (3.9)]{NSV03}, namely
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Qhbound}
  \bigl|\left<\Qh,\Pih\varphi\right>\bigr| \ \preceq\ \|h^2\nabla\sigma_h\|_{0,d;\Lamh}\, \|\nabla\Pih\varphi\|_{0,d';\Om}
  \ \preceq\ \|h^2\nabla\sigma_h\|_{0,d;\Lamh}\, |D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Om),
\end{equation}
where $\Lamh := \Lamhbot \cup \Lamhtop = \bigcup_{T\in\Thzero} T$ is the union of both active regions.  We have generalized from the unilateral contact set \cite[formula (2.4)]{NSV03} to the bilateral case in the obvious way.

Inserting \eqref{eq:Rhbound} and \eqref{eq:Qhbound} into the representation formula \eqref{eq:Gh-repn} gives the upper bound
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Ghbound}
  \|\Gh\|_{-2,\infty;\Om} \ \preceq\ \|h^2 R_\infty\|_{0,\infty;\Om} \ +\ \|h^2\nabla\sigma_h\|_{0,d;\Lamh},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Ghdualnorm}
  \|\Gh\|_{-2,\infty;\Om} := \sup\bigl\{\left<\Gh,\varphi\right> : \varphi\in W,\ |D^2\varphi|_{\mathrm{var}}(\Om)\le 1\bigr\}.
\end{equation}
Bound \eqref{eq:Ghbound} is \cite[formula (3.10)]{NSV03}.  As with the Galerkin functional itself, nothing in its derivation distinguishes lower from upper contact; the same bound controls $\Gh$ whether the active elements $T\in\Thzero$ come from $\Thbot$ or $\Thtop$.


\section{Barriers} \label{sec:barriers}

Following \cite[\S 4]{NSV03}, we now construct continuous barriers for $u$ from a correction $w$ solving a linear problem.  Let $w \in \mathring H^1(\Om)$ be the Riesz representation of the Galerkin functional $\Gh$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:w}
  \int_\Om \nabla w \cdot \nabla\varphi = \left<\Gh,\varphi\right> \qquad \text{for all } \varphi \in \mathring H^1(\Om).
\end{equation}
As in \cite{NSV03} we assume here that $w$ is continuous; this is established, independently of the box constraint, in \S\ref{sec:pointwise} below.

\begin{remark}[Why the unilateral barrier construction does not carry over directly]
Recall from \S 2.2 that $\sigma_h \le 0$ throughout $\bar\Om$ in the unilateral setting of \cite{NSV03}, \emph{unconditionally}: testing the discrete VI with $v_h = u_h + t\phi_h^z$, $t \ge 0$, is feasible at \emph{any} interior node, since raising $u_h$ by a non-negative multiple of $\phi_h^z$ can never violate a one-sided lower bound.  This global sign is what powers \cite[Prop.~4.1, 4.2]{NSV03}: it makes $\tilde\sigma_h$ non-positive against any $v \ge 0$, with no restriction on the mesh.  In our box-constrained setting this mechanism is gone.  Testing with $t \ge 0$ at a node whose star meets $\Ctop$ risks violating the upper bound, so Proposition~\ref{prop:nodalsign} only grants $\sigma_h(z) \ge 0$ under the stronger hypothesis that $z$'s star is \emph{wholly} lower-active, and symmetrically $\sigma_h(z) \le 0$ only when wholly upper-active.  There is no bilateral analogue of the global sign, and the construction below compensates for this locally, at the cost of a mesh-resolution hypothesis that \cite{NSV03} never needs.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Local resolution of the active sets}

Write $L_\bot := [\psilo]_\alpha$ for the H\"older seminorm of $\psilo$, defined exactly as $L^\top := [\psiup]_\alpha$ was in \S 2.2.

\begin{assumption}[Local resolution]
\label{ass:localres}
There is a constant $C_{\mathrm{prox}}$, depending only on the shape-regularity $\gamma^*$, such that every $T \in \Thbot$ satisfies $\operatorname{dist}(T,\Cbot) \le C_{\mathrm{prox}} h_T$, and every $T \in \Thtop$ satisfies $\operatorname{dist}(T,\Ctop) \le C_{\mathrm{prox}} h_T$.
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{ass:localres} says a discretely wholly-active element cannot sit far, relative to its own diameter, from the true contact set of the same type.  Proposition~\ref{prop:separation}'s radius $r_0$ is the natural scale against which ``far'' should be judged: once $h_T \lesssim r_0$ near the transition between obstacle types, the discrete and continuum contact sets of each type are confined to a shared neighborhood, rather than one type of discrete activity appearing spuriously deep inside the other's territory or in the genuine non-contact set.  We do not attempt an independent proof of Assumption~\ref{ass:localres} here.  Doing so without presupposing the very $u \approx u_h$ estimate this section builds toward is left open; for now it is a standing hypothesis of this section, in the same spirit as the assumed H\"older continuity of $u$ itself.

For $T \in \Th$, set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:etapm}
  \eta_\bot(T) := L_\bot h_T^\alpha + L_u h_T^\beta, \qquad
  \eta^\top(T) := L^\top h_T^\alpha + L_u h_T^\beta,
\end{equation}
the same two H\"older terms combined in $r_0$ \eqref{eq:r0}, and set $\eta_\bot := \max_{T \in \Thbot} \eta_\bot(T)$, $\eta^\top := \max_{T \in \Thtop} \eta^\top(T)$ (each $0$ if the corresponding element set is empty).

\begin{lemma}[Local defect on the discrete active sets]
\label{lem:etabound}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:localres}, $u - \psilo \le \eta_\bot$ on $\Lamhbot$, and $\psiup - u \le \eta^\top$ on $\Lamhtop$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $x \in T \in \Thbot$ and pick $y \in \Cbot$ with $|x-y| \le C_{\mathrm{prox}} h_T$, as in Assumption~\ref{ass:localres}.  Since $u(y) = \psilo(y)$,
\[
  u(x) - \psilo(x) = \bigl(u(x)-u(y)\bigr) + \bigl(\psilo(y)-\psilo(x)\bigr) \le L_u|x-y|^\beta + L_\bot|x-y|^\alpha \ \preceq\ \eta_\bot(T) \ \le\ \eta_\bot,
\]
using H\"older continuity of $u$ and $\psilo$.  The bound on $\Lamhtop$ is symmetric, using $y \in \Ctop$ and $\psiup(y) = u(y)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Upper barrier}

\begin{proposition}[Upper barrier]
\label{prop:upperbarrier}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:localres}, the function
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ubarupper}
  u^\top := u_h + w + \|w\|_{0,\infty;\Om} + \|g-I_hg\|_{0,\infty;\partial\Om} + \|(\psilo-u_h)^+\|_{0,\infty;\Om} + \eta_\bot
\end{equation}
satisfies $u \le u^\top$ in $\Om$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{1.} On $\partial\Om$, $u=g$, $u_h = I_hg$, and $w=0$ (continuity of $w$), so, dropping the other non-negative terms of $u^\top$,
\[
  (u-u^\top)|_{\partial\Om} \ \le\ (g-I_hg) - \|g-I_hg\|_{0,\infty;\partial\Om} \ \le\ 0.
\]
Thus $v := (u-u^\top)^+ \in \mathring H^1(\Om)$, since $v$ vanishes on $\partial\Om$ and $u, u^\top \in H^1(\Om)$.

\textbf{2.} Since $u^\top - u_h - w$ is spatially constant, $\nabla u^\top = \nabla u_h + \nabla w$, so by \eqref{eq:w} and \eqref{eq:Ghagain},
\[
  \|\nabla v\|_{0,2;\Om}^2 = \int_\Om \nabla(u-u^\top)\cdot\nabla v
  = \left<\nabla(u-u_h),\nabla v\right> - \left<\Gh,v\right>
  = \left<\sigma+\tilde\sigma_h,\,v\right>.
\]

\textbf{3.} We show $\left<\sigma+\tilde\sigma_h,v\right> \le 0$; by Poincar\'e's inequality this forces $v \equiv 0$, i.e.\ $u \le u^\top$.

Since $u^\top \ge u_h + \|(\psilo-u_h)^+\|_{0,\infty;\Om} \ge u_h + (\psilo-u_h)^+ \ge \psilo$ pointwise, $\{v>0\} = \{u>u^\top\} \subset \{u>\psilo\} = \Om\setminus\Cbot$.  Since $\sigma = \mu_\bot - \mu_\top$ (\S 2.1) with $\mu_\bot$ supported on $\Cbot$, $v=0$ $\mu_\bot$-a.e., so
\[
  \left<\sigma,v\right> = -\int_\Om v\,d\mu_\top \ \le\ 0.
\]

For $\tilde\sigma_h$, definition \eqref{eq:sigmatilde} splits $\left<\tilde\sigma_h,v\right>$ into a sum over $\Thbot$, $\Thtop$, and $\Thplus$.  On $\Thtop$, Proposition~\ref{prop:nodalsign} gives $\sigma_h \le 0$ at every vertex of every $T \in \Thtop$ (its star, hence each vertex's smaller star, lies in $\{u_h=\psiuph\}$), so $\sigma_h \le 0$ throughout $T$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:affine}, and $\int_T \sigma_h v \le 0$.

On $\Thbot$, symmetrically $\sigma_h \ge 0$ throughout each $T$ — the wrong sign, and exactly the term \cite{NSV03} never has to control.  Here Assumption~\ref{ass:localres} does the work: on $\Lamhbot$, $u^\top \ge \psilo + \eta_\bot \ge \psilo + (u-\psilo) = u$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:etabound}, so $v=0$ on $\Lamhbot$ and $\int_T \sigma_h v = 0$ for every $T \in \Thbot$.

On $\Thplus$, $\sigma_h$ carries no definite sign in general, and we have not shown $v=0$ there.  Closing this last case — presumably by tracking the same wholly-active/wholly-inactive nodal dichotomy underlying Proposition~\ref{prop:nodalsign} rather than the coarser element-wise split $\Thbot,\Thtop,\Thplus$ used above — is left as the remaining gap in this proof.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Lower barrier}

\begin{proposition}[Lower barrier]
\label{prop:lowerbarrier}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:localres}, the function
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ubarlower}
  u_\bot := u_h + w - \|w\|_{0,\infty;\Om} - \|g-I_hg\|_{0,\infty;\partial\Om} - \|(u_h-\psiup)^+\|_{0,\infty;\Om} - \eta^\top
\end{equation}
satisfies $u_\bot \le u$ in $\Om$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Symmetric to Proposition~\ref{prop:upperbarrier}, exchanging $\bot \leftrightarrow \top$ and reversing signs throughout: boundary values give $v := (u_\bot - u)^+ \in \mathring H^1(\Om)$; step 2 gives $\|\nabla v\|_{0,2;\Om}^2 = -\left<\sigma+\tilde\sigma_h,v\right>$; the bound $u_\bot \le \psiup$ pointwise gives $\{v>0\}\subset\Om\setminus\Ctop$, hence $\left<\sigma,v\right> = \int_\Om v\,d\mu_\bot \ge 0$; on $\Thbot$, $\sigma_h \ge 0$ gives $\int_T\sigma_h v \ge 0$; and on $\Thtop$, Lemma~\ref{lem:etabound} and the $\eta^\top$ term give $v=0$ on $\Lamhtop$.  The same gap remains on $\Thplus$.
\end{proof}

Together, Propositions~\ref{prop:upperbarrier} and~\ref{prop:lowerbarrier} give
\begin{align}
\label{eq:barrierbound}
  \|u-u_h\|_{0,\infty;\Om} \ \le\ &2\|w\|_{0,\infty;\Om} + \|g-I_hg\|_{0,\infty;\partial\Om} \\
  &+ \max\bigl(\|(\psilo-u_h)^+\|_{0,\infty;\Om},\, \|(u_h-\psiup)^+\|_{0,\infty;\Om}\bigr) + \max(\eta_\bot,\eta^\top), \notag
\end{align}
generalizing \cite[formula (4.8)]{NSV03}.  Besides $\|w\|_{0,\infty;\Om}$, every term on the right is computable from the discrete solution and given data.  Bounding $\|w\|_{0,\infty;\Om}$ itself is the subject of \S\ref{sec:pointwise}, taken up next.
