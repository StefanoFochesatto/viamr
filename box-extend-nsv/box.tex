\section{Introduction and goals} \label{sec:intro}

The VIAMR library (\href{https://github.com/StefanoFochesatto/viamr}{\texttt{github.com/StefanoFochesatto/viamr}}) implements adaptive mesh refinement (AMR) and mesh adaptation algorithms for variational inequalities (VIs).  Some of the implemented methods incorporate heuristic strategies and extend AMR for VIs to highly-nonlinear and/or degenerate problems.  However, for a classical obstacle problem, namely a unilateral obstacle problem where the operator is the (weak) Laplacian and the corresponding PDE is the Poisson equation, the library implements element marking using the fully-rigorous \emph{a posteriori} estimator from Nochetto, Siebert, \& Veeser \cite{NSV03}.  For that estimator reliability (estimator gives upper bound on error) and efficiency (lower bound as well) are proven in the $L^\infty$ pointwise norm.

This Appendix extends their proof to the bilateral case, with continuous lower \emph{and upper} bounds.  However, note that the operator must still be the ordinary linear Laplacian.  Our notation follows that in \cite{NSV03} to a significant extent, but we flag cases where it does not.

\section{Setup} \label{sec:setup}

\subsection{The variational inequality, and its residual}  Let $\Om$ be a bounded, polyhedral, not necessarily convex domain in $\mathbb{R}^d$
with $d \in \{1,2,3\}$.  Suppose
$\psilo, \psiup \in H^1(\Om) \cap C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Om)$, $0 < \alpha \le 1$, are
lower and upper obstacles, respectively, satisfying the non-degeneracy assumption
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:nondeg}
  \psilo < \psiup \qquad \text{in } \Om.
\end{equation}
Finally, let $f \in L^\infty(\Om)$ be a source function and $g \in H^1(\Om) \cap C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Om)$ be Dirichlet boundary data satisfying the compatibility condition
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:compat}
  \psilo \le g \le \psiup \qquad \text{on } \dOm.
\end{equation}

Let $\mathcal{K}$ be the non-empty, closed, convex, \emph{box-constrained} subset of $H^1(\Om)$ defined by the bilateral obstacles:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:K}
  \mathcal{K} := \{v \in H^1(\Om) \mid \psilo \le v \le \psiup \text{ a.e.\ in } \Om,
    \ v = g \text{ on } \dOm\}.
\end{equation}
The fixed boundary condition $v=g$ is in the trace sense.  The continuous VI problem reads:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:VI}
  u \in \mathcal{K}: \qquad
  \langle \nabla u, \nabla(v - u)\rangle \ge \langle f, v - u\rangle
  \qquad \text{for all } v \in \mathcal{K}.
\end{equation}
This is the immediate generalization of problem (1.1) of \cite{NSV03}, from a one-sided constraint to the bilateral case.  We state it in ``$(v-u)$'' form throughout this Appendix, matching common conventions elsewhere in the library \cite{BuelerFarrell2024,Falk1974,GraeserKornhuber2009,KinderlehrerStampacchia1980}, but obviously equivalent to the ``$(u-v)$'' form in \cite{NSV03}.  It is well-known that \eqref{eq:VI} admits a unique solution $u \in \mathcal{K}$ \cite{KinderlehrerStampacchia1980}, which is also H\"older continuous.

%% unilateral case, to Frehse & Mosco (1982); find and verify a

\begin{remark}[Non-degeneracy]
The strict separation $\psilo < \psiup$ rules out \emph{pinched} points \cite{BuelerFarrell2024},
where the two obstacles touch and both constraints are simultaneously active.
This keeps the continuum (and per-triangle) analysis below free of an extra degenerate case alongside lower- and upper-contact.  However, a discrete
implementation must still cope with near-pinching, since active-set
membership there is decided against a numerical tolerance.
\end{remark}

Let $\sigma \in H^{-1}(\Om) = \mathring H^1(\Om)^*$ be the \emph{residual functional}
defined by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigma}
  \langle \sigma, \varphi\rangle = \langle \nabla u, \nabla \varphi\rangle - \langle f, \varphi\rangle
  \qquad \text{for all } \varphi \in \mathring H^1(\Om).
\end{equation}

Again, definition \eqref{eq:sigma} switches the sign relative to \cite[formula (1.2)]{NSV03}.  However, our sign choice, consistent across the VIAMR library, matches the traditional for \emph{unilateral} complementarity ($a \ge 0$, $b \ge 0$, $ab = 0$), and it matches more of the literature.  In any case, the reader should remember our sign convention as given by \eqref{eq:sigmasign} below.  Also, the discrete counterpart $\sigma_h$ appears below in \S \ref{sec:discretization}.

Consider what happens when the solution contacts the obstacle, creating sets where the inequality is active.  Unlike the unilateral case, $\sigma$ no longer has a fixed sign globally.  Write $\Cbot := \{u = \psilo\}$ and $\Ctop := \{u = \psiup\}$ for the closed
lower- and upper-contact \emph{active sets}.  Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigmasign}
  \sigma \ge 0 \text{ on } \Cbot \qquad \text{and} \qquad \sigma \le 0 \text{ on } \Ctop,
\end{equation}
while $\sigma = 0$ on the open non-contact set $\{\psilo < u < \psiup\}$.  The proof is by taking a smooth and locally-supported test function $\phi\in H^1_0(\Omega)$, $\phi\ge 0$, and substituting $v=u+\eps \phi$ into \eqref{eq:VI}, for sufficiently small $\eps>0$ such that $v\in\mathcal{K}$.


\subsection{Separation of the bilateral active (contact) sets}  While the non-degeneracy assumption $\psilo < \psiup$ implies that the two contact sets $\Cbot, \Ctop$ are disjoint, it does not by itself say how far apart they are.  This is important because, upon discretization (\S \ref{sec:discretization}), a mesh star touching \emph{both} contact sets would break the simple sign argument which we will need for the discrete residual $\sigma_h$ introduced below.  For the argument to follow, we need to control how much of the true contact set the discretization can recognize as such, and compactness of $\bar\Om$ allows us to quantify the separation of the contact sets, as follows.

Let $[\psiup]_\alpha$ denote the H\"older seminorm of $\psiup$ on $\bar\Om$, i.e.\ the least $L^\top \ge 0$ with $|\psiup(x) - \psiup(y)| \le L^\top |x-y|^\alpha$ for all $x,y \in \bar\Om$.  This value is finite since $\psiup \in C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Om)$, but note it can be zero, related to the division convention below.

We \emph{assume}, as \cite{NSV03} does for the unilateral
problem, that the solution $u$ is also H\"older continuous on $\bar\Om$ with some exponent
$\beta \in (0,1]$ and seminorm $L_u \ge 0$.

\begin{proposition}[Separation of the contact sets]
\label{prop:separation}
Let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:deltamin}
  \delta_{\min} := \min_{\bar\Om} (\psiup - \psilo) > 0,
\end{equation}
which is attained and positive by compactness of $\bar\Om$ and continuity
and non-degeneracy of $\psiup - \psilo$. If $\Cbot, \Ctop$ are both non-empty
then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:r0}
  \operatorname{dist}(\Cbot, \Ctop) \ \ge\ r_0
  := \min\left\{ \left(\frac{\delta_{\min}}{2 L^\top}\right)^{1/\alpha},
                 \left(\frac{\delta_{\min}}{2 L_u}\right)^{1/\beta} \right\}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

Regarding possible division by zero in \eqref{eq:r0}, we make the convention that a term is omitted from the minimum if its
denominator vanishes, i.e.\ if $\psiup$ or $u$ is constant.  That is, we regard the quotients as positive infinity in such cases.

\begin{proof}
Let $x \in \Cbot$ and $y \in \Ctop$ be arbitrary, and set $r := |x-y|$.  Since $u(x) = \psilo(x)$,
\begin{equation}
  \delta_{\min} \le \psiup(x) - \psilo(x) = \psiup(x) - u(x).
\end{equation}
Insert $\psiup(y) = u(y)$ and use the two H\"older bounds:
\begin{equation}
  \delta_{\min} \le \bigl(\psiup(x) - \psiup(y)\bigr) + \bigl(u(y) - u(x)\bigr)
  \le L^\top r^\alpha + L_u r^\beta.
\end{equation}
If $r < r_0$, then
$L^\top r^\alpha < \delta_{\min}/2$ and $L_u r^\beta < \delta_{\min}/2$, by definition of $r_0$,  so $L^\top r^\alpha + L_u r^\beta < \delta_{\min}$, a contradiction. Thus
$r \ge r_0$, and $\operatorname{dist}(\Cbot,\Ctop) \ge r_0$ follows.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:separation} gives an explicit, data-determined lower bound $r_0$ on the spatial separation of the two contact sets; $r_0$ is controlled by $f,\psilo,\psiup,g$ through the regularity theory.  As it turns out (Remark~\ref{rem:starseparation2}), \S \ref{sec:discretization} does \emph{not} need to impose $h < c\,r_0$ as a hypothesis for the estimator to be well-defined and correct.  Instead, $r_0$'s role is to bound the width of the transition band so that the discretization's classification tracks the true contact sets closely.


\section{Discretization} \label{sec:discretization}

Let $\{\Th\}$ be a sequence of conforming partitions of $\bar\Om$ into \emph{closed} simplices, namely intervals, triangles, or tetrahedra in dimensions $d=1,2,3$, respectively.  Let $\Nh$ be the node set of $\Th$, $\Nhr \subset \Nh$ the interior nodes, and $\Sh$ the interior sides.

Given a (closed) simplex $T$, $h_T$ is its diameter and $\rho_T$ is the maximal radius of a ball contained in $T$.  We assume shape-regularity, namely a bound $h_T/\rho_T \le \gamma^*$ for all $T \in \Th$, with $\gamma^*$ independent of $h$.

Let $\Vh$ be the space of continuous piecewise-linear finite elements over $\Th$, with
$\Vhr := \Vh \cap H_0^1(\Om)$.  We denote the nodal basis of $\Vh$ by $(\varphi_h^z)_{z \in \Nh}$.

For $\omega \subset \bar\Om$ any subset, let
\begin{equation}
\Uh(\omega) := \bigcup\{T \in \Th : T \cap \omega \ne \emptyset\}.
\end{equation}
This closed set is a (finite) union of the closed simplices $T$.  Note that $T \cap \omega \ne \emptyset$ only requires $T$ to meet $\omega$ in a single point, so for example $\Uh(T)$ for a simplex $T$ includes every simplex with a shared vertex, edge, or face.  The star of a node $z \in \Nh$ is $\Uh(z) := \Uh(\{z\}) = \operatorname{supp} \varphi_h^z$, the closure of $\{\varphi_h^z > 0\}$.

Let $\Ih$ be the Lagrange interpolation operator onto $\Vh$, specifically $\Ih:C(\bar\Om) \to \Vh$.  As in \cite{NSV03} we write $\langle \cdot,\cdot\rangle_h$ for the lumped $L_2$ scalar product,
\begin{equation}
\langle \varphi_h, \psi_h\rangle_h := \int_\Om \Ih(\varphi_h \psi_h)
\end{equation}
Also as in \cite{NSV03}, in what follows $C$ denotes different constants depending only on $\gamma^*$, $\Om$, and $d$, and $\preceq$ indicates $\le$ up to such a constant.


\subsection{Monotonicity of nodal interpolation, and $\Kh$}

Let us start with two properties special to affine functions and piecewise-\emph{linear} interpolation, which need no acuteness or mesh-fineness assumption on $\Th$.  They do not hold generally for higher-order elements.

\begin{lemma}[Vanishing affine functions on simplices]
\label{lem:affine}
If $w$ is affine on a simplex $T$, and if $w \ge 0$ at every vertex of $T$, then $w\ge 0$ everywhere on $T$.  Furthermore, $w = 0$ somewhere in $T$ if and only if $w = 0$ at some vertex of $T$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Most parts are obvious affine function properties.  For ($\Rightarrow$) direction of the equivalence claim, write a point $x \in T$ in barycentric coordinates, $x = \sum_i \lambda_i x_i$, $\lambda_i \ge 0$, $\sum_i \lambda_i = 1$, so $w(x) = \sum_i \lambda_i w(x_i)$.  If $w(x_i) > 0$ for every vertex $x_i$, then $w(x) > 0$ for every $x \in T$ (some $\lambda_i > 0$ always, and all terms are non-negative with at least one strictly positive).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Order-preservation of $I_h$]
\label{lem:monotone}
If $\phi, \zeta \in C(\bar\Om)$ satisfy $\phi \le \zeta$ on $\bar\Om$, then
$\Ih \phi \le \Ih \zeta$ on $\bar\Om$, for any mesh $\Th$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By linearity of $\Ih$, $\Ih \zeta - \Ih \phi = \Ih(\zeta - \phi)$, and $\zeta - \phi \ge 0$ at every node. On each $T \in \Th$, $\Ih(\zeta-\phi)$ is affine, hence $\Ih(\zeta-\phi) \ge 0$ on $T$, for every $T$, by the Lemma \ref{lem:affine}.
\end{proof}

Set $\psiloh := \Ih \psilo$ and $\psiuph := \Ih \psiup$. Applying
Lemma~\ref{lem:monotone} to $\psilo \le \psiup$ and \eqref{eq:nondeg} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:disc-nondeg}
  \psiuph - \psiloh \ \ge\ \delta_{\min} \ >\ 0 \qquad \text{on } \bar\Om.
\end{equation}
Note this holds for \emph{every} mesh, not just fine ones. Applying it again to
the compatibility condition $\psilo \le g \le \psiup$ on $\dOm$
\eqref{eq:compat} gives $\psiloh \le \Ih g \le \psiuph$ on $\dOm$.

Thus we define the discrete constraint set just as in \eqref{eq:K}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Kh}
  \Kh := \{v_h \in \Vh \mid \psiloh \le v_h \le \psiuph \text{ in } \Om,
    \ v_h = \Ih g \text{ on } \dOm\},
\end{equation}
and $\Kh$ is non-empty, closed, and convex.  As noted by \cite{NSV03} for the unilateral case, $\Kh$ is in general not a subset of $\mathcal{K}$ defined by \eqref{eq:K}, so in this sense our approximation is non-conforming.  The discrete problem corresponding to \eqref{eq:VI} reads
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:discVI}
  u_h \in \Kh: \qquad
  \langle \nabla u_h, \nabla(v_h - u_h)\rangle \ge \langle f, v_h - u_h\rangle
  \qquad \text{for all } v_h \in \Kh,
\end{equation}
which again admits a unique solution.

\subsection{The discrete residual and its sign}

For interior nodes $z \in \Nhr$, define $\sigma_h \in \Vh$ nodally by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigmah}
  \langle \sigma_h, \varphi_h^z \rangle_h
  = \langle \nabla u_h, \nabla \varphi_h^z \rangle - \langle f, \varphi_h^z \rangle,
\end{equation}
whence $\sigma_h(z) = \bigl(\int_\Om \varphi_h^z\bigr)^{-1}
\bigl(\langle \nabla u_h, \nabla \varphi_h^z\rangle - \langle f, \varphi_h^z\rangle\bigr)$.  This is exactly \cite[formula (2.1)]{NSV03}, with the same sign flip as for $\sigma$ itself.
% also, \eqref{eq:sigmah} is the Galerkin (weak-form) instance of the strong-form nodal residual underlying \cite{BuelerFarrell2024}'s mixed complementarity classification in their \S 7, and it matches the convention already used by \texttt{viamr}'s \texttt{nsvmark()}

Let $\Nhbot := \{z \in \Nh : u_h(z) = \psiloh(z)\}$ and $\Nhtop := \{z \in \Nh : u_h(z) = \psiuph(z)\}$ be the \emph{nodal active sets}, the discrete lower- and upper-contact sets; they are disjoint by \eqref{eq:disc-nondeg}.

Extending \cite{NSV03}, for elements we split $\Th$ into three parts:
\begin{align}
\label{eq:Thsplit}
  \Thbot &:= \{T \in \Th : \Uh(T) \subset \{u_h = \psiloh\}\}, \\
  \Thtop &:= \{T \in \Th : \Uh(T) \subset \{u_h = \psiuph\}\}, \\
  \Thplus &:= \Th \setminus (\Thbot \cup \Thtop).
\end{align}
Then define $\Lamhbot$ and $\Lamhtop$ as the corresponding unions of elements,
\begin{equation}
\Lamhbot = \bigcup_{T \in \Thbot} T, \qquad \Lamhtop = \bigcup_{T \in \Thtop} T,
\end{equation}
which are also discrete lower- and upper-contact active sets, but now element-wise.

\begin{remark}[``Thin'' active sets]
\label{rem:thinactive}
The element sets $\Thbot$ and $\Thtop$ are exactly what the \texttt{VIAMR.thinelemactive()} method marks.  An element is marked only if it \emph{and every element sharing a node with it} is active.  This erodes, by one element ring, the seemingly-strict element-wise active set, namely method \texttt{elemactive()}, which marks an element active if all its nodes are active, i.e.~in $\Nhbot,\Nhtop$ respectively.  That erosion is precisely the content of ``$\Uh(T) \subset \{u_h = \psiloh\}$'', for example, because $\Uh(T)$ \emph{is} $T$'s ring of neighbors (every $T' \in \Th$ meeting $T$ in a node).  Concretely, the calls are
\begin{align*}
\Thbot &= \texttt{thinelemactive(uh, psiloh, boxside="lower")} \\
\Thtop &= \texttt{thinelemactive(uh, psiuph, boxside="upper")}
\end{align*}
using the \texttt{boxside} kwarg.  The original implementation of the unilateral \cite{NSV03} marking already needed the one-ring erosion in \texttt{thinelemactive()}.  Proposition~\ref{prop:nodalsign} below is the rigorous estimator-side justification of this ``safely interior'' erosion; it is not merely a heuristic.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Nodal residual sign in active cases]
\label{prop:nodalsign}
If $z \in \Nhr$ and $\Uh(z) \subset \{u_h = \psiloh\}$, i.e.\ $z$'s star is wholly lower-active, then $\sigma_h(z) \ge 0$.  Symmetrically, if $\Uh(z) \subset \{u_h = \psiuph\}$ then $\sigma_h(z) \le 0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $\Uh(z) \subset \{u_h = \psiloh\}$.  Since $\psiuph - \psiloh \ge \delta_{\min} > 0$ on $\bar\Om$ by \eqref{eq:disc-nondeg}, and because $u_h = \psiloh$ throughout $\Uh(z)$, also $\psiuph - u_h \ge \delta_{\min} > 0$ throughout $\Uh(z)$.  In particular, $\Uh(z) \cap \Nhtop = \emptyset$.

Now fix $t \in [0, \delta_{\min}/\max_{\Uh(z)} \varphi_h^z]$ and set $v_h := u_h + t \varphi_h^z \in \Vh$. Since $\varphi_h^z \ge 0$ and $u_h = \psiloh$ on $\Uh(z)$, $v_h \ge \psiloh$ there, and $v_h = u_h \ge \psiloh$ elsewhere.  By the choice of $t$, $v_h \le u_h + \delta_{\min} \le \psiuph$ on $\Uh(z)$, and $v_h = u_h \le \psiuph$ elsewhere, so $v_h \in \Kh$.  Testing \eqref{eq:discVI} with this $v_h$ and dividing by $t \ge 0$ gives $\langle \nabla u_h, \nabla \varphi_h^z\rangle \ge \langle f, \varphi_h^z\rangle$, which implies $\sigma_h(z) \ge 0$ by \eqref{eq:sigmah}.

The upper case is symmetric, testing with $t \le 0$.
\end{proof}

Observe that no separation hypothesis is needed in Proposition \ref{prop:nodalsign}.  This is important because we need to extend the \cite{NSV03} estimator using $\sigma_h$'s sign over elements in $\Thbot$ or $\Thtop$.  For a node whose star meets \emph{both} contact types, thus necessarily an element of $\Thplus$ by \eqref{eq:Thsplit}, the perturbation argument above breaks.  In fact, moving $t>0$ toward the free lower direction can collide with an upper-contact point elsewhere in the star, for any $t>0$, however small.  However, in the estimator, for $\Thplus$ elements we will never call on $\sigma_h$'s sign to begin with (see the forthcoming efficiency/lower-bounds section), so no separation hypothesis is needed for correctness.

Proposition~\ref{prop:separation}'s separation constant $r_0$ still matters for \emph{sharpness}: once $h \lesssim r_0$, $\Thplus$ shrinks to a thin transition band tracking the true free boundary, rather than swallowing up contact elements that should have been classified into $\Thbot$ or $\Thtop$.
